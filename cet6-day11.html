The Architecture of Credibility: How Quantitative Faith Displaced Qualitative Judgment

In the end, the debate over metrics is a debate about what it means to be human. Are we merely collections of measurable attributes—height, weight, income, test scores—or are we something more? The democratic promise of quantification was that it would liberate us from the arbitrary power of lords and priests. In many ways, it has. But liberty requires more than the removal of old hierarchies; it requires the courage to resist new ones. The statistician who reduces a neighbourhood to a crime index is no less reductive than the lord who reduced a peasant to a serf. The challenge of the coming decades is to build institutions that can hold both kinds of knowledge in tension: the clarity of the number and the complexity of the story. This is not an easy task. It is, however, the only decent one available to us. The alternative is to live in a world where everything is counted and nothing is known.
