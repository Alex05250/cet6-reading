It's best to think of an accent as a distinct, systematic and rule-governed way of speaking.

最好将口音理解为一种独特、系统且受规则支配的说话方式。

Accent is not a synonym for dialect, but it's related.

口音并不是方言的同义词，但两者之间存在关联。

Dialect is an umbrella term for the way a community pronounces words, creates words, and orders words; accent refers to that first part only.

方言是一个总称，涵盖一个社群的发音方式、造词方式和词序安排；而口音仅指其中的发音部分。

Many laymen presume that there is a single standard way of speaking in each country, with one single standard accent, and that this perceived standard is inherently the best form of speech, but linguists often point out that the concept of a standard accent is better understood as an ideal rather than a reality.

许多外行人认为，每个国家都存在一种单一的标准说话方式，只有一种标准口音，而且这种所谓的标准天生就是最佳的语言形式，但语言学家经常指出，标准口音这一概念更应被理解为一种理想，而非现实。

In other words, no one speaks "standard English"; rather, it is an imagined way of using language that exists only in grammar and style books.

换句话说，没有人真正说"标准英语"；它只是一种存在于语法和文体书中的、人们想象出来的语言使用方式。

One reason linguists agree there is no one true standard is that, through the years, there have been multiple supposed standards, depending on time and place.

语言学家一致认为不存在唯一真正的标准，原因之一在于，多年来，所谓的标准一直有多个，取决于时间和地域。

The standard for English in British newsreels from the 1950s, for example, differs greatly from the standard found on American national news broadcasts today.

例如，20世纪50年代英国新闻影片中的英语标准，与今天美国全国新闻广播中的标准大相径庭。

In fact, there has never been a single standard that's been fully agreed upon — and broadcast outlets across the spectrum have never consistently held to those standards anyway.

事实上，从来没有任何一种标准得到过完全一致的认可——而且各类广播媒体也从未始终如一地遵循过这些标准。

Even so, this idea of a standard accent is influential and many workplace and educational environments perpetuate the idea that accents that deviate from the current standard are less appropriate, or even inappropriate.

尽管如此，标准口音的观念依然影响深远，许多职场和教育环境都在不断强化这样一种看法：偏离当前标准的口音不够恰当，甚至不恰当。

Scholars have found that southern US accent features are more accepted in domains such as government, law and service-oriented workplaces, but less accepted in the technology sector.

学者们发现，美国南部口音的特征在政府、法律和服务型职场等领域更易被接受，但在科技行业中则接受度较低。

The acceptability of nonstandard accents may correlate with differences in class and culture, with newer or higher prestige industries expecting more standard speech in the workplace.

非标准口音的可接受度可能与阶层和文化差异相关，较新的或声望较高的行业往往要求职场中使用更标准的言语。

A

Timed traffic lights are set according to schedules that are supposed to coordinate with peak traffic hours.

定时交通信号灯依据预设的时间表运行，这些时间表本应与交通高峰时段相协调。

Cities need to monitor and update signal light time schedules at least every two years to keep these systems effective.

各城市需要至少每两年监控并更新一次信号灯时间表，以确保这些系统保持有效。

In Los Angeles, California, an ambitious project to go a step further and synchronize every red light in Los Angeles cost over 400 million dollars.

在加利福尼亚州洛杉矶，一个更进一步的宏大项目——将洛杉矶所有红灯信号同步——耗资超过4亿美元。

Ask a resident of the city and they are likely to agree that saving time on the road is well worth it.

随便问一位当地居民，他们多半会认为节省路上的时间完全值得这笔花费。

If implemented nationwide, synchronization could shave 10% off traffic congestion and 20% off air pollution.

如果在全国范围内推行，信号同步可以减少10%的交通拥堵和20%的空气污染。

Congestion costs time, money, and lives.

交通拥堵浪费时间、耗费金钱，甚至夺走生命。

Big data can help improve how millions of people safely share the road.

大数据可以帮助改善数百万人安全共享道路的方式。

However, none of this would be possible without the traffic light!

然而，如果没有交通信号灯，这一切都不可能实现！

B

The first traffic light was invented in London in the 1860's, but it was hardly recognizable — or effective.

世界上第一盏交通信号灯于19世纪60年代在伦敦发明，但当时的样子几乎让人认不出来，也谈不上有效。

It had been adapted from the railroad signal system by a railway manager, John Peak Knight, in 1868.

1868年，铁路管理员约翰·皮克·奈特将其从铁路信号系统改造而成。

The railroads used red gas lights at night to signal stops and green gas lights during the daytime.

铁路系统在夜间使用红色煤气灯表示停车，白天则使用绿色煤气灯。

Knight installed a similar gas light, semaphore system near London's Westminster Bridge in December of 1868.

1868年12月，奈特在伦敦威斯敏斯特大桥附近安装了一套类似的煤气灯臂板信号系统。

C

The life of the first traffic light signal was short-lived.

世界上第一座交通信号灯的寿命很短。

A gas leak caused one of the signal lights to explode on its police officer operator within a month of being installed.

安装还不到一个月，一次煤气泄漏就导致其中一盏信号灯在执勤警察面前爆炸。

D

Automating traffic signals stalled after the London incident.

伦敦事故之后，交通信号灯的自动化进程一度陷入停滞。

Forty years passed before cars crowding city streets urged the development of traffic lights in the United States.

又过了四十年，城市街道上日益拥堵的汽车才促使美国发展交通信号灯。

London would not see another traffic light until 1929.

此后伦敦直到1929年才再次出现交通信号灯。

E

The United States is a car culture like no other country.

美国是一个独一无二的汽车文化国家。

Unlike most European cities that started urban planning when horses were high-tech, many cities in the United States were designed around urban sprawl, suburban tracts, and motor cars.

大多数欧洲城市早在马匹还是高科技的时代就开始了城市规划，而美国的许多城市却是围绕城市扩张、郊区大片住宅区和汽车来设计的。

F

As traffic and cities grew, so did the race to solve growing traffic problems.

随着交通流量和城市规模的增长，解决日益严峻的交通问题的竞赛也在不断升级。

The first traffic signals were semaphores.

最早的交通信号装置是臂板信号机。

Semaphores are towers with moving arms signaling for traffic to stop or go.

臂板信号机是带有活动臂杆的塔架，臂杆摆动来示意车辆停下或通行。

They could be manual or automatic.

它们既可以是手动的，也可以是自动的。

There was no consistent design.

其设计并无统一标准。

Various models were invented and put into place in cities as the need arose.

随着需求的出现，各种各样的方案被发明出来，并在城市中投入实施。

G

In 1912, Lester Wire was a police officer exhausted by traffic at Main Street and 200 South.

1912年，警官莱斯特·怀尔在主街与南200街路口被交通问题搞得精疲力竭。

He developed a manually switched stop light.

他研制出一种手动切换的红绿灯。

The four-sided box, raised on a ten-foot pole, looked like a birdhouse and was operated manually by an officer.

这个四方形的箱体架在一根十英尺高的杆子上，看起来像一个鸟舍，由一名警官手动操作。

The officer would flip a switch to change between the dipped red and green lights.

警官只需拨动开关，就能在红色和绿色灯光之间切换。

H

Like the 1910 semaphore, the first electric signal used words.

与1910年的信号臂一样，最早的电气信号也是用文字显示的。

However, the words were no longer written on arms that raised and fell.

只不过，文字不再写在上下起落的信号臂上。

The words "stop" or "move" were on lights.

"停"或"行"字样直接显示在灯光上。

The posts lit up on each of four corner-posts around an intersection.

路口四周的四根角柱上都装有可点亮的柱灯。

It was not automated, though.

不过，这套系统并非自动化。

A booth with an operator was required to flip the switches.

需要设一个岗亭，由操作员负责拨动开关。

I

The system allowed police officers to move from the middle of the street to a corner.

这套系统使警官可以从街道中央转移到路口角落。

From that vantage, an officer could oversee the crowd.

从这一有利位置，执勤警员可以俯瞰人群。

If an emergency vehicle came, the officer could flip a switch and clear the intersection by triggering all red lights.

如有应急车辆驶来，警员只需扳动开关，触发所有红灯，即可清空路口。

The emergency vehicle could now pass effortlessly.

这样一来，应急车辆便可畅通无阻地通过。

Finally, in San Francisco the first red and green electric light system that could be operated automatically (or manually) was introduced in 1917.

最后，1917年，旧金山启用了第一套可自动（或手动）操控的红绿灯系统。

J

In 1920, the first three-color light system was introduced in Detroit — also known as Motor City, USA.

1920年，底特律——又称美国汽车城——启用了第一套三色信号灯系统。

The evolution of traffic light signals could not have a more appropriate context than Motor City.

红绿灯信号的演进，没有比汽车城更为恰当的背景了。

Ford Motor Company was founded in Detroit in 1903 (and other motor companies followed).

福特汽车公司于1903年在底特律创立（随后其他汽车公司也相继成立）。

Ford made cars affordable for more common people, rather than the wealthy, by creating larger scale factory production.

福特通过大规模工厂生产，使汽车不再是富人的专属，而为普通大众所负担得起。

K

Stop and go were insufficient for the new volumes of cars zooming through intersections.

对于穿梭于路口的庞大车流量来说，仅靠红灯停、绿灯行已远远不够。

Streets were full of new numbers of cars in addition to delivery wagons, bustling crowds, bicycles, horse-drawn carriages, and street cars.

街道上除了新增的大量汽车，还有送货马车、熙攘的人群、自行车、马车以及有轨电车，可谓车水马龙。

The introduction of the cautionary yellow light helped increase driver safety, decreasing accidents that come from running red lights.

警示性黄色信号灯的引入有助于提升驾驶安全，减少了因闯红灯而引发的事故。

L

Stop lights were incredibly important in chaotic city streets.

在混乱的城市街道上，红绿灯至关重要。

Crosswalks weren't even introduced until 1951.

人行横道直到1951年才出现。

The first pedestrian fatality in the United States was in 1899.

美国第一起行人死亡事故发生在1899年。

M

The development of the modern traffic light may have started in London, but it grew up in the United States.

现代交通信号灯的发展或许始于伦敦，但它是在美国发展成熟起来的。

In Cleveland, an African-American inventor named Garrett Morgan came up with an affordable, patented traffic signal.

在克利夫兰，一位名叫加勒特·摩根的非裔美国发明家设计出了一款价格低廉、获得专利的交通信号灯。

The yellow light made intersections safer than the old stop and go system.

黄灯的出现使十字路口比旧式的"停/走"信号系统更安全。

The new design was inexpensive enough that it allowed the installation of a lot more lights.

新设计成本足够低，使得大量安装信号灯成为可能。

N

Morgan sold the patent to General Electric — and the spread of the traffic light was green lighted.

摩根把专利卖给了通用电气公司——交通信号灯的推广由此"亮起了绿灯"。

Green means go.

绿灯意味着通行。

It is that simple, but it's still best to check both ways for oncoming traffic that may not be paying attention or is running the light.

就这么简单，但最好还是朝两边看看有没有驶来的车辆——它们可能注意力不集中，或者正在闯红灯。

Yellow means slow down and prepare to stop.

黄灯意味着减速并准备停车。

It does not mean full-throttle ahead.

这并不意味着全速前进。

While you may get away with gunning it into the intersection, running a yellow can lead to tickets and dangerous accidents.

虽然你或许能侥幸一脚油门冲过路口，但闯黄灯可能会招来罚单，甚至引发危险的交通事故。

Red lights are a clear and consistent warning to stop.

红灯是明确且一致的停车信号。

Even where right turns are allowed on red lights, you must come to a complete stop before turning right.

即使在允许红灯右转的路口，你也必须在右转前完全停车。

You must also yield to any through traffic, which has the right of way.

你还必须让行享有通行权的直行车辆。

In most states and cities, you can take a right on red unless otherwise indicated.

在大多数州和城市，除非另有指示，你可以在红灯时右转。

New York City does not allow a right on red.

纽约市不允许红灯右转。

O

Red arrows mean you cannot turn right on a red in most states.

在大多数州，红色箭头表示不能在红灯时右转。

The green arrow, in these cases, is the only time you can make the turn.

在这种情况下，只有绿色箭头亮起时才能转弯。

However, you can take a right on a red arrow after a full stop in the states of Illinois, Kansas, Louisiana, Maryland, Michigan, New Hampshire, North Dakota, Oregon, South Carolina, Utah, Washington State and Wyoming.

不过，在伊利诺伊、堪萨斯、路易斯安那、马里兰、密歇根、新罕布什尔、北达科他、俄勒冈、南卡罗来纳、犹他、华盛顿州和怀俄明这些州，完全停车后可以按红色箭头指示右转。

P

Flashing Red is the same as a stop sign, generally used at four-way intersections where there is minimal traffic.

闪烁红灯等同于停车标志，通常设在车流量很少的四向交叉路口。

When arriving at a flashing red light, make a full and complete stop.

遇到闪烁红灯时，必须完全停车。

Flashing Red Arrows similarly mean to stop and then proceed to take the turn.

闪烁的红色箭头同样表示先停车，然后再转弯。

Flashing Yellow lights mean yield or signal caution, like outside of a fire station.

闪烁的黄灯表示让行或警示，例如设在消防站附近。

There are no all-direction flashing yellow lights, since it is prohibited in the United States.

由于美国禁止使用全方向闪烁黄灯，因此不存在这种信号灯。

If you have a flashing yellow light, you are to yield to oncoming traffic.

如果遇到闪烁的黄灯，你应当让行对向来车。

Q

Flashing Yellow Arrows are part of a four-signal traffic light.

闪烁黄箭头是四灯式信号灯的一部分。

These are used in some states after a protected green turn signal, or arrow, but before a steady yellow arrow.

在美国一些州，这种信号用于受保护的绿色转弯信号（即绿色箭头）之后、恒亮黄箭头之前。

When a flashing yellow arrow is shown, you can take the turn after yielding to pedestrians and traffic.

当出现闪烁黄箭头时，可在让行行人和车辆后进行转弯。

Steady Yellow Arrows maintain the normal yellow light caution in three-light signals, meaning to stop or prepare to stop.

恒亮黄箭头与三灯式信号灯中的普通黄灯含义相同，表示停车或准备停车。

In a four-signal system that has a flashing yellow arrow, the steady yellow arrow is a signal that the yield period is ending.

在设有闪烁黄箭头的四灯式信号系统中，恒亮黄箭头表示让行时段即将结束。

The red arrow is about to start.

红色箭头即将亮起。

Vehicles should stop if safely possible.

如能安全停车，车辆应当停车。

Traffic tickets are possible for running a steady yellow arrow.

闯恒亮黄箭头可能会被开具交通罚单。

R

Traffic light meaning is fairly consistent across the board with various deviations.

各地交通信号灯的含义总体相当一致，但也存在各种差异。

Green means it's safe to proceed (though you should still exercise caution).

绿灯表示可以安全通行（但仍应保持谨慎）。

Yellow means caution and yield.

黄色代表警示与让行。

Red means danger and stop.

红色代表危险与停止。

Always drive defensively and obey traffic signals.

行车时应始终保持防御性驾驶，并遵守交通信号。

Section C · 仔细阅读 Passage 1453 词 · 19 句

P1

Mindfulness has been shown to have a number of meaningful health benefits — it can help reduce anxiety and promote healing in those suffering from chronic illness.

研究表明，正念对健康有诸多益处——它有助于减轻焦虑，并促进慢性病患者的康复。

P2

The practice is based on an insight first described in ancient Buddhist texts that human beings have the capacity to observe experience without being caught up in it.

这种练习基于古代佛教典籍中最早提出的一种洞见：人类有能力观察自己的体验而不深陷其中。

This means simply that it is possible to observe ourselves having a craving, or a happy thought, or even a scary emotion, without reacting in the moment in a way that amplifies the feeling or sends the mind spiraling off into thinking about old memories or anticipating events.

这简单地说，就是我们可以观察到自己正在产生某种渴望、某个快乐的念头，甚至一种可怕的情绪，而不会在当下作出反应，放大这种感受，或让思绪失控地陷入对往事的回忆、对未来的预想。

P3

This practice of mindfulness can help calm the mind and the body as we learn not to react to experience with likes and dislikes or judgments of good and bad.

当我们学会不再以好恶或是非评判来回应体验时，正念练习有助于使身心平静下来。

It does not make us cold or indifferent but more fully present.

它不会让我们变得冷漠或麻木，而是让我们更加全然地活在当下。

P4

One of the challenges of practicing mindfulness in our contemporary world is that there has been a profound transformation in human attention.

在当代社会践行正念面临的挑战之一，是人类的注意力已经发生了深刻的转变。

The artist Jenny Odell argues that in our "attention economy" human attention has been transformed into a commodity that big corporations buy and sell.

艺术家珍妮·奥德尔指出，在我们的“注意力经济”中，人类的注意力已被转化为一种由大公司买卖的商品。

This economy rests on a technological revolution of mobile phones and social media that makes it possible for corporations to target our attention with their content at every moment.

这种经济建立在移动电话和社交媒体的技术革命之上，使企业得以随时随地用他们的内容争夺我们的注意力。

P5

The devices most people use are a perpetual diversion from the present moment.

大多数人使用的设备，让人一刻不停地偏离当下。

The result is that it can feel as though our ability to focus, and be fully present, has been stolen.

结果便是，我们似乎觉得自己的专注能力和全然临在的能力已被窃取。

But mindfulness can help us resist the attention economy and relish the things that make life special, like being together with those we love.

但正念可以帮助我们抵御注意力经济的侵蚀，尽情品味那些让生活变得美好的事物，比如与所爱之人相伴。

P6

Most mindfulness research focuses on the individual benefits of the practice, but some scholars assert that we can not only practice mindfulness for ourselves but also practice it for others.

大多数关于正念的研究都聚焦于练习对个人的益处，但有些学者主张，我们不仅可以为自己修炼正念，也可以为他人修炼正念。

It can help us build stronger, healthier relationships.

它可以帮助我们建立更牢固、更健康的亲密关系。

P7

The sad truth is that living in the attention economy, most of us have become bad listeners.

可悲的事实是，在注意力经济中生活，我们大多数人都变成了糟糕的倾听者。

However, just as it is possible to watch ourselves having an experience without reacting, it's possible to watch another person have an experience without getting tied up in reactivity and judgment.

然而，正如我们可以在不做出反应的情况下观察自己的经历一样，我们也可以在不被情绪反应和评判牵绊的情况下去观察另一个人的经历。

P8

The gift of mindfulness is a practice of listening with compassion to others describe their experiences.

正念的馈赠，是一种带着慈悲心倾听他人讲述自身经历的修行。

To give this gift means putting away your phone, turning off social media, and setting aside other common distractions.

要送出这份礼物，意味着收起手机、关闭社交媒体，并将其他常见的干扰源暂时搁置一旁。

It means practicing being fully present in another person's presence and listening to them with complete attention without judgment, while resisting the urge to make the interaction about you.

这意味着练习在另一个人面前全然在场，带着完全的专注去倾听，不加评判，同时克制住把互动变成以自己为中心的冲动。

P9

If we judge the value of gifts based on their price, this gift may seem worthless, but in a distracted world, it is a precious one.

如果我们以价格来衡量礼物的价值，这份礼物或许看似一文不值；但在一个充满分心的世界里，它却无比珍贵。

Section C · 仔细阅读 Passage 2451 词 · 27 句

P1

The other day I had to log into a service I hadn't used before.

前几天，我需要登录一个从未使用过的服务。

Since I was a new user, the website decided that it needed to check I wasn't a robot and so set me a Captcha test to determine whether the user is a person or a machine.

由于我是新用户，网站认为有必要确认我不是机器人，于是给我出了一道验证码测试，以判断用户是真人还是机器。

P2

I was presented with an image of a roadside scene over which was laid a grid.

我面前出现了一张路边场景的图片，上面覆盖着一层网格。

My "challenge" was to click on each cell in the grid that contained a traffic sign.

我的“挑战”是点击网格中每一个含有交通标志的方格。

I did so, a bit irritated.

我照做了，心里有些恼火。

Then I was presented with another image and another grid — also with a request to identify road signs.

接着又出现了一张新图片和一层新的网格——同样是要求识别道路标志。

Like a lamb, I complied, after which the website accepted my input.

我像个羔羊一样乖乖照做，之后网站才接受了我的输入。

P3

And then I realised what I had been doing was adding to a dataset for training the machine-learning software that guides self-driving cars.

随后我才意识到，我所做的其实是在为训练指导自动驾驶汽车的机器学习软件提供数据集。

So, to gain access to an automated service that will benefit financially from my input, I first have to do some unpaid labor to help improve the performance of autonomous vehicles.

也就是说，为了使用一项能从我的输入中获利的自动化服务，我首先得无偿劳动，以帮助提升自动驾驶车辆的性能。

P4

Neat, eh?

够精明的吧？

But note also the delicious additional irony that the Captcha is described as an "automated Turing test".

但请注意其中另一层绝妙的讽刺：这道验证码被称为“自动图灵测试”。

The Turing test was conceived, you may recall, as a way of enabling humans to determine whether a machine could respond in such a way that one couldn't tell whether it was a human or a robot.

你或许还记得，图灵测试的构想是让人能够判断机器的应答方式是否让人无法分辨它是人还是机器人。

So we have wandered into a world in which machines make us do tricks to prove that we are humans!

于是我们误入了一个怪圈：机器反倒要我们耍把式、做题，来证明自己是人！

P5

The strangest aspect of this unparalleled shift is how under-discussed it has been.

这场前所未有的变革最奇怪的一点，是人们对此讨论之少。

The metaphor of the boiling frog comes to mind.

这让人想起温水煮青蛙的比喻。

Have we become so subtly conditioned by digital technology that we don't see what's been happening to us?

我们是否已被数字技术潜移默化地驯化，以至于对发生在自己身上的事情浑然不觉？

Have we been conditioned to accept a world governed by "smart" tech, trading convenience to the point where we become a bit like machines ourselves?

我们是否已被驯化到接受一个由“智能”科技主导的世界，为了换取便利而不断让步，最终自己也变得有点像机器？

P6

In a recent startling and thoughtful book, two scholars — Brett Frischmann, a law professor, and Evan Selinger, a philosopher — argue that the answer to that question is "yes".

最近一本发人深省的著作中，两位学者——法学教授布雷特·弗里希曼和哲学家埃文·塞林格——认为，对这个问题的回答是“是的”。

P7

And they're right.

他们说得没错。

There's nothing technophobic about that.

这绝非什么技术恐惧症。

We shape our tools and then our tools shape us.

我们塑造工具，工具继而塑造我们。

Technology is supposed to provide tools that serve human ends.

技术本应提供服务于人类目的的工具。

But, as the machine-learning Captcha demonstrates, a significant proportion of digital tech now sees (and uses) humans as means to ends that are not ours.

然而，正如机器学习版验证码所表明的，如今相当一部分数字技术把人视为（并当作）实现非人类目的的手段。

In the process, they reduce us to the status of cheery rats running on treadmills designed by people who do not have our interests at heart.

在这个过程中，我们被降格成了兴高采烈地在跑步机上奔跑的老鼠，而设计跑步机的人并不把我们的利益放在心上。

So back to the frog metaphor.

那么，再回到青蛙的比喻。

Are we smart enough to jump out before it's too late?

我们是否足够聪明，能在为时已晚之前跳出来？

You don't even have to Google it to know the answer.

你甚至不需要上网搜索就能知道答案。
