A

According to recent research, behavioral change involves physical changes in the brain.

根据近期研究，行为的改变伴随着大脑的生理变化。

In the past decade, researchers have shown that when it comes to the duration of making a new behavior a deep-rooted habit there is not a simple answer.

过去十年间，研究人员发现，关于把新行为变成根深蒂固的习惯需要多长时间，并没有一个简单的答案。

Even for the most productive and disciplined among us, undoing something that has become an automatic part of who we are takes more than an overnight effort.

即使对于我们当中最高效、最有自律性的人来说，要摒弃已经成为我们自身自动组成部分的行为，也绝非一夜之间就能做到。

Once we've successfully made that change, we then have to make other adjustments to our lives to ensure that we continue to maintain it, which is often a whole other challenge in itself.

一旦成功实现了这一改变，我们还得对生活作出其他调整，以确保能够持续保持下去，而这本身往往又是另一大挑战。

B

At its core, success in changing and maintaining a behavior rarely occurs without the introduction of some sort of system.

从本质上讲，成功改变并维持一种行为，几乎离不开某种体系的引入。

When there isn't the right framework in place, we face a greater likelihood of derailing our hard-earned progress.

当缺少恰当的框架时，我们便更有可能让来之不易的进展半途而废。

To ensure success in changing and maintaining a behavior, we should stay away from some detrimental habits.

为了确保成功改变并保持一种行为，我们应当远离某些有害的习惯。

关于人的意志力是有限还是无限的研究尚无定论，但专家们普遍认为，如果你仅仅依靠意志力，就无法改变并坚持一个习惯。

The old military saying "You never rise to the occasion, you only sink to the level of training" also applies to behavior change.

军事上有一句老话：'你永远无法临场超常发挥，你只会达到训练水平'，这句话同样适用于行为改变。

The idea is simple—you repeat something so many times that it becomes automatic.

其原理很简单——你反复做某件事，直到它成为一种自动行为。

The second one to avoid is focusing on negative goals.

第二个需要避免的是专注于负面目标。

Sometimes, it's not your process that lets you down, but the habit that you want to change in the first place.

有时候，让你失望的不是你所遵循的过程，而恰恰是你最初想要改变的那个习惯。

However, this habit-learning system isn't so effective when it comes to learning not to do something.

然而，这套习惯学习机制在学会"不做"某件事方面效果并不好。

That's why rather than giving up something, think about introducing something in its place.

Focus on actions you are going to take that will ultimately conflict with the behaviors you want to stop.

When your attention is on doing something new, you give your habit system a chance to operate.

当你把注意力放在做一件新事情上时，你就给了自己的习惯系统一个发挥作用的机会。

The third one to avoid is using the same strategies in different circumstances.

第三个需要避免的是在不同情境下使用相同的策略。

Because we are creatures of habit, it's natural to assume that when we do manage to adopt and sustain a desirable behavior, that same strategy will work when we want to make another behavior change.

因为我们是习惯的动物，所以很自然地会认为，既然我们成功养成并坚持了一种可取的行为，那么在想要改变另一种行为时，同样的策略也会奏效。

But that's not always the case.

但情况并非总是如此。

Sometimes, the system that got you to change one behavior might not work for another.

有时候，帮你改变了一种行为的那套方法，未必适用于另一种行为。

Sometimes we become accustomed to relying on our guts when it comes to decision-making.

有时，我们在做决定时会习惯于依赖直觉。

This serves us well in certain situations, but can hinder us in others especially when we need to consider metrics and data, rather than letting our instinct override everything.

这在某些情况下对我们颇有裨益，但在另一些情况下却可能成为阻碍，尤其是当我们需要考量指标和数据，而不是让直觉凌驾于一切之上时。

For example, if you want to stop checking your email first thing in the morning, you might decide to substitute another activity in its place.

例如，如果你想改掉早上一睁眼就查看电子邮件的习惯，你可能会决定用另一项活动来替代它。

But if you want to stop indulging in video games, simply deciding you will go for a run might not be as effective.

但如果你想戒掉沉迷电子游戏的习惯，仅仅决定去跑步可能就没有那么有效。

You might need to introduce another reinforcement, such as meeting a friend and booking an exercise class together.

你可能需要引入另一种强化机制，比如约上朋友一起报一个健身课。

I

The fourth one to avoid is not forgiving ourselves for slipping up.

要避免的第四点，就是因一时失误便不肯原谅自己。

Of course, even the best-laid plans fail sometimes.

当然，即使是计划得再周全的方案，有时也会失败。

You might have stuck to your screen-free nighttime routine for five days, and then a big project landed on your desk and you found yourself in bed with your laptop before you went to sleep.

你可能已经连续五天坚持睡前远离屏幕的作息，结果一个大项目突然落到你桌上，你发现自己又抱着笔记本电脑上了床。

Or you prepared meals on Sunday and stuck to eating healthy dinners at home, but by Friday you found yourself so exhausted and opted to order greasy takeout.

又或者你在周日就备好了餐食，坚持在家吃健康的晚餐，可到了周五你精疲力竭，最终选择了油腻的外卖。

Life happens and even if your behavior change is small, every single day can prove pretty inflexible, and at some point your luck may run out, even if just for a day.

生活中总有意外发生，即使你的行为改变幅度很小，每一天都可能充满变数，总有一天运气会用尽，哪怕只是一天也不例外。

The perfectionist in you might be screaming to abandon your goals altogether, but try to see it in the bigger picture.

你内心那个完美主义者可能正咆哮着让你干脆放弃目标，但请试着从大局来看待这件事。

Just because you might have temporarily strayed off course doesn't mean you can't start afresh the next day.

即便你一时偏离了轨道，也不意味着你不能在第二天重新开始。

J

The final one to avoid is discounting small progress.

最后一个要避免的误区是轻视微小的进步。

There's a habit that many perfectionists tend to fall into when they try to establish a behavior change.

许多完美主义者在尝试改变行为时，往往会陷入一种习惯。

They focus too much on the big goal and don't take the time to celebrate the small progress they make in the process.

他们过于关注宏大的目标，却不肯花时间去庆祝自己在此过程中取得的点滴进步。

Your brain responds to rewards.

大脑会对奖励作出反应。

The basal ganglia, the brain region linked to our performance of habits, is most active at the beginning of a behavior, when the habit is cued, and at the end, when it's rewarded.

基底神经节是大脑中与习惯行为执行相关的区域，它在行为开始（习惯被触发）和行为结束（获得奖励）时最为活跃。

Say your goal is to run five miles three times a week, and this week you ran one mile on Monday, Wednesday, and Friday.

假设你的目标是每周跑步三次、每次跑五英里，而这周你在周一、周三和周五各跑了一英里。

Rather than focusing on how far you've gone toward your goal, think about how you can reward yourself for the progress you've made.

与其盯着自己离目标还有多远，不如想想如何为自己已经取得的进步犒赏自己。

It doesn't have to be big or expensive; it can be something as simple as making your favorite fruit juice after your run.

奖励不必隆重或昂贵，可以简单到跑完步后榨一杯你最爱的果汁。

Whatever your reward, it has to be more than just the activity itself to get you going.

无论奖励是什么，它必须超越活动本身，才能真正激励你行动起来。

K

原因之一在于，我们错误地以为，当初用来启动改变的策略，在帮助我们维持改变时同样有效。

But they won't.

但他们不会。

If our mood is low, the wisdom to behave differently seems to disappear and we go back to eating more and exercising less.

如果我们情绪低落，做出不同行为的智慧似乎就会消失，我们便会重新回到吃得更多、运动更少的状态。

The key, then, to maintaining new behaviors is to be happy!

请记住，克服阻碍我们落实新改变的行为惯性——比如健康饮食或坚持锻炼——从长远来看对我们有益，能够改善我们的身心健康。

They were all learned, and can all, therefore, be unlearned.

所有习惯都是后天习得的，因此也都可以被戒除。

The question is: how badly do you really want to change?

问题在于：你究竟有多想改变？

P2

"Without my job I don't know who I am," is a sentence that has been uttered on more than a handful of occasions from my office chair.

“没有了工作，我就不知道自己是谁了。”——这样的话，我坐在办公室的椅子上，已经不知听过多少次。

Indeed, it can be one of the most challenging aspects I work on with clients who have lost or been forced into changing their jobs.

事实上，对于那些失去工作或被迫转行的来访者，这可能是我与他们共同面对的最具挑战性的问题之一。

This loss provokes an identity crisis much greater than the loss of the job itself.

这种丧失引发的身份危机，远比失去工作本身要严重得多。

P3

One of the things I have come to understand, however, is that our identity is much more complex than we recognise at first glance.

然而，我逐渐明白的一点是：我们的身份远比乍看之下要复杂得多。

If we take the time to reflect we might recognise that as well as our work we can also identify as a friend, a spouse, a son or daughter, a parent, a member of a sports team or religious community.

如果肯花时间反思，我们或许会意识到，除了工作，我们还可以是朋友、配偶、子女、父母、运动队成员或宗教团体的一员。

We may recognise that we feel and act differently in these roles and relationships than we do at work.

我们或许会察觉到，在这些角色和关系中，自己的感受和行为方式与工作时截然不同。

The passive daughter becomes an assertive leader at work.

那个在家中温顺的女儿，在职场上却是一位果敢的领导者。

Furthermore, our identities at work are not static.

此外，我们在职场中的身份并非一成不变。

They change over time.

它们会随时间而改变。

I myself have been a shop assistant, a waitress, a student, a graduate, and a clinical psychologist.

我本人曾做过店员、服务员、学生、毕业生和临床心理学家。

At each stage my ability to adapt to and develop my career identity has been crucial to my wellbeing.

在每一个阶段，我适应并发展职业身份的能力对我的幸福感都至关重要。

Whilst we like to eliminate uncertainty in our lives at some level we have to manage uncertainty, especially in today's volatile and ever-shifting job market.

虽然我们在某种程度上希望消除生活中的不确定性，但有时不得不应对不确定性，尤其是在当今动荡且瞬息万变的就业市场中。

P4

How we see ourselves is central to the issue of our identity.

我们如何看待自己，是身份认同问题的核心。

When we tell ourselves "I'm good at starting projects but not so great at seeing them through" it can become part of our belief system.

当我们告诉自己"我擅长启动项目，但不太擅长把它们做完"时，这种说法可能成为我们信念体系的一部分。

But if you have the unfortunate experience of an enforced job change you will need to examine those beliefs to see how grounded in reality they are.

但如果你不幸遭遇被迫的职业转变，你就需要审视这些信念，看看它们在多大程度上符合现实。

You will be required to ask yourself how helpful these beliefs are and consider personal change.

你需要扪心自问这些信念究竟有多大帮助，并考虑做出个人改变。

We can change our beliefs, behaviours and emotional experience at any time through experimentation, practice and conscious self-discipline.

我们随时都可以通过尝试、练习和有意识的自我约束来改变自己的信念、行为和情绪体验。

In an age where career progression may lead us into new sectors it is ever more important to challenge our sense of self and explore whether you can create a new experience of your identity by changing the beliefs you hold about yourself in order to expand your career options.

在职业发展可能引领我们进入新行业的时代，审视自我认知变得尤为重要，探索你是否能通过改变对自己的固有信念来创造新的身份体验，从而拓宽职业选择。

Ultimately it is you who define who you are.

归根结底，是你自己定义了你是谁。

You are only your job if you let it be so.

只有当你任由工作定义自己时，你才会仅仅等同于你的工作。
