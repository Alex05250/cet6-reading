A rainbow is a multi-colored, arc-shaped phenomenon that can appear in the sky.

彩虹是一种可以出现在天空中的多色弧形现象。

The colors of a rainbow are produced by the reflection and dispersion of light through water droplets present in the atmosphere.

彩虹的颜色是由光在大气中的水滴上发生反射和色散而产生的。

An observer may perceive a rainbow to be located either near or far away, but this phenomenon is not actually located at any specific spot.

观察者可能会觉得彩虹出现在近处或远处，但实际上这一现象并不位于任何特定的地点。

Instead, the appearance of a rainbow depends entirely upon the position of the observer in relation to the direction of light.

相反，彩虹的出现完全取决于观察者相对于光线方向所处的位置。

In essence, a rainbow is an optical illusion.

从本质上说，彩虹是一种光学错觉。

Rainbows present a spectrum made up of seven colors in a specific order.

彩虹呈现出由七种颜色按特定顺序组成的光谱。

In fact, school children in many English-speaking countries are taught to remember the name "Roy G. Biv" as an aid for remembering the colors of a rainbow and their order.

事实上，许多英语国家的学童都被教导记住“Roy G. Biv”这个名字，以此帮助记忆彩虹的颜色及其排列顺序。

"Roy G. Biv" stands for: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, and violet.

“Roy G. Biv”代表：红（red）、橙（orange）、黄（yellow）、绿（green）、蓝（blue）、靛（indigo）和紫（violet）。

The outer edge of the rainbow arc is red, while the inner edge is violet.

彩虹弧的外缘是红色，而内缘是紫色。

A rainbow is formed when light (generally sunlight) passes through water droplets hanging in the atmosphere.

当光（通常是阳光）穿过悬浮在大气中的水滴时，彩虹便形成了。

The light waves change direction as they pass through the water droplets, resulting in two processes: reflection and refraction.

光波在穿过水滴时改变方向，从而产生两个过程：反射和折射。

When light reflects off a water droplet, it simply bounces back in the opposite direction from where it originates.

当光从水滴上反射时，它只是朝着与入射方向相反的方向弹回。

When light refracts, it takes a different direction.

光发生折射时，其传播方向会发生改变。

Some individuals refer to refracted light as "bent light waves."

有些人把折射光称为“弯曲的光波”。

A rainbow is formed because white light enters the water droplet, where it bends in several different directions.

彩虹之所以形成，是因为白光进入水滴后，在其中向几个不同方向发生偏折。

When these bent light waves reach the other side of the water droplet, they reflect back out of the droplet instead of completely passing through the water.

当这些弯曲的光波到达水滴的另一侧时，它们会反射回来并射出水滴，而不是完全穿过水滴。

Since the white light is separated inside of the water, the refracted light appears as separate colors to the human eye.

由于白光在水滴内部被分离开来，折射后的光在人眼中就呈现出各种不同的颜色。

Section B · 长篇阅读（信息匹配）1202 词 · 51 句

A

Phyllis Hartman knows what it's like to make one's way through the depths of office meeting hell.

菲利斯·哈特曼深知在一场又一场令人窒息的会议室炼狱中摸爬滚打是何种滋味。

Managers at one of her former human resources jobs arranged so many meetings that attendees would fall asleep at the table or intentionally arrive late.

她曾在一家人力资源机构任职，那里的管理者安排了太多会议，以至于与会者在会议桌前打瞌睡，或故意迟到。

With hours of her day blocked up with unnecessary meetings, she was often forced to make up her work during overtime.

一天中大量时间被无关紧要的会议占据，她常常被迫在加班时补做本职工作。

"I was actually working more hours than I probably would have needed to get the work done," says Hartman, who is founder and president of PGHR Consulting in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania.

“我实际工作的时长，比完成这些工作原本所需的可能还要多。”哈特曼说道。她是匹兹堡PGHR咨询公司的创始人兼总裁，公司位于宾夕法尼亚州匹兹堡市。

B

She isn't alone in her frustration.

感到沮丧的并不只有她一人。

Between 11 million and 55 million meetings are held each day in the United States, costing most organisations between 7% and 15% of their personnel budgets.

美国每天召开的会议在1100万至5500万场之间，耗费了大多数组织7%至15%的人力预算。

Every week, employees spend about six hours in meetings, while the average manager meets for a staggering 23 hours.

每周，员工花费约六小时参加会议，而管理者的会议时间更是高达惊人的23小时。

C

And though experts agree that traditional meetings are essential for making certain decisions and developing strategy, some employees view them as one of the most unnecessary parts of the workday.

尽管专家一致认为，传统会议对于作出特定决策和制定战略不可或缺，但一些员工却视其为工作日中最没有必要的环节之一。

The result is not only hundreds of billions of wasted dollars, but an annoyance of what organisational psychologists call "meeting recovery syndrome (MRS)": time spent cooling off and regaining focus after a useless meeting.

其结果不仅是数千亿美元的浪费，还有组织心理学家所称的“会议恢复综合征（MRS）”所带来的困扰：即在一场毫无意义的会议之后，人们需要时间平复情绪、重新集中注意力。

If you run to the office kitchen to get some relief with colleagues after a frustrating meeting, you're likely experiencing meeting recovery syndrome.

如果一场令人沮丧的会议结束后，你跑去公司茶水间和同事聊聊天以求放松，那你很可能正在经历会议恢复综合征。

D

Meeting recovery syndrome is a concept that should be familiar to almost anyone who has held a formal job.

会议恢复综合征这一概念，对于几乎所有有过正式工作经历的人来说都不陌生。

It isn't ground-breaking to say workers feel fatigued after a meeting, but only in recent decades have scientists deemed the condition worthy of further investigation.

说员工在会议后感到疲惫并非什么开创性的观点，但直到近几十年来，科学家才认为这一状况值得进一步研究。

With its links to organisational efficiency and employee wellbeing, MRS has attracted the attention of psychologists aware of the need to understand its precise causes and cures.

由于与组织效率和员工身心健康密切相关，会议恢复综合征（MRS）已引起心理学家的关注，他们意识到有必要弄清其确切成因和对策。

E

Today, in so far as researchers can hypothesise, MRS is most easily understood as a slow renewal of finite mental and physical resources.

目前，在研究者所能提出的假设范围内，MRS最易被理解为有限的心智与体力资源的缓慢恢复过程。

When an employee sits through an ineffective meeting their brain power is essentially being drained away.

当员工熬过一场毫无成效的会议时，其脑力实际上正在被白白消耗。

Meetings drain vitality if they last too long, fail to engage employees or turn into one-sided lectures.

如果会议时间过长、无法调动员工参与，或沦为一言堂式的说教，就会损耗人的精力。

The conservation of resources theory, originally proposed in 1989 by Dr Stevan Hobfoll, states that psychological stress occurs when a person's resources are threatened or lost.

资源保存理论由斯蒂文·霍布福尔博士于1989年首次提出，该理论认为，当个人的资源受到威胁或丧失时，就会产生心理压力。

When resources are low, a person will shift into defence to conserve their remaining supply.

当资源不足时，人会转入防御状态，以保存剩余的资源储备。

In the case of office meetings, where some of employees' most valuable resources are their focus, alertness and motivation, this can mean an abrupt halt in productivity as they take time to recover.

就办公室会议而言，员工最宝贵的资源之一便是专注力、警觉性和积极性，这意味着生产力会骤然中断，因为他们需要时间来恢复。

F

As humans, when we transition from one task to another on the job—say from sitting in a meeting to doing normal work—it takes an effortful cognitive switch.

作为人类，在工作中从一项任务切换到另一项任务时——比如从开会转到正常工作——需要付出努力来完成认知转换。

We must detach ourselves from the previous task and expend significant mental energy to move on.

我们必须从上一项任务中抽离出来，并消耗大量脑力才能继续前行。

If we are already drained to dangerous levels, then making the mental switch to the next thing is extra tough.

如果我们已经精疲力竭到危险的程度，那么在心理上切换到下一项任务就会格外困难。

It's common to see people cyber-loafing after a frustrating meeting, going and getting coffee, interrupting a colleague and telling them about the meeting, and so on.

人们在经历一场令人沮丧的会议后上网摸鱼、去倒杯咖啡、打扰同事并跟他们聊会议的事等，都是很常见的现象。

G

Each person's ability to recover from horrible meetings is different.

每个人从糟糕的会议中恢复过来的能力各不相同。

Some can bounce back quickly, while others carry their fatigue until the end of the workday.

有些人能迅速恢复精力，而另一些人则会带着疲惫撑到工作日结束。

Yet while no formal MRS studies are currently underway, one can loosely speculate on the length of an average employee's lag time.

然而，尽管目前尚无正式的MRS（会议残留综合征）研究正在进行，人们还是可以大致推测一下普通员工的恢复时长。

Switching tasks in a non-MRS condition takes about 10 to 15 minutes.

在非MRS条件下切换任务大约需要10到15分钟。

With MRS, it may take as long as 45 minutes on average.

而在MRS条件下，平均可能需要长达45分钟。

It's even worse when a worker has several meetings that are separated by 30 minutes.

如果员工的几场会议之间仅间隔30分钟，情况就更糟了。

"Not enough time to transition in a non-MRS situation to get anything done, and in an MRS situation, not quite enough time to recover for the next meeting," says researcher Joseph Allen.

研究人员约瑟夫·艾伦说："在非MRS情况下，没有足够的时间完成任务切换；而在MRS情况下，也没有足够的时间为下一场会议恢复精力。"

"Then, add the compounding of back-to-back bad meetings and we may have an epidemic on our hands."

"再加上一场接一场糟糕会议的累积效应，我们可能正面临一场流行病。"

H

In an effort to combat the side effects of MRS, Allen, along with researcher Joseph Mroz and colleagues at the University of Nebraska-Omaha, published a study detailing the best ways to avoid common traps, including a concise checklist of do's and don'ts applicable to any workplace.

为了对抗MRS带来的副作用，艾伦与研究人员约瑟夫·姆罗兹以及内布拉斯加大学奥马哈分校的同事们合作，发表了一项研究，详细阐述了避免常见陷阱的最佳方法，其中包括一份适用于任何工作场所的简明"宜""忌"清单。

Drawing from around 200 papers to compile their comprehensive list, Mroz and his team may now hold a remedy to the largely undefined problem of MRS.

姆罗兹及其团队参考了约200篇论文来编制这份全面的清单，如今他们或许已经找到了针对这一在很大程度上尚未被定义的MRS问题的解决之道。

I

Mroz says a good place to start is asking ourselves if our meetings are even necessary in the first place.

姆罗兹说，一个好的起点是扪心自问：我们的会议是否真的有必要。

If all that's on the agenda is a quick catch-up, or some non-urgent information sharing, it may better suit the group to send around an email instead.

如果议程上只是简短的沟通交流，或是分享一些非紧急的信息，那么发一封电子邮件可能更适合这个团队。

"The second thing I would always recommend is keep the meeting as small as possible," says Mroz.

姆罗兹说："我总是建议的第二点，就是尽可能让会议规模保持小。"

"If they don't actually have some kind of immediate input, then they can follow up later. They don't need to be sitting in this hour-long meeting."

如果他们实际上并没有什么当场需要提供的意见，那么他们可以稍后再跟进。他们没必要坐在那儿听整整一小时的会议。

Less time in meetings would ultimately lead to more employee engagement in the meetings they do attend, which experts agree is a proven remedy for MRS.

减少会议时间，最终会让员工在真正参加的会议上更加投入；专家们一致认为，这是治疗MRS（会议后遗症）的可靠良方。

J

Employees also feel taxed when they are invited together to meetings that don't inspire participation, says Cliff Scott, professor of organisational science.

组织科学教授克利夫·斯科特说，当员工被一并召集去参加那些令人提不起参与兴致的会议时，也会感到不堪重负。

It takes precious time for them to vent their emotions, complain and try to regain focus after a pointless meeting—one of the main traps of MRS. Over time as employees find themselves tied up in more and more unnecessary meetings—and thus dealing with increasing lag times from MRS—the waste of workday hours can feel insulting.

一场毫无意义的会议之后，他们还要耗费宝贵的时间去发泄情绪、抱怨，并努力重新集中注意力——这正是MRS的主要陷阱之一。长此以往，当员工发现自己被卷入越来越多的无谓会议，因而要承受MRS越来越长的余波时，这种对工作时间的浪费会让人感到被冒犯。

K

Despite the relative scarcity of research behind the subject, Hartman has taught herself many of the same tricks suggested in Mroz's study, and has come a long way since her days of being stuck with unnecessary meetings.

尽管围绕这一主题的研究相对匮乏，哈特曼还是自学了许多与姆罗兹研究中所建议的相同技巧，与当初被无谓会议困住的日子相比，她已大有改观。

The people she invites to meetings today include not just the essential employees, but also representatives from every department that might have a stake in the issue at hand.

如今她邀请来开会的，不仅有关键员工，还有每一个可能与当前议题利害相关的部门的代表。

Managers like her, who seek input even from non-experts to shape their decisions, can find greater support and cooperation from their workforce, she says.

她说，像她这样的管理者，即便非专业人士的意见也会听取，以此来完善决策，因而能从员工那里获得更多的支持与配合。

L

If an organisation were to apply all 22 suggestions from Mroz and Allen's findings, the most noticeable difference would be a stark decrease in the total number of meetings on the schedule, Mroz says.

姆罗兹说，如果一个组织把姆罗兹与艾伦研究中的22条建议全部付诸实施，最明显的变化将是日程表上会议总数的大幅减少。

Less time in meetings would ultimately lead to increased productivity, which is the ultimate objective of convening a meeting.

减少会议时间最终会带来更高的生产力，而这正是召开会议的终极目的。

While none of the counter-MRS ideas have been tested empirically yet, Allen says one trick with promise is for employees to identify things that quickly change their mood from negative to positive.

艾伦说，尽管这些对抗MRS的办法尚未经过实证检验，但其中一项颇有希望的做法，是让员工找出那些能迅速让自己心情由阴转晴的事物。

As simple as it sounds, finding a personal happy place, going there and then coming straight back to work might be key to facilitating recovery.

听起来再简单不过，但找到属于自己的那片快乐天地，去那里待上一会儿，然后马上回到工作中，或许正是促进恢复的关键。

M

Leaders should also see themselves as "stewards of everyone else's valuable time", adds Steven Rogelberg, author of The Surprising Science of Meetings.

《会议的惊人科学》一书作者史蒂文·罗格尔伯格补充说，领导者也应把自己视为"他人宝贵时间的守护者"。

Having the skills to foresee potential traps and treat employees' endurance with care allows leaders to provide effective short-term deterrents to MRS.

领导者若具备预见潜在陷阱的技能，并能体恤员工的承受力，就能为应对MRS（会议恢复综合征）提供有效的短期预防手段。

N

Most important, however, is for organisations to awaken to the concept of meetings being flexible, says Allen.

然而，艾伦指出，最重要的是让组织意识到会议应当具有灵活性。

By reshaping the way they prioritise employees' time, companies can eliminate the very sources of MRS in their tracks.

通过重新调整对员工时间的优先排序方式，企业可以从源头上根除MRS的诱因。

Section C · 仔细阅读 Passage 1442 词 · 25 句

P1

Sarcasm and jazz have something surprisingly in common: You know them when you hear them.

讽刺和爵士乐有一个出人意料的共同点：一听就知道。

Sarcasm is mostly understood through tone of voice, which is used to portray the opposite of the literal words.

讽刺主要靠语调来理解，语调所传达的往往与字面意思相反。

For example, when someone says, "Well, that's exactly what I need right now," their tone can tell you it's not what they need at all.

比如，当有人说“嗯，这正是我现在所需要的”时，从他的语调你能听出，他根本不需要这个。

P2

Most frequently, sarcasm highlights an irritation or is, quite simply, mean.

讽刺最常用来表达恼怒，或者说，纯粹就是刻薄。

P3

If you want to be happier and improve your relationships, cut out sarcasm.

如果你想更快乐、改善人际关系，就别再说讽刺话了。

Why?

为什么？

Because sarcasm is actually hostility disguised as humor.

因为讽刺其实是披着幽默外衣的敌意。

P4

Despite smiling outwardly, many people who receive sarcastic comments feel put down and often think the sarcastic person is rude, or contemptible.

尽管表面上在笑，许多遭到讽刺的人却感到被贬低，并且常常认为讽刺者粗鲁或可鄙。

Indeed, it's not surprising that the origin of the word sarcasm derives from the Greek word "sarkazein" which literally means "to tear or strip the flesh off."

事实上，“讽刺”（sarcasm）一词源自希腊语“sarkazein”，字面意思是“撕扯或剥离皮肉”，这并不令人意外。

Hence, it's no wonder that sarcasm is often preceded by the word "cutting" and that it hurts.

因此，讽刺常被冠以“尖刻”（cutting）一词，而且会伤人，也就不足为奇了。

P5

What's more, since actions strongly determine thoughts and feelings, when a person consistently acts sarcastically it may only serve to heighten their underlying hostility and insecurity.

更重要的是，由于行为在很大程度上决定思想和感受，一个人若总是表现得尖酸刻薄，反而只会加剧其内心深处的敌意和不安全感。

After all, when you come right down to it, sarcasm can be used as a subtle form of bullying—and most bullies are angry, insecure, or cowardly.

毕竟说到底，讽刺可以被用作一种隐蔽的霸凌方式——而大多数霸凌者都是愤怒、缺乏安全感或懦弱的人。

P6

Alternatively, when a person stops voicing negative comments, especially sarcastic ones, they may soon start to feel happier and more self-confident.

反之，当一个人不再发表负面评论，尤其是带有讽刺意味的评论时，他往往会很快变得更快乐、更自信。

Also, other people in their life benefit even more because they no longer have to hear the emotionally hurtful language of sarcasm.

此外，他身边的其他人受益更大，因为他们再也不必听到那些带有情感伤害的讽刺语言了。

P7

Now, I'm not saying all sarcasm is bad.

当然，我并不是说所有讽刺都不好。

It may just be better used sparingly—like a potent spice in cooking.

讽刺或许只是少用为妙——就像烹饪中的一种烈性香料。

Too much of the spice, and the dish will be overwhelmed by it.

香料放得太多，整道菜就会被它的味道盖过。

Similarly, an occasional dash of sarcastic wit can spice up a chat and add an element of humor to it.

同样，偶尔来一点讽刺的妙语，能让闲谈更有滋味，增添几分幽默。

But a big or steady serving of sarcasm will overwhelm the emotional flavor of any conversation and can taste very bitter to its recipient.

但若大量或持续地使用讽刺，就会淹没任何对话的情感韵味，而且会让听者感到苦涩难当。

P8

So, tone down the sarcasm and work on clever wit instead, which is usually without any hostility and thus more appreciated by those you're communicating with.

所以，少一些讽刺，多在机智风趣上下功夫。机智风趣通常不带任何敌意，因而更受交谈对象的欣赏。

In essence, sarcasm is easy while true, harmless wit takes talent.

说到底，讽刺容易做到，而真正的、无伤大雅的机智却需要才华。

P9

Thus, the main difference between wit and sarcasm is that, as already stated, sarcasm is often hostility disguised as humor.

因此，机智与讽刺的主要区别在于——正如前面所说——讽刺常常是伪装成幽默的敌意。

It can be intended to hurt and is often bitter and biting.

讽刺可能有意伤人，而且往往尖酸刻薄。

Witty statements are usually in response to someone's unhelpful remarks or behaviors, and the intent is to untangle and clarify the issue by emphasizing its absurdities.

机智的言辞通常是针对他人无益的言论或行为而作出的回应，其意图是通过凸显事情的荒谬之处来理清并阐明问题。

Sarcastic statements are expressed in a cutting manner; witty remarks are delivered with undisguised and harmless humor.

讽刺性话语措辞尖刻；机智妙语则以坦率无害的幽默来表达。

Section C · 仔细阅读 Passage 2443 词 · 24 句

P1

Variability is crucially important for learning new skills.

变异性对学习新技能至关重要。

Consider learning how to serve in tennis.

以学习网球发球为例。

Should you always practise serving from the exactly same location on the court, aiming at the same spot?

你是否应该总是在球场上的同一位置练习发球，并瞄准同一个落点？

Although practising in more variable conditions will be slower at first, it will likely make you a better tennis player in the end.

尽管在更多变的条件下练习起初会较慢，但最终很可能让你成为更好的网球选手。

This is because variability leads to better generalisation of what is learned.

这是因为变异性有助于对所学内容实现更好的泛化。

P2

This principle is found in many domains, including speech perception and learning categories.

这一原理存在于许多领域，包括语音感知和类别学习。

For instance, infants will struggle to learn the category "dog" if they are only exposed to Chihuahuas, instead of many different kinds of dogs.

例如，如果婴儿只接触吉娃娃而不接触多种不同的狗，他们将难以学会"狗"这一类别。

P3

"There are over ten different names for this basic principle," says Limor Raviv, the senior investigator of a recent study.

近期一项研究的首席研究员利莫尔·拉维夫（Limor Raviv）表示："这一基本原理有十多种不同的名称。"

"Learning from less variable input is often fast, but may fail to generalise to new stimuli."

"从变异性较低的输入中学习往往很快，但可能无法泛化到新的刺激上。"

P4

To identify key patterns and understand the underlying principles of variability effects, Raviv and her colleagues reviewed over 150 studies on variability and generalisation across fields, including computer science, linguistics, categorisation, visual perception and formal education.

为了识别关键模式并理解变异性效应背后的原理，拉维夫和同事们回顾了来自计算机科学、语言学、分类、视觉感知和正规教育等领域的150多项关于变异性与泛化的研究。

P5

The researchers discovered that, across studies, the term variability can refer to at least four different kinds of variability, such as set size and scheduling.

研究人员发现，在各项研究中，"变异性"一词至少可以指代四种不同的变异类型，例如集合规模和时间安排。

"These four kinds of variability have never been directly compared—which means that we currently don't know which is most effective for learning," says Raviv.

拉维夫说："这四种变异类型此前从未被直接比较过——这意味着我们目前还不知道哪种对学习最有效。"

P6

The impact of variability depends on whether it is relevant to the task or not.

变异性的影响取决于它是否与任务相关。

But according to the 'Mr. Miyagi principle', practising seemingly unrelated skills may actually benefit learning of other skills.

但根据“宫城先生原则”，练习看似无关的技能，实际上可能有助于其他技能的学习。

P7

But why does variability impact learning and generalisation?

然而，变异性为何会影响学习与泛化呢？

One theory is that more variable input can highlight which aspects of a task are relevant and which are not.

一种理论认为，更具变异性的输入能够凸显任务中哪些方面是相关的、哪些是无关的。

P8

Another theory is that greater variability leads to broader generalisations.

另一种理论认为，变异性越大，泛化的范围就越广。

This is because variability will represent the real world better, including atypical examples.

这是因为变异性能更好地呈现真实世界，其中也包括非典型例子。

P9

A third reason has to do with the way memory works: when training is variable, learners are forced to actively reconstruct their memories.

第三个原因与记忆的运作方式有关：当训练具有变异性时，学习者被迫主动重建记忆。

P10

"Understanding the impact of variability is important for literally every aspect of our daily life. Beyond affecting the way we learn language, motor skills, and categories, it even has an impact on our social lives,"

“理解变异性的影响，对我们日常生活的方方面面都至关重要。它不仅影响我们学习语言、运动技能和类别的方式，甚至对我们的社交生活也有影响。”

explains Raviv.

拉维夫解释道。

"For example, face recognition is affected by whether people grew up in a small community or in a larger community. Exposure to fewer faces during childhood is associated with diminished face memory."

“例如，人脸识别会受到人们是成长于小社区还是大社区的影响。童年时期接触到的面孔越少，面孔记忆能力就越弱。”

P11

"We hope this work will spark people's curiosity and generate more work on the topic," concludes Raviv.

“我们希望这项工作能激发人们的好奇心，并推动更多关于这一课题的研究。”拉维夫总结道。

"Our paper raises a lot of open questions. Can we find similar effects of variability beyond the brain, for instance, in the immune system?"

“我们的论文提出了许多悬而未决的问题。比如，我们能否在大脑之外，例如在免疫系统中，发现类似的变异性效应？”
