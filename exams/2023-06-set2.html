Imagine sitting down to a big dinner: a massive steak, a large portion of fried potatoes, and cake for dessert.

想象一下坐下来享用一顿丰盛的大餐：一大块牛排、一大份炸土豆，还有作为甜点的蛋糕。

After eating so much, you should be too full to eat another bite.

吃了这么多之后，你应该已经撑得一口也吃不下了。

But some people experience a powerful urge to keep eating, even after indulging in a huge meal, a behavior that makes little sense, as most adults are well-versed in the dangers of obesity, which researchers have shown correlates with innumerable health issues and is even linked to increased mortality risk.

然而，有些人即使在大吃一顿之后，仍会产生强烈的继续进食的冲动——这种行为令人费解，因为大多数成年人都深知肥胖的危害，而研究人员已证实，肥胖与无数健康问题相关，甚至会增加死亡风险。

But some people still keep eating long after they should stop, a phenomenon Dr. Susan Thompson calls "insatiable hunger."

但有些人仍然在本该停止进食之后继续大快朵颐，苏珊·汤普森博士将这种现象称为"永不满足的饥饿"。

It is characterized by two main attributes: not being satisfied by eating, and having a desire to stay sedentary.

它具有两大特征：一是进食无法带来满足感，二是倾向于久坐不动。

This is at odds with how humans are biologically programmed.

这与人类的生理本能背道而驰。

When there was a great deal of food available, ancient people would gorge on calories; this massive calorie intake was accompanied by an urge to get active.

当食物充足时，古人会大量摄入热量；而这种热量的大量摄入往往伴随着想要活动身体的冲动。

Humans were also programmed for something called "compensation," which is the brain's regulatory mechanism for preventing the accumulation of excess weight.

人类的本能中还包含一种名为"补偿"的机制，即大脑为防止体重过度增加而进行的调节机制。

With compensation, if you eat one large meal in the morning, you are naturally inclined to eat less for the rest of the day.

在补偿机制的作用下，如果你早上吃了一顿大餐，一天中剩下的时间自然会倾向于少吃。

But recent studies show that 70% of American adults have lost the ability to naturally compensate for the calories they consume; worse still, a significant number of them report diminishing hunger halfway through an eating session, but, by the end of the meal, they feel the same or higher levels of hunger than when they sat down.

但近期研究表明，70%的美国成年人已经丧失了对摄入热量进行自然补偿的能力；更糟糕的是，其中相当一部分人反映，在进食过程中饥饿感会逐渐减弱，但到了用餐结束时，他们的饥饿感却与刚坐下时持平甚至更强烈。

Dr. Thompson argues that the main cause of this phenomenon is the modern diet, which is comprised of food high in sugar, carbohydrates and calories.

汤普森博士认为，造成这一现象的主要原因是现代饮食——它由高糖、高碳水化合物和高热量的食物构成。

A

When psychologist Jessica Pryor lived near an internationally renowned university, she once saw a student walking into a library holding a sleeping bag and a coffee maker.

心理学家杰西卡·普赖尔曾住在一所国际知名大学附近，有一次她看到一名学生背着睡袋、端着咖啡机走进图书馆。

She has heard of graduate students spending 12 to 18 hours at a time in the lab.

她听说过有的研究生一次在实验室里待上12到18个小时。

Their schedules are meant to be literally punishing: If they are scientists-in-training, they won't allow themselves to watch Netflix until their experiments start generating results.

他们的日程安排简直就是自我折磨：如果他们是正在接受训练的科学家，他们就不会允许自己在实验开始出结果之前看Netflix。

"Relationships become estranged—people stop inviting them to social gatherings or dinner parties, which leads them to spend even more time in the lab," Pryor told me.

"人际关系变得疏远——人们不再邀请他们参加社交聚会或晚宴，这反过来又让他们在实验室里花更多时间，"普赖尔告诉我。

B

Along with other therapists, Pryor, who is now with the Family Institute at Northwestern University, is trying to sound the alarm about a tendency among young adults and college students to strive for perfection in their work—sometimes at any cost.

与普赖尔现在任职于西北大学家庭研究所，她与其他心理治疗师一起，正在就年轻人和大学生中一种追求工作完美（有时不惜一切代价）的倾向发出警报。

Though it is often portrayed as a positive trait, Pryor and others say extreme perfectionism can lead to depression, anxiety, and even suicide.

虽然追求完美常被描绘成一种积极的品质，但普赖尔等人指出，极端的完美主义可能导致抑郁、焦虑甚至自杀。

C

What's more, perfectionism seems to be on the rise.

更重要的是，完美主义似乎正在加剧。

In a study of thousands of American, Canadian, and British college students published earlier this year, Thomas Curran of the University of Bath and Andrew Hill of York St. John University found that today's college students report higher levels of perfectionism than college students did during the 1990s or early 2000s.

巴斯大学的托马斯·柯伦和约克圣约翰大学的安德鲁·希尔在今年早些时候发表的一项针对数千名美国、加拿大和英国大学生的研究中发现，当今大学生报告的完美主义程度高于20世纪90年代或21世纪初的大学生。

They measured three types of perfectionism: self-oriented, or a desire to be perfect; socially prescribed, or a desire to live up to others' expectations; and other-oriented, or holding others to unrealistic standards.

他们测量了三种类型的完美主义：自我导向型，即渴望做到完美；社会规定型，即渴望达到他人的期望；以及他人导向型，即以不切实际的标准要求他人。

From 1989 to 2016, they found, self-oriented perfectionism scores increased by 10 percent, socially prescribed scores rose by 33 percent, and other-oriented perfectionism increased by 16 percent.

他们发现，从1989年到2016年，自我导向型完美主义得分上升了10%，社会规定型得分上升了33%，他人导向型完美主义上升了16%。

D

A person living with an other-oriented perfectionist might feel criticized by the perfectionist spouse for not doing household chores exactly the "right" way.

与一个他人导向型完美主义者共同生活的人，可能会因为家务没有按照完全"正确"的方式做而受到完美主义配偶的批评。

"One of the most common things couples argue about is the proper way of loading the dishwasher," says Amy Bach, a psychologist in Providence, Rhode Island.

"夫妻之间最常见的争吵之一，就是往洗碗机里放碗碟的正确方式，"罗德岛州普罗维登斯的心理学家艾米·巴赫说。

E

Curran describes socially prescribed perfectionism as "My self-esteem is contingent on what other people think."

柯伦将社会规定型完美主义描述为：“我的自尊取决于别人怎么看我。”

His study didn't examine the causal reasons for its rise, but he assumes that the rise of both standardized testing and social media might play a role.

他的研究并未探究这一现象加剧的因果原因，但他认为标准化考试和社交媒体的兴起可能起到了一定作用。

These days, LinkedIn alerts us when our rival gets a new job, and Instagram can let us know how well "liked" our lives are compared with a friend's.

如今，当竞争对手获得新工作时，领英会提醒我们，而Instagram则能让我们知道，与朋友相比，我们的生活获得了多少“点赞”。

In an opinion piece earlier this year, Curran and Hill argue that society has also become more dog-eat-dog.

在今年早些时候的一篇评论文章中，柯伦和希尔指出，社会也变得更加弱肉强食。

"Over the last 50 years, public interest and civic responsibility have been progressively eroded," they write, "replaced by a focus on self-interest and competition in a supposedly free and open marketplace."

“过去50年间，公共利益和公民责任不断遭到侵蚀，”他们写道，“取而代之的是，在一个所谓的自由开放的市场中，对自身利益和竞争的追逐。”

We strive for perfection, it seems, because we feel we must in order to get ahead.

我们之所以追求完美，似乎是因为觉得唯有如此才能出人头地。

Michael Brustein, a clinical psychologist in Manhattan, says when he first began practicing in 2007, he was surprised by how prevalent perfectionism was among his clients, despite how little his graduate training had focused on the phenomenon.

曼哈顿的临床心理学家迈克尔·布鲁斯坦说，2007年他刚开业时，曾惊讶于完美主义在他的来访者中如此普遍，而他的研究生课程却很少涉及这一现象。

He sees perfectionism in, among others, clients who are entrepreneurs, artists, and tech employees.

他发现，来访者中有创业者、艺术家和科技公司员工等，都存在完美主义倾向。

"You're in New York because you're ambitious, you have this need to strive," he says.

“你来纽约是因为你有雄心，你有这种拼搏的需要，”他说。

"But then your whole identity gets wrapped into a goal."

“但之后，你的整个身份认同都被捆绑在一个目标上了。”

F

Perfectionism can, of course, be a positive force.

当然，完美主义也可以成为一种积极的力量。

Think of professional athletes, who train aggressively for ever-higher levels of competition.

想想职业运动员吧，他们为追求更高水平的竞技而积极训练。

In well-adjusted perfectionism, someone who doesn't get the gold is able to forget the setback and move on.

在适应性完美主义中，未能摘金的人能够忘却挫折，继续前行。

In maladaptive perfectionism, meanwhile, people make an archive of all their failures.

而在适应不良的完美主义中，人们会把所有的失败都记录归档。

They revisit these archives constantly, thinking, as Pryor puts it, "I need to make myself feel terrible so I don't do this again."

他们不断重温这些失败记录，正如普赖尔所说，心想：“我必须让自己感到难受，这样才不会再犯同样的错误。”

Then they double down, "raising the expectation bar even higher, which increases the likelihood of defeat, which makes you self-critical, so you raise the bar higher, work even harder," she says.

接着他们会变本加厉，“把期望值抬得更高，这会增加失败的可能性，失败又让你自我苛责，于是你把标准提得更高，工作得更加拼命，”她说道。

Next comes failure, shame, and pushing yourself even harder toward even higher and more impossible goals.

接下来就是失败、羞愧，以及逼迫自己朝着更高、更不可能实现的目标更加拼命地努力。

Meeting them becomes an "all or nothing" premise.

实现这些目标变成了一种“全有或全无”的前提。

Pryor offered this example: "Even if I'm an incredible attorney, if I don't make partner in the same pacing as one of my colleagues, clearly that means I'm a failure."

普赖尔举了这样一个例子：“即使我是一名出色的律师，如果我不能像某位同事那样按同样的节奏晋升为合伙人，那显然就意味着我是个失败者。”

G

Brustein says his perfectionist clients tend to devalue their accomplishments, so that every time a goal is achieved, the high lasts only a short time, like "a gas tank with a hole in it."

布鲁斯坦说，他的完美主义来访者往往贬低自己的成就，因此每当实现一个目标，那种兴奋感只能维持很短的时间，就像“一个有漏洞的油箱”。

If the boss says you did a great job, it's because he doesn't know anything.

如果老板说你干得不错，那是因为他什么都不懂。

If the audience likes your work, that's because it's too stupid to know what good art actually is.

如果观众喜欢你的作品，那是因为他们太愚蠢，根本不懂什么是真正的优秀艺术。

But, therapists say, there are also different ways perfectionism manifests.

但是，治疗师们表示，完美主义的表现方式也各不相同。

Some perfectionists are always pushing themselves forward.

有些完美主义者总是不断鞭策自己前进。

But others actually fall behind on work, unable to complete assignments unless they are, well, perfect.

但另一些人确实在工作上落后，除非任务做到“完美”，否则根本无法完成。

Or they might handicap their performance ahead of time.

或者，他们可能会提前给自己的表现设下障碍。

They're the ones partying until 2 a.

这类人会在期末考试前夜派对到凌晨两点，

m. the night before the final, so that when the grade C rolls in, there's a ready excuse.

这样一来，当成绩得C时，便有了现成的借口。

H

While educators and parents have successfully convinced students of the need to be high performing and diligent, the experts told me, they haven't adequately prepared them for the inevitability of failure.

教育专家告诉我，尽管教育工作者和家长已经成功地让学生们认识到高分表现和勤奋刻苦的必要性，但他们并没有充分地让学生为失败的不可避免做好准备。

Instead of praises like "You're so smart," parents and educators should say things like "You really stuck with it," Pryor says, to emphasize the value of perseverance over intrinsic talent.

Pryor说，家长和教育者不应说“你真聪明”之类夸奖的话，而应该说“你真的很坚持”，以此强调坚持努力比天赋更重要。

Pryor notes that many of her clients are wary she'll "turn them into some degenerate couch potato and teach them to be okay with it."

Pryor指出，她的许多来访者担心她会“把他们变成某种堕落的沙发土豆，并教他们安于现状”。

Instead, she tries to help them think through the parts of their perfectionism they'd like to keep, and to lose the parts that are ruining their lives.

相反，她会帮助来访者梳理自己的完美主义，思考哪些部分想保留，哪些破坏了他们生活的部分应该摒弃。

I

Bach, who sees many students from Brown University, says some of them don't even go out on weekends, let alone weekdays.

Bach接待了大量布朗大学的学生，她说其中一些人周末甚至不出门，更不用说工作日了。

She tells them, "Aim high, but get comfortable with good enough."

她告诉他们：“目标要高，但要学会接受‘足够好’。”

When they don't get some award, she encourages them to remember that "one outcome is not a basis for a broad conclusion about the person's intelligence, qualifications, or potential for the future."

当他们没有获得某项奖励时，她会鼓励他们记住：“一次结果并不能作为对其智力、资历或未来潜力作出全面结论的依据。”

J

The treatment for perfectionism might be as simple as having patients keep logs of things they can be proud of, or having them behave imperfectly in small ways, just to see how it feels.

治疗完美主义的方法可能很简单：让患者记录下自己可以引以为豪的事情，或者让他们故意在小事上表现得不完美，看看感觉如何。

"We might have them hang the towels crooked or wear some clothing inside out," says Martin Antony, a professor in the department of psychology at Ryerson University in Toronto.

"我们可能会让他们把毛巾挂歪，或者把衣服反着穿，"多伦多瑞尔森大学心理学系教授马丁·安东尼说。

K

Brustein likes to get his perfectionist clients to create values that are important to them, then try to shift their focus to living according to those values rather than achieving specific goals.

布鲁斯坦喜欢让他的完美主义来访者确立对自己而言重要的价值观，然后努力将注意力转向按照这些价值观去生活，而不是去达成特定的目标。

It's a play on the "You really stuck with it" message for kids.

这句话是对写给孩子的"你真的坚持下来了"这句鼓励语的巧妙化用。

In other words, it isn't about doing a headstand in yoga class; it's about going to yoga class in the first place, because you like to be the kind of person who takes care of herself.

换句话说，重点不在于在瑜伽课上完成倒立，而在于首先去上瑜伽课，因为你喜欢成为那种懂得照顾自己的人。

But he warns that some people go into therapy expecting too much—an instant transformation of themselves from a pathological perfectionist to a (still high-achieving) non-perfectionist.

但他警告说，有些人接受治疗时期望过高——期望自己立刻从一个病态的完美主义者脱胎换骨，变成一个（仍能取得高成就的）非完美主义者。

They try to be perfect, in other words, at no longer being perfect.

换句话说，他们试图在"不再追求完美"这件事上做到完美。

P1

How on earth did we come to this?

我们究竟是怎么走到这一步的？

We protect our children obsessively from every harm; we scrutinize every carer, teacher or doctor with whom they come into contact.

我们不遗余力地保护孩子免受各种伤害；对每一位与他们接触的看护人、教师或医生，我们都仔细审查。

Yet despite all this, one group, which in no way has their best interests at heart, has almost unconstrained access.

然而，尽管如此，有一个群体几乎可以毫无限制地接近我们的孩子，而他们丝毫没有把孩子的最大利益放在心上。

P2

We seem to take it for granted that advertisers and marketers are allowed to condition even the youngest children.

我们似乎理所当然地认为，广告商和营销人员可以对最小的孩子进行诱导和塑造。

Before children have even developed a proper sense of their own identity, or learned to handle money, they're encouraged to associate status and self-worth with stuff, and to look to external things such as fame and wealth for validation.

在孩子们尚未形成正确的自我认同感、也还没学会理财之前，他们就被鼓励将身份地位和自我价值与物质挂钩，并从名利等外在事物中寻求认同。

We're turning out little consumers rather than young citizens who will value themselves for what they contribute to the society in which they live.

我们培养出来的是一个个小消费者，而不是会因自己对所处社会的贡献而珍视自身价值的年轻公民。

P3

We've reached this point so gradually that many of us have never questioned it.

我们是如此渐进地走到了这一步，以至于许多人从未对此提出过质疑。

It's crept up on us in the 60 years since advertisers started to target the young and found that they could recruit them to a commercial assault on their parents.

自从广告商开始以年轻人为目标，并发现可以利用他们向父母发起商业攻势以来，这种现象在60年间悄然蔓延到我们身上。

We've come to know it as "pester power" or the ability of children to pressure parents to make certain purchases.

我们将其称为'纠缠力量'，即孩子向父母施压、迫使其购买某些商品的能力。

P4

Many psychologists, child development experts and educators point to research suggesting that this emerging cradle-to-grave consumerism is contributing to growing rates of low self-esteem, depression and other forms of mental illness.

许多心理学家、儿童发展专家和教育工作者援引研究指出，这种从摇篮到坟墓的消费主义正在助长自卑、抑郁以及其他形式的心理疾病，其发病率不断攀升。

P5

Not all psychologists agree.

并非所有心理学家都持相同看法。

There're plenty working hand in glove with a ￡12bn-a-year industry that has turned the manipulation of adult emotions and desires into an art form—often literally.

不少心理学家与一个年产值120亿英镑的行业联手合作，该行业已将操控成年人的情感与欲望变成了一门艺术——往往还是字面意义上的艺术。

It's also one that's forever developing new ways to persuade our children to desire material possessions, and because of advertisements' viral effect they only need to infect a few to reach the many.

它还是一种不断推陈出新、想方设法诱使我们的孩子渴求物质占有之物的东西，而由于广告的病毒式传播效应，它们只需感染少数几个孩子，就能触及众多孩子。

Advertising and marketing can serve a useful purpose for children.

广告和营销对儿童也能发挥有益的作用。

Marketing may help socialize children as consumers, inform them about products, and help them carve out unique identities as they reach adulthood.

营销有助于儿童成长为消费者，让他们了解产品，并在他们步入成年之际帮助他们塑造独特的身份认同。

P6

Then, should we ban all advertising aimed at young children?

那么，我们是否应该禁止所有针对幼儿的广告？

I say yes.

我认为应该。

P7

Of course there'll be plenty of objections to an outright ban on advertising to the under-11s.

当然，全面禁止面向11岁以下儿童的广告会招来诸多反对。

There'll be those who argue that would be a breach of freedom of speech and infringe the rights of corporations to brainwash little children into demanding their products.

会有人争辩说，这将侵犯言论自由，并损害企业向小孩子洗脑、让他们要求购买其产品的权利。

P8

Most parents hate what advertising does to their children, but we do have the power to end it and let our children grow up free from many of the pressures of consumerism until they're old enough to make their own decisions.

大多数父母都厌恶广告对自己孩子的影响，但我们确实有能力终结这一切，让孩子在成长过程中免受消费主义的诸多压力，直到他们足以自主做出决定的年纪。

And though advertising is only part of an all-pervasive marketing culture we need to make a start somewhere.

尽管广告只是无孔不入的营销文化的一部分，我们仍需从某处着手。

Let's ban all advertising targeting children of primary school age and younger now.

让我们现在就禁止所有针对小学适龄及更年幼儿童的广告。

Section C · 仔细阅读 Passage 2450 词 · 22 句

P1

Many oppose workplace surveillance, because of the inherent dehumanizing effect it has and the relentless pressure it brings.

许多人反对职场监控，因为它固有的非人化效应，以及它带来的持续压力。

But it's on the rise around the world as firms look to become more efficient by squeezing more productivity from their workers.

但在全球范围内，随着企业试图通过压榨员工以榨取更多生产力来提高效率，这种做法正日益普遍。

More than half of companies with over $750m in annual revenue used "non-traditional" monitoring techniques on staff last year.

去年，年营收超过7.5亿美元的公司中，有一半以上对员工采用了"非传统"监控技术。

P2

Monitoring employee performance gives firms the ability to assess how their staff are performing and interacting, which can be good for both the firm and employees themselves.

监控员工表现使企业能够评估员工的工作与互动情况，这无论对企业还是员工自身都有益处。

A growing number of analytics companies offer this service.

越来越多的数据分析公司提供这项服务。

They gather "data exhaust" left by employees' email and instant messaging apps, and use name badges equipped with radio-frequency identification devices and microphones.

它们收集员工电子邮件和即时通讯应用留下的"数据尾迹"，并使用配备射频识别装置和麦克风的工牌。

These can check how much time you spend talking, your volume and tone of voice, even if you do not dominate conversations.

这些设备可以统计你的说话时长、音量和语调，即使你并未主导对话。

While this may sound intrusive, exponents argue that it can also protect employees against bullying and sexual harassment.

尽管这听起来可能令人不适，但倡导者认为它也可以保护员工免受欺凌和性骚扰。

P3

Some of this data analysis can produce unexpected results.

其中一些数据分析可能产生意想不到的结果。

For example, it was found that people who sat at 12-person lunch tables tended to interact, share ideas more and outperform those who regularly sat at four-person tables, a fact that would probably have gone undetected without such data analysis.

例如，研究发现，坐在12人餐桌旁的人往往互动更多、分享想法更频繁，表现优于经常坐在4人餐桌旁的人——若没有这样的数据分析，这一事实很可能永远不会被发现。

P4

Over the last few years a Stockholm co-working space called Epicenter has gone much further and holds popular "chipping parties", where people can have microchips implanted in their hands.

过去几年里，斯德哥尔摩一家名为Epicenter的联合办公空间走得更远，定期举办受欢迎的"芯片植入派对"，人们可以在那里将微型芯片植入手中。

They can use the implants to access electronically-controlled doors, or monitor how typing speed correlates with heart rate.

他们可以用植入物开启电子门禁，或者监测打字速度与心率之间的关联。

Implanted chips may seem extreme, but it is a relatively small step from ID cards and biometrics to such devices.

植入式芯片听起来或许有些极端，但从身份证和生物识别技术到这类设备，其实只是一小步。

P5

As long as such schemes are voluntary, there will probably be a growing number of convenience-oriented uses so that a substantial number of workers would opt to have a chip inserted.

只要这类方案是自愿参与的，出于便利考虑的用途就可能越来越多，从而会有相当数量的员工选择植入芯片。

But if implanted chips are used to reduce slack time or rest breaks, that could prove to be detrimental.

但如果植入式芯片被用来压缩怠工时间或休息时间，那就可能产生负面影响。

And if surveillance tools take away autonomy, that's when they prove most unpopular.

而当监控工具剥夺了员工的自主权时，恰恰是它们最不受欢迎的时候。

A lot depends on how such monitoring initiatives are communicated and this could prevent possible revolts being staged.

很大程度上取决于此类监控措施如何与员工沟通，良好的沟通可能防止潜在的反抗发生。

P6

If bosses don't communicate effectively, employees assume the worst.

如果老板沟通不到位，员工往往会往最坏的方向想。

But if they're open about the information they're collecting and what they're doing with it, research suggests 46% of employees are generally okay with it.

但如果他们坦诚说明正在收集哪些信息以及如何使用这些信息，研究显示有46%的员工总体上是可以接受的。

Although many such monitoring schemes use anonymised data and participation is voluntary, many staffers remain sceptical and fear an erosion of their civil liberties.

尽管许多此类监控方案使用的是匿名数据且参与是自愿的，许多员工仍持怀疑态度，担心公民自由受到侵害。

P7

So workplace surveillance could be empowering for staff and useful for companies looking to become more efficient and profitable.

因此，职场监控既可能赋予员工更多主动权，也有助于希望提高效率和盈利的公司。

But implemented in the wrong way, it could also become an unpopular tool of oppression that proves counterproductive.

但如果实施方式不当，它也可能沦为不得人心的压迫工具，最终适得其反。
