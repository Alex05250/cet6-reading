Most financial crises have plenty in common: they tend to start in the banking sector and involve excessive borrowing, together with an asset bubble.

大多数金融危机都有许多共同之处：它们往往始于银行业，涉及过度借贷，并伴随资产泡沫。

The global crisis of 2008 was no different, with the asset bubble focused on US real estate.

2008年的全球危机也不例外，当时的资产泡沫集中在美国房地产市场。

But my research suggests this crisis had another underlying cause—that some people in the banking sector were playing or "gaming" the system for their own financial gain.

但我的研究表明，这场危机还有另一个深层原因——银行业内部有些人为了个人经济利益而在钻制度的空子，或者说“玩弄”体制。

The game being played had several important features.

这场“游戏”有几个重要特征。

The first was the deliberate complexity of the financial products at its core.

第一个特征是其核心金融产品的刻意复杂化。

Then there are the behavioural biases that permeate decision-making at all levels of the banking industry.

其次是渗透于银行业各层级决策过程的行为偏差。

Banking often attracts people with certain personality traits, including those who are overconfident and take excessive risks.

银行业往往吸引具有某些性格特质的人，包括过度自信、甘冒过大风险的人。

Such people tend to like complexity for its own sake, but they often do not fully understand the implications of that complexity for the stability of the financial system as a whole.

这类人往往单纯因为复杂而喜欢复杂，但他们常常不能充分理解这种复杂性对整个金融体系稳定的影响。

Often they do not care—they are primarily interested in gaming the system to maximise their bonuses.

而且他们往往并不在意——他们主要关心的是钻制度的空子，把奖金最大化。

The next element is risk.

下一个要素是风险。

There are parts of the banking sector that will always be prone to risk, but many bankers have come to feel immune to its potential impact, because they presume that, however recklessly banks behave, governments will always be there to bail them out.

银行业中总有一些领域容易与风险相伴，但许多银行家已觉得自己可以免受风险潜在冲击的影响，因为他们认为，无论银行行事多么鲁莽，政府总会出手救助。

Gaming in the banking sector seems virtually impossible to eliminate.

银行业中的这种钻空子行为似乎几乎不可能根除。

The only effective measure to end it would be to make bankers personally liable for losses, but no government has ever passed such a law, and no single government could do so on its own, since this would immediately cause their entire national banking sector to move wholesale to another jurisdiction.

唯一有效的终结办法是让银行家对损失承担个人责任，但没有任何一个政府曾通过这样的法律，也没有哪一个政府能够单方面这样做，因为这会立即使其整个国内银行业整体迁往其他司法管辖区。

Section B · 长篇阅读（信息匹配）1201 词 · 52 句

A

Five hundred years ago, forests covered much of the Iberian peninsula.

五百年前，森林覆盖了伊比利亚半岛的大部分地区。

But that soon changed.

但这种情况很快发生了改变。

Centuries of wars and invasions, agricultural expansion and woodcutting for firewood and shipping wiped out most of the woods and transformed places like Matamorisca, a small village in northern Spain, into degraded landscapes.

数百年的战争与入侵、农业扩张，以及为获取燃料和造船用材而进行的砍伐，摧毁了大部分林地，并将马塔莫里斯卡这样的地方——西班牙北部的一个小村庄——变成了退化的荒地。

B

The region's dry climate and depleted soils would be a recipe for disaster in your average program to replant trees, but for the Amsterdam-based Land Life Company it's an ideal place.

该地区干燥的气候和贫瘠的土壤，对常规的植树项目而言无异于灾难的根源，但对总部设在阿姆斯特丹的生命树公司（Land Life Company）来说，这里却是理想的场所。

"We typically operate where nature does not come back by itself," says Jurrian Ruys, its CEO.

其首席执行官尤里安·鲁伊斯（Jurrian Ruys）表示："我们通常在自然无法自行恢复的地方开展工作。"

"We go where there are rougher conditions in terms of weather, with rough or very hot summers."

"我们去往天气条件更为恶劣的地区，那里夏季酷热难耐或极为粗糙。"

In Matamorisca, they intervened in 17 barren hectares owned by the regional government and peppered them with their signature device: a biodegradable cardboard container they call the cocoon which can hold 25 litres of water underground to aid a seedling's first year.

在马塔莫里斯卡，他们在地方政府所有的17公顷荒地上进行了干预，并在其间布设了公司的标志性设备：一种可降解的硬纸板容器，他们称之为"茧"（cocoon），能够在地下储存25升水，帮助树苗度过第一年。

Around 16,000 trees, including oaks, ashes, walnuts, rowans, and whitebeams, were planted in May 2018, and the company reports that 96% of them survived that year's burning hot summer without extra irrigation, a critical milestone for a young tree.

2018年5月，约16000棵树被种下，包括橡树、白蜡树、核桃树、花楸树和欧洲白蜡树。公司报告称，其中96%在当年酷热的夏季无需额外灌溉便存活了下来，这对幼树而言是一个关键的里程碑。

C

"Does nature return by itself?"

"大自然会自行恢复吗？"

asks Arnout Asjes, Land Life Company's chief technology officer, who oversees a mix of drone and satellite imagery, big data analysis, soil enhancement, QR tagging and site-specific tree configuration designs.

生命树公司的首席技术官阿尔诺特·阿杰斯（Arnout Asjes）问道。他负责统筹无人机与卫星影像、大数据分析、土壤改良、二维码标签以及因地制宜的树木配置设计等多项工作。

"Probably, but it can take decades or hundreds of years, so we are speeding things up."

"可能会，但需要几十年甚至几百年的时间，所以我们正在加快这一进程。"

D

His company belongs to a global movement of organisations trying to save degraded or deforested areas, ranging from fertile tropical lowlands to dry hills in temperate regions.

他所在的公司属于一个全球性的组织网络，致力于拯救退化或森林砍伐的地区，范围从肥沃的热带低地一直到温带地区的干旱丘陵。

Spurred by global biodiversity loss and climate change, these groups are pushing the boundary of how to revive forest cover.

在全球生物多样性丧失和气候变化的推动下，这些团体正在不断突破恢复森林覆盖的边界。

"It's not a theoretical proposition," says Walter Vergara, a forest and climate specialist at the World Resources Institute(WRI).

世界资源研究所（WRI）森林与气候专家沃尔特·韦尔加拉（Walter Vergara）说：“这并非纸上谈兵。”

"It requires the right incentives, the right stakeholders, the right analysis and sufficient capital, but it can happen."

“它需要恰当的激励机制、合适的利益相关方、科学的分析以及充足的资本，但这是可以实现的。”

E

How these factors come together for a particular project—and whether saving destroyed woods is even possible—depends on what kind of ecosystem you have in mind.

这些因素能否在某个具体项目中协同发挥作用——以及拯救已被毁坏的森林是否真的可行——取决于你所设想的是哪种生态系统。

Secondary forests in the Amazon are different from Texas pines recovering after wildfires or the Arctic timber woods that cover much of Sweden.

亚马逊的次生林不同于得克萨斯州野火后正在恢复的松林，也不同于覆盖瑞典大片国土的北极林木。

Each provides different reasons for reforestation programs and each has particular needs.

不同的森林为造林项目提供了不同的理由，也各有其特殊需求。

In the dry conditions around Matamorisca and similar areas in Spain, Land Life Company worries about rapid desertification.

在马塔莫里卡及西班牙类似地区的干旱环境中，生命土地公司（Land Life Company）担心的是快速荒漠化问题。

As its focus is on restoring an ecosystem, it works with organisations that don't expect their money back.

由于公司的重点在于恢复生态系统，它与那些不指望收回资金的组织合作。

F

With around 600 hectares replanted around the world since 2015 and another 1,100 planned for this year, the company's drive fits into the Bonn Challenge, a global effort to restore 150 million hectares of the world's deforested and degraded land by 2020.

自2015年以来，该公司已在全球重新种植了约600公顷，今年还计划再种植1100公顷；该公司的努力与“波恩挑战”相契合——这是一项全球性行动，旨在到2020年恢复全球1.5亿公顷的毁林和退化土地。

That's an area roughly the size of Iran or Mongolia.

这一面积大致相当于伊朗或蒙古的国土面积。

By 2030, the goal is to reach 350 million hectares—20% more land than India.

到2030年，目标是达到3.5亿公顷——比印度的国土面积还多20%。

These targets include both the rehabilitation of forested areas that have lost density or look a bit weak (a process known as 'restoration' in forestry jargon) and the recovery of forest cover in areas where it was completely cleared (what's known as 'reforestation').

这些目标既包括对林木密度下降或长势疲弱的林区进行修复（林业术语称之为“生态恢复”），也包括在森林被彻底砍伐的地区重新造林（即所谓“造林”）。

G

That global target is broken into smaller pieces and takes shape in Latin America as the 20x20 Initiative, an effort to contribute 20 million hectares to the overall goal by initiating small to medium projects with the political backing of governments.

这一全球目标被分解为若干子目标，在拉丁美洲落地为“20x20倡议”——该项目旨在以中小规模项目为依托，在各国政府的政治支持下，为整体目标贡献2000万公顷林地。

Unlike Land Life Company, this region-wide project makes the economic and business case for reforestation, even though they are after biodiversity conservation.

与陆地生命公司不同，这个覆盖整个区域的项目从经济和商业角度论证了造林的可行性，尽管其最终目的同样是保护生物多样性。

"You need to bring in private sector money," says WRI's Vergara, who leads the initiative, "and that capital needs to see a return on their investment."

世界资源研究所的维尔加拉是该倡议的负责人，他说：“你需要引入私营部门的资金，而这些资本需要看到投资回报。”

A study he led predicts Latin America would see an estimated net present value of about $23bn over a 50-year period if it reaches its goal.

由他主导的一项研究预测，如果拉丁美洲实现其目标，未来50年内该地区将获得约230亿美元的净现值。

H

Money can come from timber sales in sustainably managed forests or from harvesting 'non-wood products' like nuts, oils and fruits from the trees.

资金可以来自可持续经营林木的木材销售，也可以来自从树木中采集坚果、油脂和果实等“非木质产品”。

You can take note of how much carbon dioxide your forest is capturing and sell carbon credits to companies keen to offset their emissions.

你可以记录森林吸收了多少二氧化碳，然后将碳信用额出售给希望抵消自身排放的企业。

Or you can even grow the forest hoping that biodiversity will attract ecotourists who'd pay for lodging, birdwatching tours and meals.

或者，你甚至可以培育森林，寄望于生物多样性能够吸引生态旅游者，从而带来住宿、观鸟之旅和餐饮方面的收入。

I

Still, these backers are not your high-street bank.

不过，这些投资方并非你平时光顾的银行。

Money for the 20x20 Initiative comes mostly from financial organisations with triple goal—a modest return on their investment, environmental benefits and social gains—known as impact investors.

“20x20倡议”的资金主要来自具有三重目标的金融机构——在投资上获得适度回报、产生环境效益并取得社会效益——这类机构被称为影响力投资者。

Take the German fund 12Tree for instance, one of the 20x20 partners.

以德国基金12Tree为例，它是“20x20倡议”的合作方之一。

It put $9.5 million into Cuango, a 1,455-hectare property on the Caribbean coast of Panama that combines a commercial cocoa tree plantation with timber extraction from a sustainably managed secondary forest.

该基金向位于巴拿马加勒比海沿岸、占地1455公顷的库安戈项目投入了950万美元，该项目将商业可可种植园与可持续经营的次生林木材采伐结合起来。

With their money, they reforested a former cattle ranch, provided high-quality jobs for the surrounding communities and made a return on their investment.

借助这笔资金，他们重新造林了原为牧场的土地，为周边社区提供了高质量的就业机会，同时获得了投资回报。

J

Even on land cleared decades ago and currently used by farmers, some crops can coexist with the forest, if you find the right balance.

即便是在几十年前就已开垦、目前由农民耕种的土地上，只要找到恰当的平衡，一些作物也能与森林共存。

Although not technically reforestation, agroforestry (a system of land use in which harvestable trees or shrubs are grown among or around crops or on pasture land, as a means of preserving or enhancing the productivity of the land) provides an opportunity for small-holder farmers to sustain their livelihoods while adding some forest cover to their farms.

农林复合经营虽然严格来说不算重新造林，但它（一种将可收获的树木或灌木种植在作物或牧场周围或之间的土地利用方式，旨在保护或提升土地生产力）为小农户提供了一个机会，让他们在为农场增添一些森林覆盖的同时维持生计。

K

A global project called Breedcafs is studying how trees behave in coffee farms, hoping to find crop varieties that manage to grow under the shade of trees.

一个名为Breedcafs的全球项目正在研究树木在咖啡园中的生长状况，希望找到能在树荫下成功生长的作物品种。

Coffee grows naturally in such forests, so copying that in farms is taking the crop back to its roots.

咖啡原本就生长在这类森林中，因此在农场中复制这种模式，就是让作物回归其本源。

"By reintroducing trees in the landscape, we impact positively humidity, rain capture, soil conservation and biodiversity preservation," says coffee expert Benoit Bertrand, who leads the project from the French Agricultural Research Centre for International Development.

法国农业发展国际研究中心（CIRAD）的咖啡专家贝努瓦·贝特朗（Benoit Bertrand）领导该项目，他说："通过在景观中重新引入树木，我们对湿度、雨水截留、土壤保护和生物多样性保护产生了积极影响。"

Bertrand is analysing which of dozens of coffee varieties fits better for this system.

贝特朗正在分析数十种咖啡品种中哪一种最适合这种种植体系。

A similar approach can be applied to land with cocoa, vanilla and fruit trees.

类似的方法也可用于种植可可、香草和果树的土地。

L

Not every piece of land is fit for reforestation.

并非所有土地都适合重新造林。

Vergara's partners look for safe investments, and even Land Life Company only runs major projects in what they consider "low-risk" countries.

韦尔加拉（Vergara）的合作伙伴注重安全投资，即便是生命土地公司（Land Life Company）也只在他们认为"低风险"的国家开展大型项目。

"We tend to avoid large-scale operations in countries in some parts of the Middle East or Africa where the permanence is not secured," says Ruys.

Ruys说："我们倾向于避免在中东或非洲部分地区的国家开展大规模项目，因为那里的长期稳定性无法得到保障。"

M

But in the right location, perhaps all you need is time.

但在合适的地方，或许你所需要的只是时间。

In the Central Pacific of Costa Rica, the 330-hectare National Wildlife Refuge Baru looks nothing like the cattle ranch it was before 1987, when Jack Ewing decided to turn this large estate into an ecotourism destination.

在哥斯达黎加中部太平洋沿岸，占地330公顷的巴鲁国家野生动物保护区看起来与1987年之前的养牛场截然不同——那一年，杰克·尤因（Jack Ewing）决定将这片大型庄园改造成生态旅游目的地。

Instead of intervening, a friend told him to let nature have its way.

一位朋友没有选择插手，而是劝他顺其自然。

Baru's former grazing lands are now thriving woods and the property boasts over 150 hectares of secondary forests with no intervention from humans.

巴鲁昔日的牧场如今已变成茂密的林地，这片庄园拥有150多公顷的次生林，完全依靠自然生长，未加人工干预。

In the past 10 years, animals such as howling monkeys, scarlet macaws and even migratory cougars have returned to the refuge's land, boosting tourism and revitalising the ecosystem.

过去十年间，吼猴、红金刚鹦鹉甚至迁徙的美洲狮等动物纷纷回到这片保护区，带动了旅游业的发展，也让生态系统焕发了生机。

Ewing, now 75, explains this success using the words his friend chose three decades ago: "In Costa Rica, when you stop managing the shrubs, the jungle comes back for its revenge."

现年75岁的尤因用三十年前那位朋友的话来解释这一成功：'在哥斯达黎加，当你不再打理灌木丛时，丛林就会卷土重来，展开报复。'

Section C · 仔细阅读 Passage 1448 词 · 22 句

P1

What does self-discipline look like at work?

自律在工作中是什么样子的？

Sometimes, it's the ability to resist temptation, such as distractions to avoid a particular task.

有时候，自律是一种抵制诱惑的能力，比如抵御让人逃避某项任务的干扰。

Other times it's the ability to persist with a task when your energy and motivation seem low.

另一些时候，自律是在精力和动力似乎都很低落时仍能坚持完成任务的能力。

P2

What do you believe about the nature of your own self-discipline?

你对自己的自律本质上持何种看法？

Is it something that runs on internal energy that is spent with use?

它是一种消耗内在能量、越用越少的东西吗？

Or is it something that is bolstered as you apply it and persist?

还是一种越运用、越坚持就越能被强化的东西？

P3

Your answer matters, as it determines how much self-discipline you experience.

你的答案很重要，因为它决定了你能体验到多少自律。

For years, research in psychology pointed to self-control as a resource that is depleted with use and needs to be restored through breaks, rest, or sleep.

多年来，心理学研究一直认为自我控制是一种会被消耗的资源，需要通过休息、小憩或睡眠来恢复。

More recently, however, research points to the importance of mindset.

然而，近年来的研究则强调心态的重要性。

If you view self-discipline as an expendable resource, your willpower tends to fade with use.

如果你把自律视为一种可消耗的资源，你的意志力往往就会越用越弱。

If you believe that exercising your self-control strengthens and fuels it, that is more likely to be your experience as you resist temptation or push yourself to get something done that is not appealing.

如果你相信运用自我控制会使其更强、为其补充能量，那么当你抵制诱惑或逼迫自己完成一件毫无吸引力的事情时，你更可能获得这样的体验。

P4

If you don't already view self-discipline as a force that feeds itself, what might you do?

如果你还没有把自律看作一种能自我滋养的力量，你可以怎么做呢？

Reflect on times that you had initial difficulty resisting temptation, getting started on something, or sticking with it, but pushed ahead and accomplished the task or met the goal.

回想那些起初难以抵制诱惑、难以开始行动或难以坚持下去，但最终仍然奋力前行并完成任务或实现目标的经历。

That's the kind of experience we're talking about with this particular belief about self-discipline, and you've already had such an experience, so you've shown that it's possible.

我们所说的关于自律的这种特定信念，指的就是这类经历，而且你已经有过这样的经历，所以你已经证明了这是有可能的。

When you feel your willpower and motivation fading, remind yourself of those previous experiences and imagine the same happening now.

当你的意志力和动力逐渐消退时，提醒自己回忆那些以往的经历，并想象此刻同样的事情正在发生。

P5

What if your experiences have always seemed to indicate that your self-control diminishes with use, and you need breaks to recharge?

如果你的过往经历似乎一直都表明，你的自控力会随着使用而不断消耗，你需要休息来恢复精力，那该怎么办？

There's nothing wrong with that as long as you can afford to take those breaks.

只要你承受得起这些休息，这也没什么不好。

If not, remind yourself that not everyone experiences self-discipline as a depletable resource and that your experience might be different if you focus on gathering more energy at this point rather than focusing on how difficult it is to continue or the need for a break.

但如果承受不起，就提醒自己，并非所有人都把自律视为一种会耗尽的资源，而且如果你此刻专注于积蓄更多能量，而不是专注于继续下去有多艰难或者需要休息，你的经历可能会有所不同。

P6

Also, if you know someone who is a role model of self-discipline, picture that person in your situation, persisting in the task.

此外，如果你认识某个自律方面堪称榜样的人，就设想那个人处于你目前的境况中，坚持完成这项任务。

Regardless of how you do it, as you push forward to continue to accomplish progress, be sure to mentally reinforce yourself by taking pride in your ability to exert more willpower as you persist.

无论你采用哪种方式，在你继续推进、不断取得进展的过程中，一定要在心理上肯定自己，为坚持的过程中能够调动更多意志力而感到自豪。

P7

The benefits of exerting willpower are cumulative.

意志力的运用带来的益处是累积的。

Your self-discipline is akin to a muscle, and each time you resist temptation or persist in a task despite a lack of motivation, you strengthen that muscle, leading to less effort and resistance in similar instances in the future.

你的自律就像一块肌肉，每一次你抵制诱惑或在缺乏动力的情况下坚持完成一项任务，你都在强化这块肌肉，从而在未来遇到类似情况时付出更少的努力、遇到更小的阻力。

Section C · 仔细阅读 Passage 2444 词 · 22 句

P1

Smartphones have always posed challenges for parents of teens.

智能手机始终给青少年的父母带来种种挑战。

From social media apps to excessive screen time, the digital world is as threatening as the physical.

从社交媒体应用到屏幕使用时间过长，数字世界和现实世界一样令人担忧。

P2

A new study shows that about half of parents of teens are worried about their teens being exposed to explicit content online.

一项新研究显示，约有一半的青少年父母担心自己的孩子在网上接触到露骨内容。

This is a valid concern, of course.

这种担忧当然是合情合理的。

But there are ways to alleviate the spread of explicit content, from changing the settings in their kids' phone to monitoring such content with apps like Bark.

但缓解露骨内容传播的方法是有的，比如更改孩子手机的设置，或使用Bark等应用监控此类内容。

P3

Just as alarming, if not more so, is that the amount of time teens spend on social media can lead to anxiety and depression.

同样令人担忧、甚至有过之而无不及的是，青少年花在社交媒体上的时间会引发焦虑和抑郁。

P4

Excessive screen time has detrimental effects, for a lot of obvious reasons.

出于许多显而易见的原因，屏幕使用时间过长会带来有害影响。

Constant media consumption inhibits healthy activities associated with childhood, like spending time outside, exercising, talking to friends and family.

持续不断地浏览媒体会妨碍那些与童年息息相关的健康活动，比如到户外活动、锻炼身体、与朋友和家人交流。

If it interferes with sleep, that's even worse.

如果它还干扰睡眠，那就更糟糕了。

P5

Ample research shows too much smartphone use produces significant anxiety, depression, and feelings of isolation in teens.

大量研究显示，过度使用智能手机会引发青少年严重的焦虑、抑郁和孤独感。

Teens log on to social media or spend time online trying to make connections, but end up feeling more alone and sad than ever.

青少年登录社交媒体或上网，本想与人建立联系，结果却比以往任何时候都更加孤独和悲伤。

Faceless communication not only produces feelings of isolation and loneliness but increases instances of online bullying and susceptibility to criminal conducts.

不见面的交流不仅会带来孤立感和孤独感，还会增加网络欺凌的发生率，并使青少年更容易遭受犯罪行为的侵害。

P6

Though I've banned most social media, I've still battled smartphone time with my own kids.

虽然我已经禁止孩子们使用大多数社交媒体，但和自己的孩子在手机使用时间上的博弈依然让我头疼不已。

A lot of the time I wish I could throw my kids' smartphones into the nearest lake.

很多时候，我恨不得把孩子们的手机扔进最近的湖里。

The convenience of communication—when they need a ride home from school after practice, for instance—often feels like it isn't worth the battle to keep their screen time low and productive, and continue to encourage "real-life" activities outside and with friends and family.

沟通的便利——比如孩子们放学后练完体育需要搭车回家时——往往让人觉得，为了让他们少看屏幕、把时间用在有意义的事情上，同时继续鼓励他们参加户外活动以及与朋友和家人的面对面相处而不断抗争，实在得不偿失。

I understand the addiction and often tell them I'm not sure how I would have handled having a smartphone as a teen.

我理解这种成瘾性，也常对他们说，如果我十几岁时就有智能手机，不知道自己会怎样应对。

As an adult, I too, struggle with overuse.

作为成年人，我自己也在和过度使用手机作斗争。

P7

In a recent TV interview to promote a new film about a mother and daughter navigating social media use, actress Kate Winslet expressed her view on teens and social media—and her concern mirrors the trends.

在最近一次为一部关于母女二人共同应对社交媒体使用问题的新片做宣传的电视采访中，女演员凯特·温斯莱特表达了她对青少年与社交媒体的看法——她的担忧与当下的趋势不谋而合。

"I do definitely feel that the world of social media is frightening to parents because we don't really know what's there," she said.

她说：'我确实觉得社交媒体的世界令父母感到恐惧，因为我们根本不知道那里究竟有什么。'

"We don't know how our children are using it. We don't know the effects that it's really having on them."

'我们不知道孩子们是怎么使用它的。我们不知道它究竟对他们产生了什么影响。'

P8

I don't think the U.S. government should regulate social media and teen smartphone use, but it is a vital topic for parents to understand and take seriously.

我认为美国政府不应监管社交媒体和青少年的手机使用，但这是父母必须了解并认真对待的一个至关重要的话题。

Unfortunately, now, parents don't just have to worry about real-world dangers, but all the ones sneaking online and the effects they have on teenagers and their mental health.

遗憾的是，如今父母不仅要提防现实世界中的种种危险，还要警惕网络上潜藏的所有隐患，以及它们对青少年及其心理健康造成的影响。
