Modern U.S. cities were designed to make exercise unnecessary.

现代美国城市在设计之初，就是为了让人们无须运动。

Cars and elevators once symbolized urban areas as machines for more efficient living.

汽车和电梯曾被视为城市作为高效生活机器的象征。

Now it is clear that these improvements provide great benefits but also impose health costs.

如今人们已经清楚地认识到，这些进步虽然带来了巨大的便利，却也付出了健康的代价。

Recent studies show that urban sprawl encourages more driving and is associated with heavier weight.

近期研究表明，城市无序蔓延会促使人们更多地驾车，并与体重偏重相关联。

This correlation suggests that the layout and design of cities can hinder or promote healthier lifestyle choices and it is imperative that urban planners bear this in mind.

这种相关性表明，城市的布局与设计会阻碍或促进更健康的生活方式选择，城市规划者务必将这一点铭记于心。

Unfortunately, urban planning still centers in large part on solving the problems of the past.

遗憾的是，城市规划在很大程度上仍以解决过去的问题为中心。

Of course cities still need to foster standard public health practices, such as separating toxic facilities from homes and restricting heavy truck traffic through dense residential areas.

诚然，城市仍需推动常规的公共卫生措施，例如将有毒设施与住宅区隔离，以及限制重型卡车穿越人口密集的住宅区。

But it's also important to create healthier cities—and the discussion is already underway.

但打造更健康的城市同样重要——而相关讨论已经展开。

More and more city planners are paying increasing attention to encouraging physical activity by making it easier and safer for people to recreate, walk, bike and take public transportation.

越来越多的城市规划者正日益重视通过让人们更便捷、更安全地进行休闲、步行、骑车和乘坐公共交通，来鼓励体育活动。

Many studies of longevity show that people live the longest in environments where physical activity is part of everyday life.

许多关于长寿的研究表明，在体育活动融入日常生活的环境中，人们最为长寿。

Providing more walkable spaces, better protected bike lanes and more recreational spaces are important steps.

提供更多适合步行的空间、防护更好的自行车道以及更多的休闲场所，是重要的举措。

But even smaller changes can be effective.

但即便是更小的改变，也能起到效果。

Cities can close off streets on weekends to encourage communities to get out and walk.

城市可以在周末封闭街道，鼓励社区居民走出家门、步行活动。

They also can provide more seating in public places, so that less-fit residents can rest during their journeys.

它们还可以在公共场所增设座椅，让体能较弱的居民在途中得以休息。

Using public spaces in cities as places where people can exercise promotes equity, rather than allowing physical activity to become restricted to private gyms with often-expensive monthly fees that deter less wealthy people from joining.

把城市公共空间用作人们锻炼身体的场所，有助于促进公平，而不是让体育活动仅限于私人健身房——这些健身房的月费往往昂贵，令经济条件较差的人望而却步。

Section B · 长篇阅读（信息匹配）1226 词 · 59 句

A

You may have heard that learning another language is one method for preventing or at least postponing the onset of dementia.

你可能听说过，学习另一门语言是预防——或至少延缓——痴呆症发病的一种方法。

Dementia refers to the loss of cognitive abilities, and one of its most common forms is Alzheimer's disease.

痴呆症是指认知能力的丧失，其最常见的形式之一是阿尔茨海默病。

At this time, the causes of the disease are not well understood, and consequently, there are no proven steps that people can take to prevent it.

目前，人们对这种疾病的病因尚不十分清楚，因此也没有经证实的、人们可以采取的预防措施。

Nonetheless, some researchers have suggested that learning a foreign language might help delay the onset of dementia.

尽管如此，一些研究人员提出，学习外语可能有助于延缓痴呆症的发病。

B

To explore this possibility more deeply, let's look at some of the common misconceptions about dementia and the aging brain.

为了更深入地探讨这种可能性，让我们来看看人们对痴呆症和衰老的大脑存在的一些常见误解。

First of all, dementia is not an inevitable part of the normal aging process.

首先，痴呆症并非正常衰老过程中不可避免的一部分。

Most older adults do not develop Alzheimer's disease or other forms of dementia.

大多数老年人都不会患上阿尔茨海默病或其他形式的痴呆症。

It is also important to remember that dementia is not the same thing as normal forgetfulness.

同样需要记住的是，痴呆症与正常的健忘并不是一回事。

At any age, we might experience difficulty finding the exact word we want or have trouble remembering the name of the person we just met.

在任何年龄，我们都可能一时想不起确切的措辞，或记不住刚刚认识的人的名字。

People with dementia have more serious problems, like feeling confused or getting lost in a familiar place.

痴呆症患者的问题则更为严重，比如感到意识混乱，或在熟悉的地方迷路。

Think of it this way: If you forget where you parked your car at the mall, that's normal; if you forget how to drive a car, that may be a signal that something more serious is going on.

可以这样理解：如果你在商场忘了车停在哪里，那是正常的；如果你忘了怎么开车，那就可能预示着出现了更严重的问题。

C

The idea that dementia can be prevented is based on the comparison of the brain to a muscle.

认为痴呆症可以预防的想法，是基于把大脑比作肌肉的认识。

When people talk about the brain, they sometimes say things like "It is important to exercise your brain" or "To stay mentally fit, you have to give your brain a workout."

人们谈论大脑时，有时会说“锻炼大脑很重要”，或者“要想保持心理健康，就得让大脑做做运动”之类的话。

Although these are colorful analogies, in reality the brain is not a muscle.

尽管这些比喻很生动，但实际上大脑并不是一块肌肉。

Unlike muscles, the brain is always active and works even during periods of rest and sleep.

与肌肉不同，大脑始终处于活跃状态，即使在休息和睡眠时也在工作。

In addition, although some muscle cells have a lifespan of only a few days, brain cells last a lifetime.

此外，虽然有些肌肉细胞的寿命只有短短几天，但脑细胞却能伴随人的一生。

Not only that, but it has been shown that new brain cells are being created throughout one's lifespan.

不仅如此，已有研究表明，人的一生中新脑细胞都在不断生成。

D

While it makes for a colorful analogy, comparing the brain to a muscle is inaccurate and misleading.

把大脑比作肌肉虽然生动形象，但这种说法既不准确，也容易引起误解。

So, if the brain is not a muscle, can it still be exercised?

那么，既然大脑不是肌肉，它还能被“锻炼”吗？

Once again, researchers don't know for sure.

同样，研究者目前也无法给出确切答案。

There are now many computer, online, and mobile device applications that claim to be able to "train your brain," and they typically tap into a variety of cognitive abilities.

如今有许多电脑、网络和移动设备应用程序声称能够“训练你的大脑”，它们通常会调动多种认知能力。

However, research suggests that although this type of training may improve one's abilities at the tasks themselves, they don't seem to improve other abilities.

然而，研究表明，这种训练或许能提升人在该项任务上的表现，却似乎无法改善其他方面的能力。

In other words, practicing a letter-detection task will, over time, improve your letter-detection skills, but it will not necessarily enhance your other perceptual abilities.

换言之，长期练习字母检测任务会提高你的字母检测技能，但这并不一定会增强你的其他感知能力。

E

However, there is some reason to believe that learning languages might be different.

不过，有理由相信，学习语言可能有所不同。

The best evidence that foreign language learning confers cognitive benefits comes from research with those who are already bilingual.

外语学习能带来认知益处，最有力的证据来自对已经掌握双语者的研究。

Bilingualism most commonly occurs when children are exposed to two languages, either in the home (mom speaks Dutch, dad speaks Spanish) or more formally in early schooling.

双语现象最常见于儿童同时接触两种语言的情况，无论是在家中（母亲说荷兰语，父亲说西班牙语），还是在早期教育中更正式地学习。

But bilingualism certainly occurs in adulthood as well.

但双语现象在成年人中当然也会出现。

F

Bilingualism and multilingualism are actually more common than you might think.

双语和多语现象实际上比你想象的更为普遍。

In fact, it has been estimated that there are fewer monolingual speakers in the world than bilinguals and multilinguals.

事实上，据估计，世界上只会一种语言的人比双语者和多语者还要少。

Although in many countries most inhabitants share just one language, other countries have several official languages.

尽管在许多国家大多数居民只使用一种语言，但另一些国家却有多种官方语言。

Switzerland, for example, has four official languages: German, French, Italian, and Romansh.

例如，瑞士就有四种官方语言：德语、法语、意大利语和罗曼什语。

Throughout large parts of Africa, Arabic, Swahili, French, and English are often known and used by individuals who speak a different, native language in their home than they do in the marketplace.

在非洲的广大地区，阿拉伯语、斯瓦希里语、法语和英语常常被一些人所掌握和使用，而这些人在家里说的母语与在市场上使用的语言并不相同。

So bilingualism and multilingualism are to be found throughout the world.

因此，双语和多语现象遍布世界各地。

And with regard to cognitive abilities, the research on those who speak more than one language paints an encouraging picture.

至于认知能力，针对使用多种语言者的研究呈现出令人鼓舞的局面。

G

For one thing, bilinguals are better at multitasking.

首先，双语者更擅长多任务处理。

One explanation of this superiority is that speakers of two languages are continually inhibiting one of their languages, and this process of inhibition confers general cognitive benefits to other activities.

对这种优势的一种解释是，双语者需要持续抑制其中一种语言，而这种抑制过程能为其他活动带来普遍的认知益处。

In fact, bilingual individuals outperform their monolingual counterparts on a variety of cognitive tasks, such as following complex instructions, and switching to new instructions.

事实上，在各种认知任务中，双语者的表现优于单语者，例如遵循复杂指令、切换到新的指令等。

For the sake of completeness, it should be noted that the advantages of being bilingual are not universal across all cognitive domains.

为全面起见，需要指出的是，双语优势并非普遍适用于所有认知领域。

Bilingual individuals have been shown to have smaller vocabularies and to take longer in retrieving words from memory when compared to monolinguals.

研究表明，与单语者相比，双语者的词汇量更小，从记忆中提取词汇所需的时间也更长。

In the long run, however, the cognitive and linguistic advantages of being bilingual far outweigh these two drawbacks.

然而从长远来看，双语带来的认知和语言优势远远超过这两点不足。

H

If the benefits of being bilingual spill over to other aspects of cognition, then we would expect to see a lower incidence of Alzheimer's disease in bilinguals than in monolinguals, or at least a later onset of Alzheimer's for bilinguals.

如果双语的好处能延伸到认知的其他方面，那么我们预期双语者患阿尔茨海默病的几率会低于单语者，或者至少双语者发病时间会更晚。

In fact, there is evidence to support this claim.

事实上，有证据支持这一说法。

The psychologist Ellen Bialystok and her colleagues obtained the histories of 184 individuals who had made use of a memory clinic in Toronto.

心理学家埃伦·比亚韦斯托克及其同事获取了多伦多一家记忆诊所184名就诊者的病史资料。

For those who showed signs of dementia, the monolinguals in the sample had an average age of 71.4 years at time of onset.

在表现出痴呆症状的人中，样本中的单语者平均发病年龄为71.4岁。

The bilinguals, in contrast, received their diagnosis at 75.5 years, on average.

相比之下，双语者被确诊时的平均年龄为75.5岁。

In a study of this sort, a difference of four years is highly significant, and could not be explained by other systematic differences between the two groups.

在这类研究中，四年的差异具有高度显著性，无法用两组之间的其他系统性差异来解释。

For example, the monolinguals reported, on average, a year and a half more schooling than their bilingual counterparts, so the effect was clearly not due to formal education.

例如，单语者报告的受教育年限平均比双语者多一年半，因此这一效应显然并非由正规教育所致。

I

A separate study, conducted in India, found strikingly similar results: bilingual patients developed symptoms of dementia 4.5 years later than monolinguals, even after other potential factors, such as gender and occupation, were controlled for.

在印度开展的另一项研究得出了惊人相似的结果：即使控制了性别、职业等其他潜在因素，双语患者出现痴呆症状的时间仍比单语者晚4.5年。

In addition, researchers have reported other positive effects of bilingualism for cognitive abilities in later life, even when the person acquired the language in adulthood.

此外，研究人员还报告了双语能力对晚年认知能力的其他积极影响，即使该语言是在成年后习得的也不例外。

Crucially, Bialystok suggested that the positive benefits of being bilingual were only found in those who used both languages all the time.

至关重要的是，比亚韦斯托克指出，双语的益处只体现在那些一直同时使用两种语言的人身上。

J

But encouraging as these kinds of studies are, they still have not established exactly how or why differences between bilinguals and monolinguals exist.

然而，尽管这类研究令人鼓舞，但它们仍未确切查明双语者与单语者之间的差异究竟是如何产生、为何产生的。

Because these studies looked back at the histories of people who were already bilingual, the results can only say that a difference between the two groups was found, but not why that difference occurred.

由于这些研究是回顾已经具备双语能力的人的经历，因此结果只能说明两组人之间存在差异，而无法说明这种差异因何而生。

Further research is needed to determine what caused the differences in age of onset between the two groups.

还需要进一步研究，以确定是什么导致了两组人在发病年龄上的差异。

K

Other studies of successful aging suggest that being connected to one's community and having plenty of social interaction is also important in delaying or even preventing the onset of dementia.

其他有关成功老龄化的研究表明，与社区保持联系、拥有充足的社交互动，对于延缓甚至预防痴呆症的发生同样重要。

Once again, however, the results are far less clear than the popular media might lead you to believe.

然而，这些结果远没有大众媒体让你相信的那样明确。

Older individuals who lead active social lives are, almost by definition, healthier than their counterparts who rarely leave their homes or interact with others.

社交生活活跃的老年人，几乎从定义上说，就比那些很少出门或很少与他人互动的同龄人更健康。

So we can't really say whether being socially active prevents the onset of dementia, or if people who don't have dementia are more likely to be socially active.

因此，我们无法真正断定是社交活跃能预防痴呆症的发生，还是没有患痴呆症的人更有可能保持社交活跃。

L

But even if studying a foreign language is not a magical cure-all, there is one thing it will do: It will make you a better speaker of a foreign language.

但即使学习外语并非什么包治百病的灵丹妙药，有一点是确定无疑的：它会让你的外语说得更好。

Doing that confers a whole host of advantages we do know about.

而这一点能带来一大堆我们确实了解的益处。

Section C · 仔细阅读 Passage 1448 词 · 25 句

P1

Research is meant to benefit society by raising public awareness and creating products and innovations that enhance development.

研究的意义在于服务社会：提升公众认知，并开发出促进发展的产品和创新成果。

For research to serve its full purpose, the results must leave the confines of research laboratories and academic journals.

研究要充分发挥其作用，研究成果就不能局限于研究实验室和学术期刊之内。

P2

Findings effectively communicated can go a long way to serve the interests of the public.

有效传播的研究发现能够在很大程度上维护公众利益。

They can help address social injustices or improve treatments offered to patients.

它们有助于解决社会不公问题，或改善对患者的治疗。

P3

Many researchers seem to be content with sharing the results of their studies in academic journals or at conferences.

许多研究者似乎满足于将研究结果发表在学术期刊上或在学术会议上分享。

But few journals allow everybody to read the findings.

然而，能够允许所有人阅读研究发现的期刊寥寥无几。

Even articles freely available are usually written in academic language incomprehensible to the average reader.

即使是可免费获取的文章，通常也以学术语言撰写，普通读者难以理解。

P4

For researchers in the tenure-track system, their main goal is winning tenure, which in part can be achieved by getting a number of papers published in prominent journals.

对于身处终身教职轨道的研究者来说，他们的主要目标是获得终身教职，而在知名期刊上发表一定数量的论文是实现这一目标的途径之一。

Pressures like this mean community-level outreach is not prioritised.

这样的压力意味着面向社区的科普工作得不到优先重视。

P5

Many researchers lack the writing skills to describe their results to a general audience.

许多研究者缺乏向普通大众描述研究成果的写作能力。

They may also worry about whether the public will understand their findings, or about findings being used to influence controversial policies.

他们也可能担心公众能否理解自己的研究发现，或者担心研究成果被用来影响存在争议的政策。

These concerns cause some researchers to shy away from communicating their findings outside the academic community.

这些顾虑使一些研究者不愿在学术圈之外传播自己的研究发现。

P6

Propagating research findings beyond academic publications is particularly crucial for addressing certain social discrepancies.

将研究成果传播到学术出版物之外，对于消除某些社会差距尤为重要。

It can help families, communities, healthcare providers, policymakers, government agencies and other stakeholders to understand and respond to crises that plague society.

这有助于家庭、社区、医疗服务提供者、政策制定者、政府机构及其他利益相关方理解并应对困扰社会的各种危机。

P7

The benefits of sharing findings flow both ways.

分享研究成果的好处是双向的。

Engaging with other researchers and the public can lead to unexpected new connections and new ideas that could suggest fruitful new directions for research.

与其他研究者和公众交流互动，可能带来意想不到的新联系和新想法，为研究指出富有成效的新方向。

P8

To benefit both researchers and the communities, the need to find innovative, accessible ways to share the work cannot be overstated.

为了让研究者和社区双方都受益，必须想方设法以创新、易懂的方式分享研究工作，这一点再怎么强调都不为过。

P9

Institutions and funding organisations should support more researchers to publish in open access journals so that the public doesn't have to pay to read them.

各研究机构和资助组织应支持更多研究者在开放获取期刊上发表论文，使公众无需付费即可阅读。

Institutions and researchers should invest in partnerships that expand capacity for sharing results more broadly.

各机构和研究者应投资于合作关系，以扩大更广泛地分享研究成果的能力。

P10

Furthermore, ethics committees should make it mandatory for researchers to share their results with the public.

此外，伦理委员会应强制要求研究者向公众公开其研究结果。

Every research participant should opt in or out of receiving results, as part of the process of giving informed consent.

每一位研究参与者都应在知情同意过程中，自主选择是否接收研究结果。

P11

There could be misunderstanding of the findings presented by the researcher because of technical terms.

由于专业术语的存在，研究者所呈现的研究发现可能会引发误解。

But this can be resolved by researchers engaging the services of professional writers or communication officers to help with translating their study into more accessible language and share it widely with media outlets and the public.

但研究者可以聘请专业撰稿人或传播专员，协助将研究内容转化为更通俗易懂的语言，并通过媒体和公众广泛传播，从而解决这一问题。

P12

Sharing results with the people who are most affected by them makes us better researchers and ensures that our work can be used to improve people's lives.

与受研究结果影响最深的人群分享成果，能使我们成为更好的研究者，并确保我们的工作能够切实改善人们的生活。

Institutions and collaborators must recognise the value of doing so.

各机构及其合作者必须认识到这样做的价值。

Section C · 仔细阅读 Passage 2448 词 · 21 句

P1

Spiders make their presence felt in late August and through early autumn.

蜘蛛在八月下旬到初秋这段时间尤为引人注目。

This is the mating season of some of the most common varieties, when male house spiders come out of hidden corners to look for females, and garden spiders reach adult size and spin their most dazzling webs.

这是某些最常见蜘蛛品种的交配季节，雄性家蛛会从隐蔽的角落爬出来寻找雌蛛，园蛛也长到成体大小，编织出最为华丽的蛛网。

P2

Yet while the spider is a familiar fixture of nursery poems or songs and Halloween decorations, its relationship with humans is complicated.

然而，尽管蜘蛛是童谣童歌和万圣节装饰中再熟悉不过的形象，它与人类的关系却并不简单。

Fear of spiders is common and has serious impacts on the lives of sufferers.

对蜘蛛的恐惧十分普遍，且严重影响着患者的生活。

Its prevalence appears unrelated to any rational assessment of risk.

这种恐惧的广泛存在似乎与任何理性的风险评估无关。

Spiders in the UK are almost all harmless.

英国的蜘蛛几乎全都无害。

Farmland species perform valuable ecosystem services, by preying on insects that are our competitors for crops.

农田蜘蛛种类通过捕食与人类争夺作物的昆虫，提供了宝贵的生态服务。

But they are a constant source of human anxieties—with a cultural association with witches and wickedness dating back to the middle ages.

但它们始终是人类焦虑的来源——自中世纪以来，蜘蛛就与女巫和邪恶有着文化上的关联。

P3

Does this perhaps explain, in part, the lack of data about how spiders are faring in our age of ecological crisis?

这是否在一定程度上解释了为何我们缺乏蜘蛛在当今生态危机时代生存状况的数据？

British butterflies are the most studied group of insects in the world, due to the long tradition of collecting and observing them.

由于长期的采集和观察传统，英国蝴蝶是全球被研究最多的昆虫类群。

But spider conservationists point out that it was only in the 1980s that the classification of house spiders was properly sorted out.

但蜘蛛保护人士指出，直到20世纪80年代，家蛛的分类才得到妥善梳理。

And while information about insect populations is gathered by experiments that measure the numbers hitting windscreens or traps, there have been few attempts to count spiders.

而且，虽然关于昆虫种群的信息是通过实验测量撞上挡风玻璃或落入陷阱的数量来收集的，但迄今鲜有人尝试统计蜘蛛的数量。

P4

The huge reductions in the numbers of flying insects can only mean a reduction in spiders' food supply.

飞行昆虫数量的大幅减少，只能意味着蜘蛛食物来源的减少。

A recent landmark study identified a 75% fall in insect populations between 1989 and 2016, with pesticide use thought to be to blame along with the destruction of wild areas for development.

最近一项具有里程碑意义的研究发现，1989年至2016年间昆虫种群数量下降了75%，农药的使用以及为开发而破坏野生区域被认为是罪魁祸首。

This means the overall picture for spiders is worrying, as it is for most creatures.

这意味着蜘蛛的总体状况令人担忧，大多数生物亦然。

But conservationists are most concerned about those varieties that are threatened due to habitat loss and fragmentation, which makes it impossible for them to migrate.

但自然保护人士最为担心的，是那些因栖息地丧失和破碎化而受到威胁的种类——栖息地破碎化使它们无法迁移。

P5

Of around 650 spider species regularly recorded in the UK, the majority thrive in marshes and wasteland.

在英国经常被记录在案的约650种蜘蛛中，大多数在沼泽和荒地上繁衍生息。

Conservation efforts, often led by determined individuals, have helped some species to recover by reintroducing them to new areas.

由意志坚定的个人牵头开展的保护工作，通过将一些物种重新引入新的区域，帮助它们恢复了种群。

With rewilding now firmly on the environmental policy agenda, the hope is that in future, spiders will be enabled to migrate by themselves, adapting to climate change by moving along wildlife corridors.

随着“再野化”如今已稳居环境政策议程，人们希望未来蜘蛛能够自行迁移，沿着野生动物廊道迁徙以适应气候变化。

P6

It seems unlikely that spiders will ever attract the same level of human enthusiasm as bees, birds or butterflies, in spite of their unique status as nature's spinners.

尽管蜘蛛拥有“自然界织网者”的独特地位，但它们似乎不太可能像蜜蜂、鸟类或蝴蝶那样激发人们同等的热情。

But as they reveal themselves in all their splendour this autumn, it would be a good thing if more animal lovers recognised the ways in which spiders are simply terrific.

但当它们今秋尽显其绚丽风采之时，若能有更多的动物爱好者认识到蜘蛛的种种了不起之处，将是一件好事。
