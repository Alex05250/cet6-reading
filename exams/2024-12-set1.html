With the rapid progress in the economy, it is natural that people's ideology is bound for change.

随着经济的飞速发展，人们的思想观念必然会随之改变，这是再自然不过的事。

Being thrifty is no longer a virtue to some people, and they are not alone in holding this view.

在有些人看来，节俭已不再是美德，而持这种看法的人绝非少数。

Each might have different viewpoints on how to lead one's lifestyle: some prefer simplicity, some love luxuries, some spend extravagantly, some accumulate as much as they can like a country mouse.

每个人对于生活方式各有不同的选择：有人崇尚简朴，有人贪恋奢华，有人挥金如土，有人像乡下老鼠一样拼命积攒。

As I was born poor and in the countryside, the simple rural life seldom loses its appeal to me, but as my station changes, I have an opportunity to mingle with the newly rich, an experience which has revolutionized my view of lifestyle.

我出身贫寒，生于农村，质朴的乡村生活对我始终不乏吸引力；但随着身份地位的变化，我有机会与新富阶层交往，这段经历彻底改变了我对生活方式的看法。

It is evil or sinful to spend little and to save much, an idea only recently being revealed to me.

少花钱、多储蓄反倒是邪恶或有罪的——这种观念直到最近才映入我的眼帘。

Actually the whole world is in a rage to borrow and to spend, with the USA leading the trend.

事实上，整个世界都在狂热地借贷和消费，美国更是引领了这一潮流。

The USA is still the richest and most powerful country in the world, but the irony is that they borrow money from us.

美国仍然是世界上最富裕、最强大的国家，但颇具讽刺意味的是，他们却向我们借钱。

Though a layman of economics, at least I know that investing with borrowed money will turn the borrower into the predator rather than the decent and kind lender.

尽管我是经济学的门外汉，但至少我明白：用借来的钱去投资，会把借款人变成掠夺者，而不是体面而善良的出借人。

An apparent case is real estate.

最明显的例子就是房地产。

Those who are shrewd and have got loans from the bank become relatively rich by selling their purchased home at a price several folds higher.

那些精明能干、从银行贷到款的人，以高出数倍的价格转手卖掉所购房产，从而变得相对富有。

Even education is not immune from this theory.

就连教育也未能幸免于这套理论。

Many of those poor parents who have the vision to send their children to college have benefited from this investment, even though they have to scrape a living.

许多有远见、倾力供子女上大学的贫困父母都从这项投资中获益，尽管他们不得不勉强糊口。

It is reasonable to conclude that spending is praiseworthy, supposing it is not beyond your means.

可以合理地得出这样的结论：只要不超出自己的经济能力，花钱消费是值得肯定的。

Section B · 长篇阅读（信息匹配）1194 词 · 63 句

A

On a small fruit farm near the Straits of Malacca, Lim Kok Ann is down to just one tree growing kedondong, a crunchy, sour berry that Malaysians mostly use in pickles and salads.

在马六甲海峡附近的一座小果园里，林国安（Lim Kok Ann）只剩下最后一种果树——结着kedondong（一种口感脆爽、味道酸涩的浆果）的树。马来西亚人大多用它来做腌菜和沙拉。

"It's not very well-known," says the 45-year-old, who is instead focusing on longan berries and pineapples, which have bigger markets.

“它不怎么出名，”这位45岁的果农说。他转而主攻龙眼和菠萝，因为这两种水果的市场更大。

"We have to grow what is profitable," he says.

“我们得种赚钱的东西，”他说。

B

But less than an hour away in the Malaysian countryside, inside three giant, silver domes, scientists are trying to change the future of food.

然而，不到一小时车程之外的马来西亚乡村，科学家们正在三个巨大的银色穹顶之内，试图改变食物的未来。

They're pushing the boundaries of what humans eat by growing and processing so-called "alternative" crops—such as kedondong.

他们通过种植和加工所谓的“替代”作物——比如kedondong——来拓展人类食用的边界。

At the headquarters of global research centre Crops For the Future (CFF) this particular under-used fruit has been turned into a sugar-free juice, high in vitamin C and getting top marks in sensory evaluations.

在全球研究中心“未来作物”（Crops For the Future，简称CFF）的总部，这种长期被忽视的水果已被制成无糖果汁，维生素C含量高，并在感官评价中获得最高分。

"Anything you see here is a forgotten crop," says Sayed Azam-Ali of the abundant plants weaving through the gardens of CFF outside Malaysia's capital Kuala Lumpur.

“你在这里看到的每一种植物，都是被遗忘的作物。”赛义德·阿扎姆-阿里（Sayed Azam-Ali）指着CFF园区内缠绕交错的繁茂植物说。该中心位于马来西亚首都吉隆坡郊外。

C

Prof Azam-Ali explains that just four crops—wheat, maize, rice and soybean—provide two-thirds of the world's food supply.

阿扎姆-阿里教授解释说，仅小麦、玉米、水稻和大豆这四种作物，就提供了全世界三分之二的食物供应。

"We're dependent on these four," he says.

“我们的食物供应依赖于这四种作物，”他说。

"But actually there're 7,000 crops we've been farming for thousands of years. We ignore all of those."

“但实际上，我们耕种了几千年的作物多达7000种，而我们却对它们视而不见。”

Researchers are trying to unlock the potential of these ignored crops—plants they describe as forgotten, under-used or "alternative" as they are displaced by increasingly uniform diets fuelled by processed ingredients from the major crops.

研究人员正试图释放这些被忽视作物的潜力——由于主要作物的加工配料催生出越来越单一的饮食结构，这些植物被挤出主流，因而被人们描述为被遗忘、未充分利用或“替代”作物。

D

It's a timely quest.

这是一次恰逢其时的探索。

The food sector is already responsible for nearly a third of global greenhouse gas emissions.

食品行业目前占全球温室气体排放量的近三分之一。

By 2050 it estimates the world must produce 50% more food to feed the projected global population of 10 billion.

该报告预计，到2050年，全球粮食产量必须增加50%，才能养活预计达到100亿的世界人口。

Meeting this demand without contributing to climate change calls for urgent solutions.

要在不加剧气候变化的前提下满足这一需求，亟需找到解决方案。

E

Forgotten crops hold key answers.

被遗忘的作物蕴含着关键答案。

By investing in neglected local plants, countries can reduce their reliance on imported crops and their carbon-heavy supply chains.

通过投资开发被忽视的本地作物，各国可以减少对进口作物及其高碳排放供应链的依赖。

Bringing back the variety of crops humans once ate also boosts food security at a time warming climates threaten existing crops.

恢复人类曾经食用过的作物多样性，还能在气候变暖威胁现有作物之际增强粮食安全。

On top of that forgotten crops are among the most climate-resilient and nutritious, argues Azam-Ali.

阿扎姆-阿里指出，除此之外，被遗忘的作物还是最具气候韧性和营养价值的作物之一。

His summary is plain: "Dietary diversification is critical to the future of humanity."

他的总结简明扼要："饮食多样化对人类的未来至关重要。"

F

Food security experts agree.

粮食安全专家对此表示认同。

"There is no food insecurity in the world, there is food ignorance," says Cecilia Tortajada, a senior research fellow at the Institute of Water Policy at the National University of Singapore.

新加坡国立大学水政策研究所高级研究员塞西莉亚·托尔塔哈达说："世界上不存在粮食不安全的问题，存在的只是对粮食的无知。"

"Whenever we have native crops we tend to disregard them as if they were not valuable but they are," she adds.

她补充道："每当我们拥有本土作物时，往往视而不见，仿佛它们毫无价值，但实际上它们价值非凡。"

G

Azam-Ali knows that scepticism firsthand.

阿扎姆-阿里对这种质疑深有体会。

He came across alternative crops in the 1980s through the work of women farmers he met in Niger.

20世纪80年代，他在尼日尔结识了几位女农人，通过她们的工作首次接触到了替代作物。

The then-PhD student remembers marvelling at the crops they grew in their backyards, without the benefits of technology, to feed their families when the big crops failed.

这位当时的博士生回忆说，那些女农人在自家后院种植的作物令他惊叹不已——没有借助任何技术手段，她们靠这些作物养活家人，度过大田作物歉收的难关。

He saw a tremendous opportunity to build alternative food systems.

他看到了构建替代粮食体系的巨大机遇。

But "the resistance was enormous," he recalls.

但据他回忆，"当时的阻力非常大。"

H

Undeterred, he ploughed on.

他毫不气馁，坚持推进。

Project after project helped prove these crops were viable in different environments as alternatives to the staple ones.

一个又一个项目陆续开展，证明了这些作物能够在不同环境中种植，可作为主粮的替代品。

But the question of whether these crops would be marketable remained.

但这些作物能否打开市场，始终是个悬而未决的问题。

"That's the critical thing," he says.

他说："这是最关键的一点。"

In one of the centre's domes, food technologist Tan Xinlin uses powdered moringa leaves in place of some wheat flour to bake a cake lower in gluten and higher in nutrients.

在该中心的一座穹顶温室内，食品技术员谭歆林（Tan Xinlin）用辣木叶粉替代部分小麦粉来烘焙蛋糕，使蛋糕的麸质含量更低、营养更丰富。

Tan's job is to create recipes with these still-unfamiliar ingredients that will appeal to both local and international tastes.

谭歆林的工作，是用这些仍不为人熟知的食材研发食谱，使其同时迎合本地与国际的口味。

In recent years she has used some of the forgotten crops grown at CFF, such as moringa and bambara groundnut, to make everything from instant soup to Indian snacks.

近年来，她利用CFF种植的一些被遗忘的作物，如辣木和班巴拉豆，制作了从速食汤到印度小吃等各种食品。

"I try to modernise forgotten crops instead of using old recipes." says Tan, who is also a trained chef.

谭歆林也是一名受过专业训练的厨师，她说："我致力于让被遗忘的作物焕发新生，而不是照搬老配方。"

It's a strategy to appeal to the world's growing middle classes who are increasingly turning to the fast and processed food industries.

这一策略旨在吸引全球日益壮大的中产阶级，他们正越来越多地转向快餐和加工食品产业。

It's also a way to help counter perceptions of local crops as "old or poor people's food" or as inferior "women's crops", adds Tan.

谭补充说，这也是一种有助于消除人们对本地作物成见的方式——人们往往视其为"穷人或老人吃的东西"，或低人一等的"妇女作物"。

I

The roots of these connotations about local foods can run deep.

这些关于本地食物的负面联想可能根深蒂固。

The bambara groundnut, a protein-rich native crop of sub-Saharan Africa that is also grown in parts of southeast Asia, can trace its marginalisation to colonial rule.

班巴拉花生是撒哈拉以南非洲的一种高蛋白本土作物，东南亚部分地区也有种植，其被边缘化的历史可追溯至殖民统治时期。

"African women who grew bambara groundnut were actually punished for growing it," says Azam-Ali.

阿扎姆-阿里说："种植班巴拉花生的非洲妇女实际上因此受到惩罚。"

"Colonial powers said you can't grow that because there's no oil. We can't get a market for it."

"殖民当局说你们不能种这个，因为它不产油，我们卖不出去。"

But today the bambara murukku is one of CFF's best reviewed foods and they are aiming to get it into grocery stores, pointing to the success of crops like quinoa to potential investors.

但如今，班巴拉穆鲁库（一种用班巴拉花生制成的传统零食）是CFF好评最多的食品之一，他们正设法将其推向杂货店，并以藜麦等作物的成功案例向潜在投资者游说。

Some 30 years ago, quinoa was virtually unheard of outside its native mountains in Bolivia and Peru.

大约30年前，藜麦在其原产地玻利维亚和秘鲁的山区之外几乎无人知晓。

Today the nutritious grain is found on the menus of lavish restaurants across the world.

如今，这种营养丰富的谷物已出现在全球豪华餐厅的菜单上。

J

Measuring crops by nutrition instead of yield is at the heart of the forgotten foods enterprise.

以营养价值而非产量来衡量作物，正是"被遗忘的食物"这一事业的核心理念。

Ever since the "green revolution" of the 1960s, high-yielding crops have dominated modern agriculture.

自20世纪60年代"绿色革命"以来，高产作物一直主导着现代农业。

That was in part a crucial response to devastating famines at a time when the world needed to increase its food supply.

在某种程度上，这是对当时毁灭性饥荒的一种关键应对，彼时世界亟需增加粮食供应。

Today "nutrition is becoming a time bomb", says Azam-Ali, as growing carbon dioxide levels strip crops of their minerals.

阿扎姆-阿里说，随着二氧化碳浓度不断升高，农作物中的矿物质被逐渐耗尽，“营养正成为一颗定时炸弹”。

Instead of bio-fortifying major crops we should be investing in those forgotten crops that are already more nutritious, he asserts.

他主张，与其对主要作物进行生物强化，不如投资于那些本就营养更丰富的被遗忘作物。

K

In the bowels of CFF's third dome, lab manager Gomathy Sethuraman opens a window into the centre's "crown jewels", revealing vines of winged beans growing under a bright yellow light.

在CFF第三个穹顶深处，实验室经理戈马蒂·塞图拉曼打开一扇窗，向人们展示了该中心的“镇馆之宝”——在明亮的黄光下生长的四角豆藤蔓。

It's one of multiple chambers where scientists are studying the impact of higher temperatures and carbon dioxide levels on the nutritional make-up of alternative crops.

这是众多实验舱之一，科学家们在此研究升温和二氧化碳浓度升高对替代作物营养构成的影响。

This research is "the game changer", says Azam-Ali, ensuring that "future crops" are also the healthiest ones in warmer climates.

阿扎姆-阿里说，这项研究是“游戏规则的改变者”，它确保“未来的作物”在更温暖的气候中同样是最健康的。

L

There is a growing global momentum around forgotten foods, says Danielle Nierenberg, president of Food Tank, a US-based think tank.

美国智库Food Tank（食物力量）总裁丹妮尔·尼伦伯格表示，全球对被遗忘食物的关注度正与日俱增。

Other than CFF, which bills itself as the world's first research centre dedicated solely to under-utilised crops, there are other key groups championing agricultural diversity including Crop Trust, Slow Food, Icrisat and Bioversity International.

除了CFF——该中心自称是全球首家专门致力于利用不足作物的研究中心——还有其他多个关键组织在推动农业多样性，包括作物信托基金、慢食运动、国际半干旱热带作物研究所（Icrisat）和国际农业生物多样性应用研究机构（Bioversity International）。

Add to that more middle-income consumers searching for nutritious foods and others eager to try the unprocessed foods their grandparents once ate, she says.

她说，此外还有越来越多的中等收入消费者在寻找营养食品，以及一些人渴望尝试祖辈曾食用的未加工食品。

M

But the rising interest in forgotten foods in some quarters is overtaken by the global spread of Western-style diets heavy in sugar, fat and processed foods in others.

然而，一些人对被遗忘食物日益增长的兴趣，正被另一些地区西方饮食模式的全球扩张所盖过——这些饮食富含糖分、脂肪和加工食品。

N

A key obstacle to promoting fading local crops in Malaysia, for example, is "the obsession with imported products", says Jenifer Kuah, co-founder of a restaurant that champions locally-sourced food in an affluent suburb of Kuala Lumpur.

例如，吉隆坡一个高档社区的本土食材餐厅联合创始人珍妮弗·库阿表示，马来西亚推广本土日渐式微作物的一大障碍是“对进口产品的痴迷”。

Customers at Sitka, regarded as a pioneer in the country's small farm-to-table dining scene, still seek foreign ingredients as a "status symbol", she says.

她说，Sitka餐厅被视为该国小型农场直供餐饮的先驱，但其顾客仍把外国食材视为一种“身份象征”。

O

The argument for forgotten foods feels intuitive.

支持食用被遗忘食物的理由似乎不言自明。

Some analysts say it is in fact inevitable.

一些分析人士称，这其实是不可避免的。

"Climate change is going to mean almost certainly tastes are going to be forced to change," says Tim Lang, professor of food policy at City, University of London.

伦敦大学城市学院食品政策教授蒂姆·朗表示：“气候变化几乎注定会迫使人们的口味发生改变。”

We "have to get used to eating other crops" as yields of staple crops fall, he says.

他说，随着主要农作物产量下降，我们“不得不习惯食用其他作物”。

Section C · 仔细阅读 Passage 1447 词 · 26 句

P1

Imagine you're an alien sent to Earth to document the behaviour of the mammals inhabiting the planet.

想象一下，你是一名被派往地球的外星人，任务是记录这颗星球上哺乳动物的行为。

You stumble into a movie theatre that's showing the latest Hollywood horror film.

你误打误撞走进一家电影院，里面正在放映最新的好莱坞恐怖片。

P2

Several dozen humans are gathered together in a dark, undecorated room.

几十个人类聚集在一间昏暗、毫无装饰的房间里。

They're all staring at a rectangular area on which patterns of light change rapidly.

他们都盯着墙上的一个矩形区域，那上面的光影图案在飞速变幻。

P3

They are clearly in a state of high arousal.

他们显然处于高度兴奋的状态。

Their heart rate is elevated, they occasionally glance around nervously, and they sometimes jump collectively in their seats, and emit high-pitched warning calls.

他们的心率加快，不时紧张地四下张望，有时还会集体从座位上跳起来，发出尖锐的警报声。

P4

Eventually, the lights come up and the rectangular screen goes black.

终于，灯光亮起，那块矩形屏幕暗了下去。

The humans stand up and leave the room, chatting and laughing, and showing signs of pleasure.

人类站起身来离开房间，一边聊天一边欢笑，露出了愉悦的神情。

P5

Why do these humans voluntarily expose themselves to what appears to be a deeply unpleasant experience?

这些人类为什么自愿让自己经历一场看似极其难受的体验？

And why do they react so strongly to those patterns of light on a screen?

他们又为什么会对屏幕上那些光影图案做出如此强烈的反应？

P6

I am fascinated with the paradox of horror—the strange fact that many people seek out scary entertainment.

恐怖带来的悖论令我着迷——许多人竟会主动寻求惊吓式的娱乐，这一事实着实奇特。

P7

I think the answer to the puzzle lies in human nature.

我认为这个谜题的答案就在于人性。

P8

My research suggests that we humans evolved to find pleasure in situations that allow us to experience negative emotions in a safe context.

我的研究表明，人类在进化中形成了一种倾向：在安全的情境中体验负面情绪时，会感到愉悦。

You can see these elements of horror in children's games.

你可以在儿童游戏中看到恐怖的这些要素。

Take hide-and-seek for example, which is a simulation of a predator-prey interaction.

以捉迷藏为例，它模拟的正是捕食者与猎物之间的互动。

The kid hides and the adult pretends to be a predator, searching for the child while howling like a dangerous beast.

孩子躲藏起来，成年人则扮演捕食者，一边像危险的野兽般嚎叫，一边搜寻孩子。

P9

This simulation gives the child crucial information about how to avoid becoming prey, and children tend to find that kind of activity deeply satisfying, presumably because it gives them a safe experience of a potentially catastrophic scenario.

这种模拟向孩子传递了如何避免成为猎物的关键信息，而孩子们往往会觉得这类活动极具满足感，大概是因为它让孩子在安全的环境中体验了潜在的灾难性情境。

P10

They find it pleasurable, and pleasure is evolution's way of motivating us toward adaptive behaviour.

他们从中获得愉悦感，而愉悦正是进化用以激励我们采取适应性行为的方式。

P11

Horror is pleasurable to many people because it lets us play with negative emotions and develop coping strategies.

恐怖之所以让许多人感到愉悦，是因为它让我们得以把玩负面情绪，并发展出应对策略。

We learn what it feels like to be truly afraid, and we learn how to handle negative emotion.

我们得以体会真正恐惧时是什么感受，也学会如何应对负面情绪。

P12

How, then, does horror work?

那么，恐怖究竟是如何起作用的？

My research suggests that horror works by exploiting an ancient set of biological defence mechanisms—an evolved fear system, which we share with other animals.

我的研究表明，恐怖的运作方式是利用一套古老的生物防御机制——一套与其他动物共有的、经过进化的恐惧系统。

But humans are uniquely imaginative, and we use our evolved imagination to travel into virtual worlds that are full of danger.

但人类具有独特的想象力，我们运用进化而来的想象力，进入充满危险的虚拟世界。

P13

There are good reasons for watching a horror film, even if you're not a loyal horror fan.

即使你并非恐怖片的忠实拥趸，看恐怖片也有充分的理由。

If you make it through the film in one piece, you'll probably experience a strong sense of mastery, a sense that you were able to make it through an appalling experience.

如果你能完整地看完这部电影，你很可能会获得一种强烈的掌控感，一种你成功挺过了一段可怕经历的感觉。

Anyway, watching a horror film makes you better at handling your own fear, and who knows when that will become critically relevant?

总之，看恐怖片能让你更善于应对自己的恐惧，而谁又知道这种能力何时会变得至关重要呢？
