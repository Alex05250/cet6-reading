We humans love to believe we are special, but science and technology have repeatedly proved this conviction wrong.

我们人类总爱自认为与众不同，但科学和技术已一再证明这种信念是错误的。

People once thought that humans were the only animals to use tools, to form teams or to propagate culture, but science has shown that other animals do each of these things.

人们曾认为只有人类才会使用工具、组建团队或传承文化，但科学已经表明，其他动物同样具备这些能力。

Meanwhile, technology has quashed, one by one, claims that cognitive tasks require a human brain.

与此同时，技术也逐一击破了那些声称认知任务必须由人脑来完成的观点。

The first adding machine was invented in 1623.

第一台加法机发明于1623年。

This past year, a computer-generated work won an art contest.

去年，一幅由计算机生成的作品在一场艺术比赛中获奖。

I believe the moment when computers meet and surpass human intelligence is on the horizon.

我认为，计算机达到并超越人类智能的时刻已为期不远。

How will human intelligence and creativity be valued when machines become smarter and more creative than the brightest people?

当机器变得比最聪明的人更聪明、更有创造力时，人类的智慧和创造力又将如何被衡量和珍视？

There will likely be a spectrum.

届时很可能会出现多种情况并存的局面。

In some domains, people still value humans doing things, even if a computer can do it better.

在某些领域，人们仍然看重由人来做事，即使计算机做得更好也不例外。

It's been a quarter of a century since a computer beat world champion Garry Kasparov, but human chess hasn't gone away.

距离计算机击败世界冠军加里·卡斯帕罗夫已经过去了四分之一个世纪，但人类的国际象棋并没有因此消亡。

In other fields, human skill will seem costly and redundant.

在其他领域，人类的技能将显得代价高昂且多余。

Take illustration for example: most readers don't care whether the graphic accompanying a magazine article was drawn by a person or a computer — they just want it to be relevant, new and perhaps entertaining.

以插画为例：大多数读者并不在意杂志文章配图是人画的还是计算机画的——他们只希望配图切题、新颖，或许还能带来一些趣味。

And, of course, this question isn't black or white.

而且，这个问题当然并非非黑即白。

Many fields will be a hybrid, where some humans find a lucky place, but most of the work is done by computers, like in manufacturing today, where much of the work is accomplished by robots, but some humans must be there to oversee the machines.

许多领域将呈现人机混合的模式——少数人能在其中找到一席之地，但大部分工作由计算机完成，就像今天的制造业一样，大量工作由机器人完成，但仍需一些人在场监督机器运转。

If history is any guide, it's almost certain that advances in AI will cause more jobs to vanish, that creative workers with human-only skills will become richer but fewer in number, and that those who own creative technology will become the new ultra-rich.

如果以史为鉴，几乎可以肯定，人工智能的进步将导致更多工作岗位消失，拥有仅人类才具备技能的创意工作者会变得更富有但人数更少，而掌握创意技术的人将成为新的超级富豪。

A

Projects, relationships, and jobs don't always work out as planned — and it's not always clear when to give up.

项目、人际关系和工作并不总会按计划发展——而什么时候该放弃，也往往并不明朗。

We've all repeatedly heard the saying that "quitters never win", but sometimes the best decision is to cut our losses.

我们都反复听过“半途而废者永远赢不了”这句老话，但有时候，最明智的决定恰恰是及时止损。

B

Think about that relationship you held on to even after it had run its course or the job you stayed in despite your boss making you miserable.

想想那段早已走到尽头你却迟迟不肯放手的感情，或者那份让你在上司手下备受煎熬却依然固守的工作。

Many of us try to hold onto the last ray of hope, thinking we can make it work, whatever "it" is.

我们许多人都会死死抓住最后一丝希望不放，觉得自己能让事情成——不管这个“事情”究竟是什么。

And while perseverance is generally an excellent quality, it may also incline you to over-invest in something that is no longer a good idea.

诚然，毅力通常是一种极可贵的品质，但它也可能让你在早已不再明智的事情上投入过头。

C

As an executive coach, I work with numerous passionate and hard-working leaders who don't want to "quit".

作为一名高管教练，我接触过许多充满激情、勤奋努力的领导者，他们都不愿意“认输”。

But the smartest leaders learn to discern the difference between quitting too soon and holding onto something that they shouldn't.

然而，最聪明的领导者懂得分辨何时是过早放弃，何时又是在不该坚持的事情上执迷不悟。

D

We are all susceptible to the sunk-cost delusion, an unconscious bias that leads us to persist in an endeavor we have already invested time, effort, or money into — even when abandoning it would be more beneficial.

我们都会受到沉没成本谬误的影响——这是一种无意识的偏见，驱使我们继续坚持一项已经投入了时间、精力或金钱的事业，哪怕放弃它反而更有利。

We realize that we can't get back the resources we've given, so we persevere — investing in a project that should be halted, staying in a relationship that should be over, or finishing a book we no longer enjoy and should put down, because we're already halfway through it.

我们意识到，已经付出的资源无法收回，于是便选择坚持——继续投入一个本应叫停的项目，守着一段本应结束的感情，或硬着头皮读完一本早已索然无味、本该放下的书，只因为已经读了一半。

If quitting is "not your thing", you may be especially susceptible to this bias.

如果“半途而废”从来不是你的作风，你可能尤其容易受到这种偏见的影响。

E

The sunk-cost delusion causes us to overly worry about what we'll lose if we move on and not think enough about the costs of not moving on.

沉没成本谬误让我们过分忧虑一旦向前走会失去什么，却没有充分考虑不向前走的代价。

To offset this bias, deliberately refocus your thinking on the gains so you can more objectively weigh the alternatives.

要抵消这种偏见，请刻意把思路重新聚焦于收益上，从而更客观地权衡各种选择。

Ask yourself: What might I gain by cutting my losses now?

问问自己：现在及时止损，我可能会得到什么？

Will you be happier or have time for another (better) opportunity?

你会更快乐，还是会有时间去抓住另一个（更好的）机会？

Then ask yourself: What will it cost me to soldier on?

然后再问问自己：继续硬撑下去，我会付出什么代价？

For example, is it possible that you'll be throwing more good money after what you've already lost?

比如，你是否可能在已经损失的基础上，继续把更多的钱投进去？

Or that you won't have the energy to capitalize on other promising possibilities?

又或者，你是否会因为继续坚持，而没有精力去把握其他有前景的机会？

F

The illusion of control can also interfere with our best judgment, leading us to overestimate our ability to control events and attain a positive outcome.

控制错觉也会干扰我们的最佳判断，使我们高估自己控制事件、获得积极结果的能力。

This bias gives us a sense of agency and can promote mental health.

这种偏差给我们一种自主感，有助于促进心理健康。

However, the bias can also result in escalating commitment to a losing course of action.

然而，这种偏差也可能导致我们对失败的行动方针不断加深投入。

Especially when combined with an "I'm not a quitter" mentality, feeling like we have more control over an event or person than we do puts us at risk of doubling down when we should pull out.

尤其是当这种偏差与'我不是半途而废的人'的心态结合在一起时，感觉自己对某件事或某个人的控制力比实际更大，会让我们冒着应该退出时却加倍下注的风险。

G

To counteract this bias, consider your situation and make a simple two-column list of what's in your control and what's not.

要抵消这种偏差，不妨审视一下自己的处境，简单列出两栏：哪些是我能控制的，哪些是我不能控制的。

Think rigorously.

请认真地思考。

While you may be able to influence other people and various circumstances, you can't force them to change or go your way.

虽然你或许能影响他人和各种情况，但你无法强迫他们改变或按你的意愿行事。

H

Getting clear on what you can control and what you can't is essential to making a quality decision about whether to call it quits or persevere.

厘清自己能控制什么、不能控制什么，对于明智地决定是放弃还是坚持，至关重要。

With a written list in hand, you can ensure you focus on what you can control and better assess whether your continued efforts are worth the expense.

有了这份书面清单，你就能确保自己专注于可控之事，从而更好地评估继续付出的努力是否值得。

Expending effort and emotion on things you can't control can be both draining and disempowering.

在无法控制的事情上耗费精力和情感，既会令人疲惫，也会让人感到无能为力。

I

Research indicates that people link their self-identity and social status to their commitments.

研究表明，人们会将自我认同和社会地位与自己所承担的事务联系在一起。

And because we identify with our commitments, withdrawing from one can feel like a threat to our identity or status.

而且，由于我们与自己所承担的事务息息相关，退出其中一项可能会让人觉得是对自身身份或地位的威胁。

J

Take my client Ryan, a leader at a technology company.

以我的客户瑞安为例，她是一家科技公司的领导者。

Ryan had invested two years and a lot of effort in a design project that wasn't delivering the needed outcomes, but the thought of abandoning it after all she had put in was hard to bear.

瑞安已经在一个设计项目上投入了两年时间和大量精力，但该项目始终未能取得预期成果；然而，想到自己付出了这么多却要半途而废，她实在难以接受。

As Ryan and I explored whether she should cancel the initiative she'd been leading or continue to persevere, she stated, "The thing is, I'm not a quitter."

在瑞安与我探讨她究竟是应该取消自己一直主导的这个项目，还是继续坚持下去时，她说："问题是，我不是一个轻言放弃的人。"

This singular, fixed sense of self was clouding her judgment and limiting her choices for action.

这种单一而僵化的自我认知蒙蔽了她的判断力，也限制了她可选择的行动方案。

K

Our self-concept and identity are mental concepts that influence our behavior.

自我概念和身份认同是影响我们行为的心理观念。

If you strongly identify as not being a quitter, you can end up staying stuck in a course of action due to your narrow and rigid self-concept.

如果你强烈地认为自己不是一个轻言放弃的人，那么你可能会因为自我概念的狭隘与僵化，而困在既有的行动路线中无法自拔。

To overcome this kind of internal obstacle, recall your other positive traits and personality characteristics.

要克服这类内在障碍，请回想自己其他的积极特质和性格特点。

Ask yourself: What additional strengths do I have (or aspire to have) that would be helpful to employ here?

问一问自己：我还有哪些额外的优势（或渴望具备的优势）能在这里派上用场？

For example, when I asked Ryan what other positive qualities from her personality she needed to leverage for decision making, she replied, "Discernment and bravery."

例如，当我问瑞安，在她的性格中还有哪些积极品质可以运用到决策中时，她回答说：“明辨与勇气。”

L

Now, examine your situation through the lens of each of these facets of yourself.

现在，通过自身这些不同的侧面来审视你的处境。

What does the discerning part of you say?

你内心那个明辨的部分会怎么说？

The brave part?

那个勇敢的部分呢？

Considering your decision through these different characteristics will enable you to see that you have more choices.

从这些不同特质的角度来思考你的决定，能让你看到自己拥有更多选择。

It's also helpful to seek external sources of information, rather than relying solely on your thinking and instincts.

寻求外部信息源也很有帮助，而不是仅仅依赖自己的思考和直觉。

But it's important to think through whose opinions you solicit.

不过，重要的是要想清楚你该听取哪些人的意见。

M

Ideally, seek out people who are less invested in your decision than you are.

理想情况下，去找那些对你的决定投入程度不如你的人。

If you're considering whether to shut down your entrepreneurial venture, for instance, reach out to other founders as opposed to just your business partner.

比如，如果你正在考虑是否关闭自己的创业项目，就应该向其他创始人求助，而不只是找你的商业伙伴。

Similarly, if you're looking for love advice, ask someone removed from the ups and downs of your relationship instead of relying only on your best friend.

同样，如果你在寻求恋爱方面的建议，可以去问一个与你的感情起伏无关的人，而不是只依赖你最好的朋友。

In all cases, I recommend describing your situation and following up with a question like: "How would you manage this situation?"

在任何情况下，我都建议你先描述自己的处境，然后接着问一个问题，比如：“换作你会如何处理这种情况？”

N

Alternately, you could also seek out hard data that illuminates your real chance of success in whatever endeavor you are contemplating.

或者，你也可以寻找翔实的数据，来说明你在所考虑的任何事业上真正成功的机会有多大。

Sticking with the example of the entrepreneurial venture, numbers are your best friend.

仍以创业为例，数据是你最好的朋友。

Use them to remove yourself from the love you have for this idea and consider your burn rate relative to growth plan, industry growth rates, and other factors that predict start-up success.

借助数据，把你对这个想法的热爱放到一边，冷静审视你的资金消耗速度相对于增长计划、行业增长率以及其他预示创业成功因素的关系。

External sources of information can help you see angles and possibilities you might not have considered.

外部信息来源能帮助你看到自己可能未曾考虑过的角度和可能性。

O

When something that seemed so promising at the start doesn't turn out the way we imagined, we can become overly self-critical.

当一件起初看似大有希望的事情最终没有如我们所料时，我们可能会变得过度自我批评。

Nobody likes to feel that they've made a poor decision, especially if making intelligent decisions is a quality you hold in high esteem.

没有人愿意觉得自己做了一个糟糕的决定，尤其是当你极为看重自己具备明智决策能力的时候。

The most effective strategy here is to give yourself a break.

在这种情况下，最有效的策略是放过自己。

Practicing self-compassion increases your resilience, emotional intelligence, compassion toward others, and can boost your performance as well as help you develop a growth mindset.

践行自我关怀能增强你的韧性、情商和对他人的同理心，还能提升你的表现，并帮助你培养成长型思维。

P

Instead of deliberating and criticizing yourself, focus instead on what you can learn from this situation.

与其反复琢磨、自我苛责，不如把注意力放在你能从这件事中学到什么上。

For example, Ryan recognized she had developed various new skills and leadership capabilities from leading the design project.

例如，瑞安意识到，她在领导设计项目的过程中习得了各种新技能和领导能力。

And despite ultimately deciding to drop it, she was still happy she had tried and grateful for what the experience taught her.

尽管最终决定放弃这个项目，她仍然为自己曾经尝试过而感到欣慰，并对这段经历所教给她的一切心怀感激。

Q

Undeniably, perseverance can help us succeed.

不可否认，坚持不懈能帮助我们取得成功。

But for your well-being, it's important to learn how to distinguish quitting too soon and clinging to a losing course of action.

但为了你的幸福，学会区分过早放弃与死守一条注定失败的路，这一点很重要。

While letting go can be difficult, it will free up your time, energy, and mental space to imagine new possibilities and pursue new opportunities.

放手虽然不易，却能释放出你的时间、精力和心智空间，让你去构想新的可能、追寻新的机会。

As interdependent beings we cannot thrive independent of others.

作为相互依存的个体，我们离不开他人而独自茁壮成长。

We like to work with people who are like-minded, with whom we share a common interest, or who also believe in a cause we are serious about.

我们喜欢与志同道合的人合作，与有共同兴趣的人共事，或与同样认同我们所认真追求之事业的人携手。

This is evident in how we have organized ourselves into various collective groups such as nations, regions, communities, organizations, and families.

这一点在我们如何将自身组织成各种集体群体——如国家、地区、社区、组织和家庭——中显而易见。

P2

Does the group you belong to have a strong collective consciousness or shared beliefs?

你所属的群体是否有强烈的集体意识或共同信念？

Is there interdependence among its members?

其成员之间是否存在相互依存的关系？

Do you consider this group a significant part of your existence?

你是否认为这个群体是你存在中的重要组成部分？

If your answers are affirmative, then you are experiencing a micro version of solidarity.

如果你的回答是肯定的，那么你正在体验一种微观层面的团结。

P3

What is solidarity?

什么是团结？

Its simplest definition in the dictionary is: "... a unifying opinion, feeling, purpose or interest among a group of people."

词典中最简单的定义是："……一群人之间一致的意见、情感、目标或利益。

P4

In this increasingly diverse society we live in, solidarity takes on a complex and multifaceted character as it forms bonds of interdependence among unique individuals living in a fast-changing environment and working within a complex division of labor.

在我们所处的这个日益多元的社会中，团结呈现出复杂而多面的特征，因为它在快速变化的环境中、在复杂分工的协作下，把一个个独特的个体联结成相互依存的纽带。

P5

One facet of solidarity fits well with the principles of communitarianism.

团结的一个侧面与社群主义的原则十分契合。

In this setting, individuals give up their own interests to serve the common good.

在这种情境下，个体舍弃自身利益，以服务于共同的福祉。

However, its strict adherence to social cohesiveness and exclusiveness restricts expression and practice of individual differences and preferences.

然而，它对社会凝聚力与排他性的严格坚守，限制了个体差异和个人偏好的表达与实践。

Consequently, individuality and autonomy are sacrificed to maintain the unity of the group.

因此，为了维护群体的统一，个性与自主性被牺牲了。

P6

Solidarity is also viewed as a reaction to the extreme emphasis on individualism and freedom of choice.

团结也被视为是对极端强调个人主义和选择自由的一种反应。

It goes against the liberal idea of unrestricted individuals who see society as a hindrance to their freedom to make choices and to act according to what is good for them regardless of how these will affect others.

它与自由主义理念相违背；在该理念下，个人是不受约束的，他们把社会视为对其自由选择和按自身利益行事的阻碍，而不管这些行为会如何影响他人。

P7

But despite its many facets, social theorists have viewed solidarity as a key factor in bringing about order and progress in society.

然而，尽管团结具有多重面向，社会理论家们仍将其视为推动社会秩序与进步的关键因素。

They believe that for human society to survive as it becomes more diverse, more new bonds need to be formed based on the differences.

他们认为，随着人类社会日益多元化，为了生存，需要在差异的基础上形成更多新的纽带。

In the light of this, they regard a genuine solidarity as more than just shallow relationships based on common interests.

基于此，他们认为真正的团结不仅仅是以共同利益为基础的肤浅关系。

Solidarity is not just a product of strategic calculations of how much it will benefit the self.

团结并非只是经过精打细算、衡量自我能从中获益多少的产物。

It is not an exclusive class founded on the you-and-me-against-the-world principle.

它也不是建立在“你我联合对抗世界”原则之上的排他性圈子。

Rather, it is inclusive and is not forced.

相反，它是包容的，且不是被强迫的。

Acting out of solidarity means supporting and standing up for each other — in recognition that one's fate is in the fate of the other.

出于团结而行动，意味着彼此支持、相互声援——承认一方的命运就存在于另一方的命运之中。

It is a bond characterized by cooperative practices and strengthened by the concern for the well-being of others or the common good.

这是一种以合作实践为特征、并因对他人福祉或公共利益的关切而得到加强的纽带。

It fights for the protection of human dignity and listens to and acts on the cries of the most vulnerable.

它为捍卫人的尊严而奋斗，倾听最弱势群体的呼声并付诸行动。

P1

People who repeatedly give unwanted advice can be well-meaning and genuinely want to help.

那些一再给出不请自来的建议的人，可能是出于好意，真心想帮忙。

Others might do this out of a sense of self-worth around the ability to influence others.

另一些人这样做，则是出于一种自我价值感——这种价值感建立在能够影响他人的能力之上。

One study found that people with a high tendency to seek power were more likely to give advice than those with an opposite tendency.

一项研究发现，权力寻求倾向高的人比倾向相反的人更可能给出建议。

P2

Under the guise of unselfishness, people may be driven to give you unwanted advice because it makes them feel powerful or in control.

在无私的幌子下，人们之所以被驱使着向你提出不请自来的建议，是因为这样做让他们感到自己有权力或能掌控局面。

They may not be fully aware of this drive, however.

然而，他们自己可能并未充分意识到这种驱动力。

P3

These people may also display a problematic degree of emotional vulnerability, becoming upset very quickly, and taking a long time to calm down.

这些人还可能表现出程度堪忧的情绪脆弱性：很快就变得心烦意乱，而且需要很长时间才能平复。

It is possible that their emotions were only validated in childhood when they were at their loudest, encouraging them to adopt responses to discomfort that are excessive in most situations.

很可能他们的情绪只有在童年时期声嘶力竭时才得到认可，这促使他们形成了一种应对不适的方式，而在大多数情境下这种反应都显得过度。

P4

When someone is giving advice in order to make themselves feel more powerful, there is underlying anxiety to their behavior that recipients of the advice tend to notice.

当一个人给出建议是为了让自己感觉更有力量时，其行为背后潜藏着焦虑，而接受建议的人往往也能察觉到。

It can be tempting in this situation to react harshly to the advice giver and to accuse them of being manipulative, but this approach might produce an undesired result.

在这种情况下，人们很容易冲动地严厉回应给出建议的人，指责他们居心叵测、操纵他人，但这种做法可能会带来事与愿违的结果。

P5

If the act of giving advice is contributing actively to someone's feelings of self-worth, an outright rejection may be perceived as a threat, activating their fight-or-flight response, possibly causing them to double down on their validation-seeking behavior, or leading to a larger conflict.

如果给出建议这一行为本身就在主动支撑某人的自我价值感，那么断然拒绝可能会被其视为一种威胁，从而触发他们的战斗或逃跑反应，可能促使他们变本加厉地寻求认可，或引发更大的冲突。

P6

The key is to validate without over-identifying.

关键在于给予认可，但不要过度认同。

You can let them know that you have heard them and appreciate where they are coming from without taking on the potentially damaging narrative that you could not have gotten by without their help.

你可以让他们知道，你听到了他们的话，也理解他们为何这样说，但不必接受那种可能有害的说法——即没有他们的帮助你就做不到。

You had better say something like, "Thanks for the idea. I have my own plan for handling this, but I really appreciate your perspective and will take it into consideration. Can I let you know when I need help in the future?"

你最好这样说："谢谢你的建议。这件事我自己有处理方案，但我很感激你的看法，会把它纳入考虑。以后我需要帮助时再向你求助，好吗？"

P7

If you have trouble setting boundaries tactfully, prioritize working on your own ability to self-regulate.

如果你在巧妙地设定界限方面有困难，那就优先提升自我调节的能力。

As uncomfortable as it may be to continuously receive, if you can respond with compassion, the situation will likely defuse much faster.

尽管不断地接受（建议）可能让人感到不适，但如果你能以善意回应，局面往往会更快地缓和下来。

P8

Though it is usually not the intended outcome of giving unsolicited advice, many who receive it often feel stressed, offended, or simply annoyed by unwanted suggestions.

虽然给出未经请求的建议通常并非出于让人不快的目的，但许多收到建议的人往往会因此感到压力、被冒犯，或单纯因不想要的建议而感到恼火。

Setting a boundary in this regard, if you feel you need one, is perfectly reasonable and something that can bring you increased emotional safety.

如果你觉得有必要，在这方面设定一个界限是完全合情合理的，而且这能给你带来更多的情感安全感。

P9

It is best to set boundaries in a way that takes into account the individual's underlying reasons for doing what they are doing to avoid unnecessary conflict and more stress.

设定界限时，最好充分考虑对方采取这些行为的深层原因，以避免不必要的冲突和更大的压力。
