A

We're experiencing the rebirth of smallness.

我们正在经历一场对‘小’的重新回归。

Farmers markets, tiny homes, and brew pubs all exemplify our love of smallness.

农贸市场、微型住宅和精酿啤酒馆，都体现了我们对‘小’的喜爱。

So do charter schools, coffee shops, and local bookstores.

特许学校、咖啡馆和社区书店亦是如此。

Small is often (but not always) more affordable, healthier, and sustainable, but its finest characteristic, the one that turns charm into love, is that going small allows us to be more fully who we are.

小巧往往（但并非总是）更实惠、更健康、更可持续，但它最出色的特质——也是将魅力化为热爱的那一特质——在于，选择‘小’让我们能够更充分地做自己。

B

In higher education the trend is mostly in the opposite direction: Universities with 20,000 or 30,000 students are considered "mid-sized".

在高等教育领域，这一趋势却大体相反：拥有两三万名学生的大学已算是‘中等规模’。

The nation's largest university, Arizona State University, has 80,000 students on campus and aims to enroll another 100,000 students online.

全美规模最大的亚利桑那州立大学在校学生达八万人，还计划再招收十万名在线学生。

At the other end of the spectrum is a handful of colleges that have fewer than a hundred students on campus and no online courses: colleges such as Sterling College, Thomas More College of Liberal Arts, and Deep Springs College.

而另一端，则是少数几所校园内学生不足百人且不设在线课程的学院，例如斯特林学院、托马斯莫尔文理学院和深泉学院。

These colleges are so small that they can only be called "tiny."

这些学院小到只能用‘微小’来形容。

C

Tiny colleges focus not just on a young person's intellect, but on the young person as a whole.

微小的学院不仅关注年轻人的才智，更关注年轻人的全面发展。

Equally important, tiny colleges ask, "How can education contribute to human flourishing and the well-being of the world?"

同样重要的是，微小的学院会追问：‘教育如何能促进人的蓬勃成长与世界的福祉？’

And they shape a college experience to address that question.

并且他们据此设计大学生活，以回应这个问题。

They replace concerns about institutional growth with attention to the growth of students as fully developed participants in their communities.

他们不再着眼于机构规模的扩张，转而关注学生成长为社区中全面发展的参与者。

D

I've had the privilege of teaching at three different institutions of higher learning during my career—a small liberal arts college and two mid-sized public universities.

在我的职业生涯中，我有幸在三所不同的高等院校任教——一所小型文理学院和两所中等规模的公立大学。

I've also been profoundly disappointed in each of these institutions, and in many of my colleagues, especially when it comes to helping students and preparing them for the many responsibilities of adulthood.

然而，我对这三所院校以及我的许多同事都深感失望，尤其是在帮助学生、为他们承担成年后的诸多责任做准备这方面。

Administrators focus on the business of running a university, and most faculty focus on their scholarship and teaching their discipline.

管理者专注于大学的运营事务，而大多数教师则专注于学术研究和本专业的教学。

Little deliberate attention is given to how students mature as individuals and social beings.

很少有人刻意关注学生作为个体和社会成员的成长过程。

E

Having just retired from teaching at a public university, I'm now returning to my hometown of Flagstaff, Arizona, to establish a tiny college—Flagstaff College.

我刚从一所公立大学退休，现在正回到家乡亚利桑那州弗拉格斯塔夫，创办一所微型学院——弗拉格斯塔夫学院。

I'm convinced there's a need for another type of education, one devoted to helping students come into their own and into this beautiful and troubled world.

我深信，我们需要另一种教育，一种致力于帮助学生实现自我、融入这个美丽而又充满问题的世界的教育。

Young people need an education that will provide them with meaning, hope, courage, and passion, as well as information and skills.

年轻人需要的教育，不仅要传授知识和技能，更要赋予他们意义、希望、勇气和热情。

Large institutions, I believe, are particularly ill-suited to this type of education.

我认为，大型院校尤其不适合开展这种教育。

F

There's no "best of" list when it comes to tiny colleges, at least not yet.

目前还没有关于微型学院的“最佳”排行榜，至少现在还没有。

But around the country people are creating new colleges that provide an alternative to small liberal arts colleges, large public universities, and online education.

但在全国各地，人们正在创办新的学院，为小型文理学院、大型公立大学和在线教育提供一种替代选择。

G

With only 26 students, Deep Springs is the smallest college in the country and, quite likely, the most atypical.

深泉学院只有26名学生，是全国最小的学院，也很可能是最非同寻常的学院。

Located on a working cattle ranch on the California-Nevada border, Deep Springs is a private, residential, two-year college for men, committed to educating students for "a life of service to humanity."

深泉学院位于加利福尼亚州与内华达州交界处的一个牧场内，是一所私立的寄宿制男子两年制学院，致力于培养学生“为人类服务的人生”。

Founded by the electricity tycoon L.L. Nunn in 1917, Deep Springs "curriculum" revolves around academics, labor, and self-governance.

深泉学院由电力大亨L.L.纳恩于1917年创办，其"课程"围绕学业、劳动和自治三个核心展开。

In addition to their courses, students are charged with running the 155-acre ranch and overseeing the functioning of the college.

除了上课之外，学生们还肩负着经营这155英亩牧场和管理学院运转的责任。

Students chair both the admissions and the curriculum committees.

招生委员会和课程委员会均由学生担任主席。

H

"Living in close community with one's teachers and fellow students, and being forced to take on adult responsibilities, makes for one's growth as a person," says William Hunt, who graduated last year.

去年毕业的威廉·亨特说："与老师和同学共同生活在紧密的社群中，被迫承担成年人的责任，这些都有助于一个人的成长。"

"To exist for very long in a community like that, you have to get over the question of whether you're sufficiently talented or principled and get started worrying about how you can stretch yourself and your peers, how much you can manage to learn with them."

"要在这样一个社群中长久地生存下去，你必须超越'自己是否有足够的才华或信念'这个问题，转而开始思考如何超越自己和同伴，如何与他们一起尽可能多地学到东西。"

I

Sterling College, in Craftsbury Common, Vermont, is also very small—fewer than 100 students.

位于佛蒙特州克拉夫茨伯里康蒙的斯特林学院同样规模极小——学生人数不足100人。

Unlike Deep Springs, Sterling focuses its curriculum on environmental and social justice issues, but like Deep Springs it places a high value on personal responsibility and manual labor.

与深泉学院不同，斯特林学院的课程侧重于环境和社会公正问题，但与深泉学院一样，它高度重视个人责任和体力劳动。

According to its catalog, a college education at Sterling combines "rigorous academics, roll-up-your-sleeves challenges, and good old hard work."

据该校招生手册介绍，斯特林学院的大学教育将"严谨的学术训练、挽起袖子的挑战和踏踏实实的辛勤劳动"融为一体。

J

The average tuition at a small liberal-arts college is $30,000 to $40,000 a year, not including the cost of living on campus, as compared to $8,000 to $10,000 a year for tuition alone at a public university.

一所小型文理学院的平均学费为每年3万至4万美元（不含住宿生活费），相比之下，公立大学仅学费一项每年就需8千至1万美元。

Of the tiny colleges, only Deep Springs doesn't charge tuition or room and board; students pay only for books and the cost of traveling to and from college.

在这些微型学院中，只有深泉学院不收学费和食宿费；学生只需支付书本费和往返学校的交通费用。

If tiny schools are to become a player on the higher education scene, they will need to find a way to be truly affordable.

如果微型学校要在高等教育领域占有一席之地，就必须找到真正负担得起的方式。

K

Doing so may not be that difficult so long as they do not pattern themselves too closely on existing norms.

只要它们不过于拘泥于现有的模式，做到这一点也许并不那么困难。

We've come to believe that a good college should have many academic programs and excellent facilities, posh dorms, an array of athletic programs, and a world-class student activity center.

我们逐渐形成这样一种观念：一所好的大学应当拥有众多的学术项目、一流的设施、豪华的宿舍、各式各样的体育项目，以及一个世界级的学生活动中心。

Imagine a good college without a climbing wall!

想象一下，一所好大学竟然没有攀岩墙！

We also have accepted the idea that college presidents, and their many vice-presidents, should be paid like their counterparts in the business world and that higher education requires an elaborate, up-to-date technology infrastructure.

我们也接受了这样一种看法：大学校长及其众多副校长应当像商界同行那样领取薪酬；而高等教育则需要一套精密、先进的技术基础设施。

All of this drives up the cost of education.

所有这些都推高了教育成本。

L

The "trick" to making tiny colleges affordable, if that's the right word, is simplicity.

让微型大学变得负担得起的“诀窍”——如果这个词用得恰当的话——就在于简约。

At its core, education is a human-to-human interaction.

教育的核心，是人与人之间的互动。

Reflecting on his own college education, President Garfield once commented that an ideal college would consist of nothing more than the legendary teacher Mark Hopkins on one end of a log and a student on the other.

加菲尔德总统回顾自己的大学教育时曾评论道，一所理想的大学不过是：一根圆木的一端坐着传奇教师马克·霍普金斯，另一端坐着一名学生，仅此而已。

The economics of a tiny college, in other words, might be similar to that of a tiny house.

换句话说，微型大学的运作方式可能与微型住宅相似。

Because it is small, a tiny house costs less to build and less to furnish, insure, and maintain.

由于面积小，微型住宅的建造成本较低，添置家具、投保和维护的费用也更低。

But the economic benefits of a small house don't end there.

但小房子带来的经济好处不止于此。

Tiny homes discourage homeowners from buying stuff that they really don't need, because there's no place to put it.

微型住宅让房主不再购买那些他们其实并不需要的东西，因为根本没有地方存放。

M

I'm a late convert to the idea of tiny colleges, and I fully understand the need for many diverse types of educational institutions.

我是很晚才转而认同微型大学这一理念的，而且我完全理解，社会需要多种多样的教育机构类型。

Academic research and job training are important, but tiny colleges aren't suited for either.

学术研究和职业培训固然重要，但规模很小的大学并不适合承担其中任何一项。

The educational needs of a complex society are themselves complex, and no single model can meet all of these needs.

复杂社会的教育需求本身也是复杂的，没有任何单一模式能够满足所有这些需求。

But I'm now convinced there's an educational need that's now going almost completely unmet: namely, the need to help young people transition into adulthood.

但我现在确信，有一种教育需求几乎完全被忽视了，那就是帮助年轻人顺利过渡到成年阶段。

Tiny colleges can do this better than any other type of educational institution.

规模很小的大学在这件事上比任何其他类型的教育机构都做得更好。

N

The ultimate justification for a tiny college is the conviction that each of us comes into our full humanity by close interaction with those who know and care for us, and that one of the basic purposes of higher education is social.

规模很小的大学存在的最根本理由，是这样一种信念：我们每个人只有通过与了解我们、关心我们的人密切互动，才能充分实现自己作为人的价值；而高等教育的基本目的之一是社会性的。

Although we give lip service to the idea that a college education will make us better people, when all's said and done, we think of higher education primarily in economic terms.

尽管我们口口声声说大学教育会让我们成为更好的人，但归根结底，我们主要还是从经济角度来理解高等教育的。

We've come to think of higher education as a means to make a living rather than make a life.

我们已渐渐把高等教育看成谋生的手段，而非成就人生的方式。

We've also come to see higher education as a private good rather than a public one.

我们还渐渐把高等教育看成一种私人商品，而非公共产品。

Tiny colleges are not the answer to all of our educational requirements, but they're an answer to one of our most basic educational necessities; the need to produce thoughtful, engaged, and compassionate human beings.

规模很小的大学并非能满足我们所有教育需求的答案，但它却回答了我们最基本的教育需求之一：培养有思想、有担当、有同情心的人。

黑猩猩是与人类亲缘关系最近的动物，与我们共享高达98%的基因。

然而，人类和黑猩猩的生活方式却大相径庭。

Fewer than 300,000 wild chimpanzees live in a few forested corners of Africa today, while humans have colonized every corner of the globe.

如今，野生黑猩猩的数量不足30万，仅栖息在非洲少数几处森林地带，而人类却已遍布地球的每一个角落。

At more than 7 billion, human population dwarfs that of nearly all other mammals—despite our physical weaknesses.

人类人口超过70亿，规模远超几乎所有其他哺乳动物——尽管我们身体并不强壮。

P2

What could account for our species' incredible evolutionary successes?

是什么造就了我们物种惊人的进化成就？

P3

One obvious answer is our big brains.

一个显而易见的答案是我们的大脑发达。

It could be that our raw intelligence gave us an unprecedented ability to think outside the box, innovating solutions to thorny problems as people migrated across the globe.

可能是我们的原始智力赋予了我们前所未有的创造性思维能力，使我们在全球迁徙的过程中不断革新，为棘手的难题找到解决方案。

P4

But a growing number of cognitive scientists and anthropologists are rejecting that explanation.

然而，越来越多的认知科学家和人类学家正在否定这一解释。

They think that, rather than making our living as innovators, we survive and thrive precisely because we don't think for ourselves.

他们认为，我们之所以能够生存并繁荣昌盛，恰恰不是因为我们是创新者、靠自己的头脑思考，而是正因为我们不自行思考。

Instead, people cope with challenging climates and ecological contexts by carefully copying others.

相反，人类通过仔细模仿他人来应对严峻的气候和复杂的生态环境。

P5

In a famous study, psychologists Victoria Horner and Andrew Whiten showed two groups of test subjects—children and chimpanzees—a mechanical box with a treat inside.

在一项著名的研究中，心理学家维多利亚·霍纳和安德鲁·惠滕向两组实验对象——儿童和黑猩猩——展示了一个内置奖励品的机械盒子。

In one condition, the box was opaque, while in the other it was transparent.

在一种实验条件下，盒子是不透明的；在另一种条件下，盒子是透明的。

The experimenters demonstrated how to open the box to retrieve the treat, but they also included the irrelevant step of tapping on the box with a stick.

实验人员示范了如何打开盒子取出零食，但他们还在过程中加入了一个无关的步骤——用棍子敲击盒子。

P6

Oddly, the children carefully copied all the steps to open the box, even when they could see that the stick had no practical effect.

奇怪的是，孩子们仔细地模仿了打开盒子的每一个步骤，即使他们明明看到那根棍子并没有任何实际作用。

That is, they copied irrationally: Instead of doing only what was necessary to get their reward, children slavishly imitated every action they'd witnessed.

也就是说，他们不加分辨地照搬了所有动作：为了拿到奖励，他们并非只做必要的步骤，而是机械地模仿了他们所看到的每一个动作。

P7

Of course, that study only included three—and four-year-olds.

当然，那项研究只涉及三岁和四岁的幼儿。

But additional research has shown that older children and adults are even more likely to mindlessly copy others' actions, and infants are less likely to over-imitate—that is, to precisely copy even impractical actions.

但后续研究表明，年龄较大的儿童和成年人反而更容易盲目地模仿他人的行为，而婴幼儿则较少出现过度模仿——即精确模仿那些甚至毫无实用性的动作。

P8

By contrast, chimpanzees in the study only over-imitated in the opaque condition.

相比之下，研究中的黑猩猩只在不透明盒子的条件下出现了过度模仿。

In the transparent condition—where they saw that the stick was mechanically useless—they ignored that step entirely.

在透明盒子的条件下——它们能看到那根棍子在机械结构上毫无用处——便会完全忽略那个步骤。

Other research has since supported these findings.

此后，其他研究也证实了这些发现。

P9

When it comes to copying, chimpanzees are more rational than human children or adults.

在模仿这件事上，黑猩猩比人类儿童和成年人更为理性。

P10

Where does the seemingly irrational human preference for over-imitation come from?

人类这种看似不理性的过度模仿倾向究竟从何而来？

Anthropologist Joseph Henrich points out that people around the world rely on technologies that are often so complex that no one can learn them rationally.

人类学家约瑟夫·亨里奇指出，世界各地的人们依赖的技术往往极其复杂，任何人都无法凭借理性将其学会。

Instead, people must learn them step by step, trusting in the wisdom of more experienced elders and peers.

相反，人们必须一步一步地学习这些技术，信赖更有经验的长辈和同伴的智慧。

P11

So the next time you hear someone arguing passionately that everyone should embrace nonconformity and avoid imitating others, you might laugh a little bit.

因此，下次你听到有人慷慨激昂地鼓吹每个人都应该标新立异、避免随波逐流时，你不妨会心一笑。

We're not chimpanzees, after all.

毕竟，我们又不是黑猩猩。
