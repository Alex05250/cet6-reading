古典音乐的声音能产生镇静效果，促使大脑释放带来愉悦感的多巴胺，有助于控制注意力、学习和情绪反应。

它还能降低身体的压力反应，使人整体心情更加愉悦。

事实证明，愉悦的心情能让人思维更加清晰。

尽管古典音乐有巴赫、贝多芬和亨德尔等众多杰出的作曲家，但这些音乐家的作品似乎都没有莫扎特的音乐那样显著的健康功效。

According to researchers, listening to Mozart can increase brain wave activity and improve cognitive function.

研究人员指出，听莫扎特的音乐能增强脑电波活动，改善认知功能。

另一项研究发现，莫扎特音乐的独特特征能激活大脑中负责高级心理功能的区域。

甚至产房也会播放莫扎特的音乐，帮助新生儿适应母体之外的生活。

古典音乐也可以成为帮助睡眠困难者的一大利器。

一项研究发现，有睡眠困难的学生在听古典音乐时睡得更好。

无论你平时是否经常听古典音乐，每天抽出一些时间听听让你感到舒缓的音乐，总是有益无害的。

Section B · 长篇阅读（信息匹配）1230 词 · 39 句

A

In the city of Athens, Georgia, there exists a rather curious local landmark—a large white oak that is almost universally stated to own itself.

在佐治亚州的雅典市，有一处颇为奇特的当地地标——一棵巨大的白橡树，几乎所有人都说它归自己所有。

Because of this, it is considered one of the most famous trees in the world.

正因如此，它被认为是世界上最著名的树木之一。

So how did this tree come to own itself and the land around it?

那么，这棵树是如何拥有自身及周边土地的呢？

B

Sometime in the 19th century a Georgian called Colonel William Jackson reportedly took a liking to the said tree and endeavored to protect it from any danger.

据传，19世纪某时，一位名叫威廉·杰克逊上校的佐治亚人看中了这棵树，并竭力保护它免受任何危害。

As to why he loved it so, the earliest documented account of this story is an anonymously written front page article in the Athens Weekly Banner published on August 12, 1890.

至于他为何如此钟爱这棵树，关于此事最早的有文字记载见于1890年8月12日《雅典周报》头版上一篇匿名文章。

It states, "Col. Jackson had watched the tree grow from his childhood, and grew to love it almost as he would a human. Its luxuriant leaves and sturdy limbs had often protected him from the heavy rains, and out of its highest branches he had many a time gotten the eggs of the feathered singers. He watched its growth, and when reaching a ripe old age he saw the tree standing in its magnificent proportions, he was pained to think that after his death it would fall into the hands of those who might destroy it."

文中写道："杰克逊上校从小看着这棵树长大，对它的喜爱几乎如同对人一般。它茂盛的枝叶和粗壮的树干常为他遮挡暴雨，他还多次从最高的枝头取下那些鸣禽的鸟蛋。他注视着它的成长，到了垂暮之年，看着这棵树亭亭如盖、气势非凡，一想到自己身后它可能落入那些会毁掉它的人手中，便深感痛心。"

C

Towards this end, Jackson transferred by means of a deed ownership of the tree and a little land around it to the tree itself.

为此，杰克逊通过一纸契约，将这棵树及周围一小片土地的所有权转让给了这棵树本身。

The deed read, "W. H. Jackson for and in consideration of the great affection which he bears the said tree, and his great desire to see it protected has conveyed unto the said oak tree entire possession of itself and of all land within eight feet of it on all sides."

契约写道："W. H. 杰克逊出于对该树的深厚感情，以及迫切希望看到它受到保护，特将该橡树自身的完整所有权及其四周各八英尺范围内所有土地的完整所有权，转让予该橡树。"

D

In time, the tree came to be something of a tourist attraction, known as The Tree That Owns Itself.

随着时间推移，这棵树逐渐成了一处旅游景点，被称为"拥有一切的树"（The Tree That Owns Itself）。

However, in the early 20th century, the tree started showing signs of its slow death, with little that could be done about it.

然而，到了20世纪初，这棵树开始显现出缓慢衰亡的迹象，人们却无能为力。

Father time comes for us all eventually, even our often long lived, tall and leafy fellow custodians of Earth.

时间终将带走一切，哪怕是这些常年茂盛、高大葱郁、与我们共同守护地球的树木。

Finally, on October 9, 1942, the over 30 meter tall and 200-400 year old tree fell, rumor has it, as a result of a severe windstorm and/or via having previously died and its roots rotted.

最终，1942年10月9日，这棵高逾30米、树龄约200至400年的巨树轰然倒下，据传是猛烈的风暴所致，也可能是先前已经死亡、根系腐烂所致。

E

About four years later, members of the Junior Ladies Garden Club (who'd tended to the tree before its unfortunate death) tracked down a small tree grown from a nut taken from the original tree.

大约四年后，少年淑女园艺俱乐部的成员（她们曾在那棵树不幸死去之前照料过它）找到了一棵由原树所结坚果培育而成的小树苗。

And so it was that on October 9, 1946, under the direction of Professor Roy Bowden of the College of Agriculture at the University of Georgia, this little tree was transplanted to the location of its ancestor.

于是，1946年10月9日，在佐治亚大学农学院罗伊·鲍登教授的指导下，这棵小树被移栽到了它祖先所在的位置。

A couple of months later, an official ceremony was held featuring none other than the Mayor of Athens, Robert L McWhorter, to commemorate the occasion.

两个月后，一场隆重的官方典礼举行了，雅典市市长罗伯特·L·麦霍特亲自出席，以纪念这一时刻。

F

This new tree became known as The Son of the Tree That Owns Itself and it was assumed that, as the original tree's heir, it naturally inherited the land it stood on.

这棵新树被称为'自有其树之子'，人们认为，作为原树的继承者，它自然也继承了脚下的土地。

Of course, there are many dozens of other trees known to exist descending from the original, as people taking a nut from it to grow elsewhere was a certainty.

当然，已知还有数十棵其他树木是原树的后代，因为人们从原树上取坚果到别处培育是必然的事。

That said, to date, none of the original tree's other children have petitioned the courts for their share of the land, so it seems all good.

话虽如此，迄今为止，原树的其他'子女'没有一个向法院申请过属于自己的那部分土地，所以看来一切都好。

In any event, The Son of the Tree That Owns Itself still stands today, though often referred to simply as The Tree That Owns Itself.

无论如何，'自有其树之子'至今仍矗立着，尽管人们常常简称它为'自有其树'。

G

This all brings us around to whether Jackson ever actually gave legal ownership of the tree to itself in the first place and whether such a deed is legally binding.

这一切把我们带回了一个问题：杰克逊当初是否真的将树的所有权授予了树本身，以及这样的契据是否具有法律效力。

H

Well, to begin with, it turns out Jackson only spent about three years of his life in Athens, starting at the age of 43 from 1829 to 1832, sort of dismissing the idea that he loved the tree from spending time under it as a child and watching it grow, and then worrying about what would happen to it after he died.

嗯，首先，事实证明杰克逊一生中只在雅典居住了大约三年，即1829年至1832年，从43岁开始——这基本推翻了他从小在树下玩耍、看着它长大，因而深爱这棵树，又担心自己去世后树的命运的说法。

Further, an extensive search of land ownership records in Athens does not seem to indicate Jackson ever owned the land the tree sits on.

此外，对雅典土地所有权记录的广泛查阅似乎并未表明杰克逊曾拥有这棵树所在的土地。

I

He did live on a lot of land directly next to it for those three years, but whether he owned that land or not isn't clear.

那三年间，他确实居住在紧邻该树的一大片土地上，但那片土地是否属于他并不清楚。

Whatever the case, in 1832 a four acre parcel, which included the land the tree was on and the neighboring land Jackson lived on, among others, was sold to University professor Malthus A Ward.

无论情况如何，1832年，一块四英亩的土地（包括树所在的土地以及杰克逊居住的邻近土地等）被卖给了大学教授马尔萨斯·A·沃德。

In the transaction, Ward was required to pay Jackson a sum of $1,200 (about $31, 000 today), either for the property itself or simply in compensation for improvements Jackson had made on the lot.

在这笔交易中，沃德需向杰克逊支付1200美元（约合今天的31000美元），这笔钱要么是用于购买该地产本身，要么就是单纯作为对杰克逊在该地块上所做改良的补偿。

In the end, whether he ever owned the neighboring lot or was simply allowed to use it while he allegedly worked at the University, he definitely never owned the lot the tree grew on, which is the most important bit for the topic at hand.

最终，无论他是否曾拥有相邻地块，或只是在据称于大学工作期间获准使用该地块，他都从未拥有过那棵树所在的地块——而这才是当前话题最关键的一点。

J

After Professor Ward purchased the land, Jackson and his family purchased a 655 acre parcel a few miles away and moved there.

沃德教授购得该土地后，杰克逊及其家人在几英里外购买了一块655英亩的地块并搬到了那里。

Ten years later, in 1844, Jackson seemed to have come into financial difficulties and had his little plantation seized by the Clarke County Sheriff's office and auctioned off to settle the mortgage.

十年后的1844年，杰克逊似乎陷入了财务困境，他的小种植园被克拉克县治安官办公室查封，并被拍卖以清偿抵押贷款。

Thus, had he owned some land in Athens itself, including the land the tree sat on, presumably he would have sold it to raise funds or otherwise had it taken as well.

因此，如果他在雅典市内拥有任何土地，包括那棵树所在的土地，想必他也会将其出售以筹集资金，或者同样被没收。

K

And whatever the case there, Jackson would have known property taxes needed to be paid on the deeded land for the tree to be truly secure in its future.

而无论情况如何，杰克逊本应知道，必须为已登记产权的土地缴纳财产税，那棵树的未来才能真正得到保障。

Yet no account or record indicates any trust or the like was set up to facilitate this.

然而，没有任何记载或记录显示曾设立过信托或类似安排来促成此事。

L

On top of all this, there is no hard evidence such a deed ever existed, despite the fact that deed records in Athens go back many decades before Jackson's death in 1876 and that it was supposed to have existed in 1890 in the archives according to the original anonymous news reporter who claims to have seen it.

除此之外，也没有确凿证据表明这样一份契据曾存在过，尽管雅典的契据记录可追溯至杰克逊1876年去世前数十年，且据那位声称见过它的匿名新闻记者所言，该契据在1890年时本应存于档案中。

M

As you might imagine from all of this, few give credit to this side of the story.

从上述种种情况不难想象，几乎没有人相信故事的这一版本。

So how did all of this come about then?

那么，这一切究竟是怎么来的呢？

N

It is speculated to have been invented by the imagination of the said anonymous author at the Athens Weekly Banner in the aforementioned 1890 front page article titled "Deeded to Itself", which by the way contained several elements that are much more easily proved to be false.

据推测，这可能是《雅典周报旗帜报》那位匿名作者的想象产物，出自前述1890年那篇题为《自行受让》的头版文章——顺带一提，该文包含若干更容易被证伪的元素。

As to why the author would do this, it's speculated perhaps it was a 19th century version of a click-bait thought exercise on whether it would be legal for someone to deed such a non-conscious living thing to itself or not.

至于作者为何要这样做，有人推测这或许是19世纪版的“标题党”式思想实验，探讨将此类无意识的生物以契据形式转让给其自身是否合法。

O

Whatever the case, the next known instance of the Tree That Owns Itself being mentioned wasn't until 1901 in the Centennial Edition of that same paper, the Athens Weekly Banner.

无论如何，下一次有记载提到“拥有自身的树”已是1901年，刊登在同一家报纸《雅典旗帜周报》的百年纪念特刊上。

This featured another account very clearly just copying the original article published about a decade before, only slightly reworded.

这篇报道又讲了一段叙述，很显然是照抄了大约十年前发表的那篇原文，只是措辞稍作改动。

The next account was in 1906, again in the Athens Weekly Banner, again very clearly copying the original account, only slightly reworded, the 19th century equivalent of re-posts when the audience has forgotten about the original.

下一次报道出现在1906年，同样登在《雅典旗帜周报》上，同样明显是抄录原始报道、仅略作改动，堪称19世纪版的“转发”——趁读者已把原作忘得差不多时再炒一遍冷饭。

Section C · 仔细阅读 Passage 1446 词 · 25 句

P1

It is irrefutable that employees know the difference between right and wrong.

毋庸置疑，员工都分得清是非对错。

So why don't more employees intervene when they see someone exhibiting at-risk behavior in the workplace?

那么，为什么当员工在工作场所看到有人表现出危险行为时，更多的人不出面干预呢？

P2

There are a number of factors that influence whether people intervene.

有诸多因素会影响人们是否选择出面干预。

First, they need to be able to see a risky situation beginning to unfold.

首先，他们需要能够察觉到风险情境正在形成。

Second, the company's culture needs to make them feel safe to speak up.

其次，企业文化需要让他们敢于发声而无后顾之忧。

And third, they need to have the communication skills to say something effectively.

再次，他们需要具备有效沟通的技巧，才能把话说得有理有据。

P3

This is not strictly a workplace problem; it's a growing problem off the job too.

这严格来说并非仅仅是工作场所的问题；在工作之外，这一问题也日益凸显。

Every day people witness things on the street and choose to stand idly by.

每天都有人在街头目睹种种事件，却选择袖手旁观。

This is known as the bystander effect—the more people who witness an event, the less likely anyone in that group is to help the victim.

这就是所谓的旁观者效应——目睹事件的人越多，其中任何一人伸出援手的可能性就越小。

The psychology behind this is called diffusion of responsibility.

其背后的心理机制被称为责任分散。

Basically, the larger the crowd, the more people assume that someone else will take care of it—meaning no one effectively intervenes or acts in a moment of need.

简而言之，人群越大，就越多人以为别人会去处理——结果在需要有人挺身而出的时刻，没有人真正干预或采取行动。

P4

This crowd mentality is strong enough for people to evade their known responsibilities.

这种从众心理之强，足以让人们逃避自己心知肚明的责任。

But it's not only frontline workers who don't make safety interventions in the workplace.

然而，不进行安全干预的并不只是一线员工。

There are also instances where supervisors do not intervene either.

有时主管也不会出面干预。

P5

When a group of employees sees unsafe behavior not being addressed at a leadership level it creates the precedent that this is how these situations should be addressed, thus defining the safety culture for everyone.

当一群员工看到不安全行为没有在管理层得到处理时，就等于树立了一个先例，让人认为这类情况本该如此处理，从而为所有人定义了安全文化。

P6

Despite the fact that workers are encouraged to intervene when they observe unsafe operations, this happens less than half of the time.

尽管企业鼓励员工在发现不安全操作时进行干预，但实际做到的情况不足一半。

Fear is the ultimate factor in not intervening.

恐惧是不进行干预的首要因素。

There is a fear of penalty, a fear that they'll have to do more work if they intervene.

人们害怕受到处罚，也担心一旦干预就要干更多的活。

Unsuccessful attempts in the past are another strong contributing factor to why people don't intervene—they tend to prefer to defer that action to someone else for all future situations.

过去干预失败的经历也是人们不愿干预的另一大原因——在今后的所有情形中，他们往往倾向于把这项行动推给别人去做。

P7

On many worksites, competent workers must be appointed.

在许多工地，必须指定具备资质的人员。

Part of their job is to intervene when workers perform a task without the proper equipment or if the conditions are unsafe.

其职责之一，就是在员工未使用适当设备或作业条件不安全时进行干预。

Competent workers are also required to stop work from continuing when there's a danger.

当出现危险时，具备资质的人员还必须叫停作业。

P8

Supervisors also play a critical role.

主管同样扮演着关键角色。

Even if a competent person isn't required, supervisors need a broad set of skills to not only identify and alleviate workplace hazards but also build a safety climate within their team that supports intervening and open communication among them.

即便不要求配备具备资质的人员，主管也需要具备广泛的技能，不仅要识别和消除工作场所的隐患，还要在团队中营造一种安全氛围，鼓励大家主动干预、彼此坦诚沟通。

P9

Beyond competent workers and supervisors, it's important to educate everyone within the organization that they are obliged to intervene if they witness a possible unsafe act, whether you're a designated competent person, a supervisor or a frontline worker.

除了合格的员工和主管之外，还必须让组织内的每一个人都明白，一旦目击到可能的不安全行为，他们就有义务予以干预——无论你是被指定的胜任人员、主管，还是一线员工。

Section C · 仔细阅读 Passage 2446 词 · 22 句

P1

The term "environmentalist" can mean different things.

"环保主义者"这个术语可以有不同的含义。

It used to refer to people trying to protect wildlife and natural ecosystems.

它过去指的是那些努力保护野生动物和自然生态系统的人。

In the 21st century, the term has evolved to capture the need to combat human-made climate change.

到了21世纪，这个术语的内涵已经演变为涵盖应对人为气候变化的需要。

P2

The distinction between these two strands of environmentalism is the cause of a split within the scientific community about nuclear energy.

环保主义的这两大分支之间的分歧，正是科学界内部在核能问题上产生分裂的原因。

P3

On one side are purists who believe nuclear power isn't worth the risk and the exclusive solution to the climate crisis is renewable energy.

一方是纯粹主义者，他们认为核能不值得冒这个险，解决气候危机的唯一出路是可再生能源。

The opposing side agrees that renewables are crucial, but says society needs an amount of power available to meet consumers' basic demands when the sun isn't shining and the wind isn't blowing.

对立面则认同可再生能源至关重要，但指出社会需要一定量的电力供应，以便在没有阳光和风力的时刻满足消费者的基本用电需求。

Nuclear energy, being far cleaner than oil, gas and coal, is a natural option, especially where hydroelectric capacity is limited.

核能远比石油、天然气和煤炭清洁，是一种自然而然的选择，在水电产能有限的地区尤其如此。

P4

Leon Clarke, who helped author reports for the UN's Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change, isn't an uncritical supporter of nuclear energy, but says it's a valuable option to have if we're serious about reaching carbon neutrality.

利昂·克拉克曾参与撰写联合国政府间气候变化专门委员会的报告，他并非核能的无条件支持者，但认为如果我们认真对待实现碳中和的目标，核能是一个值得保留的选项。

P5

"Core to all of this is the degree to which you think we can actually meet climate goals with 100% renewables," he said.

"这一切的核心在于，你认为我们究竟能否完全依靠可再生能源实现气候目标，"他说。

"If you don't believe we can do it, and you care about the climate, you are forced to think about something like nuclear."

"如果你不相信我们能做到这一点，而你又关心气候问题，那么你就不得不考虑核能之类的选项。"

P6

The achievability of universal 100% renewability is similarly contentious.

全面实现百分之百可再生能源是否可行，同样存在争议。

Cities such as Burlington, Vermont, have been "100% renewable" for years.

像佛蒙特州伯灵顿这样的城市，已经实现"百分之百可再生能源"供电多年。

But these cities often have small populations, occasionally still rely on fossil fuel energy and have significant renewable resources at their immediate disposal.

但这些城市往往人口稀少，有时仍依赖化石燃料能源，而可再生资源就取之于其近旁。

Meanwhile, countries that manage to run off renewables typically do so thanks to extraordinary hydroelectric capabilities.

与此同时，那些完全依靠可再生能源运行的国家，通常要归功于其非凡的水力发电能力。

P7

Germany stands as the best case study for a large, industrialized country pushing into green energy.

德国是大型工业化国家推进绿色能源的最佳研究案例。

Chancellor Angela Merkel in 2011 announced Energiewende, an energy transition that would phase out nuclear and coal while phasing in renewables.

2011年，总理安格拉·默克尔宣布了"能源转型"（Energiewende）计划，即逐步淘汰核电和煤电、同时大规模引入可再生能源的能源转型。

Wind and solar power generation has increased over 400% since 2010, and renewables provided 46% of the country's electricity in 2019.

自2010年以来，风力和太阳能发电量增长了400%以上，2019年可再生能源提供了德国46%的电力。

P8

But progress has halted in recent years.

但近年来进展陷入停滞。

The instability of renewables doesn't just mean energy is often not produced at night, but also that solar and wind can overwhelm the grid during the day, forcing utilities to pay customers to use their electricity.

可再生能源的不稳定性不仅意味着夜间往往无法发电，还意味着白天太阳能和风能可能过量涌入电网，迫使电力公司付费让客户用电。

Lagging grid infrastructure struggles to transport this overabundance of green energy from Germany's north to its industrial south, meaning many factories still run on coal and gas.

落后的电网基础设施难以将这些过剩的绿色能源从德国北部输送到南部的工业区，这意味着许多工厂仍在使用煤炭和天然气。

The political limit has also been reached in some places, with citizens meeting the construction of new wind turbines with loud protests.

在一些地方，政治承受力也已达到极限，民众对新建风力发电机组的抗议声浪此起彼伏。

P9

The result is that Germany's greenhouse gas emissions have fallen by around 11.5% since 2010—slower than the EU average of 13.5%.

结果是，德国的温室气体排放自2010年以来下降了约11.5%——低于欧盟13.5%的平均水平。
